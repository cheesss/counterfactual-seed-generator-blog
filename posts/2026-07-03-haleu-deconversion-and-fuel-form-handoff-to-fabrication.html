HALEU deconversion and fuel-form handoff to fabrication

The visible uranium constraint is enrichment. The less visible constraint is the moment when enriched UF6 stops being an isotope inventory and becomes a licensed, packaged, specification-compliant fuel input that a fabricator can actually receive.

The visible uranium constraint is enrichment

That handoff is narrow because advanced reactor fuel does not consume generic HALEU. A sodium fast reactor may need uranium metal. A TRISO-fueled high-temperature gas reactor needs coated particles and pebbles. Some future molten-salt designs may need salt chemistry. Between the enrichment cascade and the reactor core sits a chain of deconversion chemistry, criticality controls, NRC Part 70 licensing, transportation package certification, quality documentation, and fuel-fabrication acceptance criteria. A kilogram of 19.75% UF6 is not the same industrial product as a kilogram of accepted metal, oxide powder, UCO kernel feed, or qualified fuel pebble.

DOE has already moved from policy rhetoric into contracting

DOE has already moved from policy rhetoric into contracting. Its HALEU deconversion program selected six companies - Nuclear Fuel Services, American Centrifuge Operating, Framatome, Global Nuclear Fuel-Americas, Orano Federal Services, and Westinghouse Government Services - to compete for deconversion work over as much as ten years, with a combined ceiling of up to $800 million. The same DOE page says the work is to convert HALEU UF6 into chemical forms such as metal or oxide for fuel fabrication, and that all activities must comply with NEPA and occur in the continental United States. Source: U.S. Department of Energy: Haleu deconversion services

The language looks like capacity. The mechanics look more like an evidence queue.

DOE's November 2023 deconversion RFP is the cleanest view of the bottleneck. The contractor was asked to receive up to 6 metric tonnes uranium per year of HALEU UF6, deconvert 3 MTU per year into metal and 3 MTU per year into oxide, package and store the material, and preserve title for DOE. The metal must meet an ASTM standard for uranium metal enriched below 20% U-235 for blending. The oxide must meet an ASTM nuclear-grade sinterable UO2 powder specification. The contractor must have NRC-approved packaging for storage and transport, and the facility must be designed and licensed to receive HALEU UF6 and load final metal or oxide for transport. The RFP also assumed roughly 18 months for NEPA and licensing application work, 30 months for facility modification after approvals and DOE's record of decision, and another 12 months before operations. Source: Leidoseemg: DOE 2023 HALEU Deconversion RFP November 2023

This is why the deconversion-to-fabrication interface deserves more attention than a simple enrichment-capacity chart. In 2024, DOE's HALEU EIS stated that no U.S. deconversion facility was capable of handling HALEU enriched to at least 19.75%, and that facilities would need to be constructed or modified. The EIS modeled a broader 10-year DOE program to acquire as much as 290 metric tonnes of HALEU and estimated that meeting the proposed action would require roughly 50 MTU per year if the output were metal or 56 MTU per year if it were oxide. Source: U.S. Department of Energy: Draft eis 0559 haleu vol 1 2024 02 29 0

That gap is not only a construction issue. It is a controlled-material evidence issue. HALEU below 20% is still Category II material when handled in forms and quantities relevant to commercial fuel fabrication. The facility safety basis, material control and accounting system, criticality safety analysis, physical protection plan, transportation package, waste path, QA program, and acceptance specification all have to line up. A deconverter can win an IDIQ award and still not have the task order, site-specific NEPA record, license amendment, operational readiness inspection, shipping package, or downstream customer acceptance file that turns a contract vehicle into throughput.

Centrus shows the distinction. DOE announced that Centrus reached the 900-kilogram mark for HALEU production at Piketon, Ohio, and described the American Centrifuge Plant as the only U.S. facility licensed to enrich uranium to 19.75% U-235. The NRC's facility page records earlier authorization to enrich to Category II levels and later amendments raising possession limits. Source: U.S. Department of Energy: Centrus reaches 900 kilogram mark haleu production and Nrc: Usecfacility

Centrus’ June 2025 contract amendment extended HALEU work under DOE with an option valued at roughly $110

That is real supply, but it is still HALEU UF6. Centrus’ June 2025 contract amendment extended HALEU work under DOE with an option valued at roughly $110 million, but the product remains DOE-owned HALEU material until the next industrial step occurs. Source: Investors

TRISO-X shows the downstream mirror image. In February 2026, X-energy announced that TRISO-X received an NRC Part 70 special nuclear material license for TX-1 and TX-2, authorizing commercial manufacture of HALEU-based TRISO fuel under an initial 40-year license. The announcement also said NRC must perform a final inspection before the facility receives special nuclear material and begins operations. DOE separately described each Xe-100 pebble as containing more than 18,000 TRISO particles, with the fuel kernel containing uranium, carbon, and oxygen and surrounded by ceramic and carbon layers. Sources: X Energy: Triso x receives first ever part 70 haleu fuel fabrication and U.S. Department of Energy: Triso x receives nrc special nuclear material license advanced fuel

A license is a critical milestone. It is not the same thing as a demonstrated multi-year HALEU fuel supply chain. TRISO-X still needs construction completion, final NRC inspection, HALEU receipt, qualified feedstock, process qualification, and customer fuel schedules. DOE has said fuel fabrication at TX-1 is expected in early 2028. Sargent & Lundy describes the facility as initially designed for 5 MTU per year, enough for about eight Xe-100 reactors, with expansion potential to 16 MTU per year in the early 2030s. Sources: U.S. Department of Energy: Triso x receives nrc special nuclear material license advanced fuel and Sargentlundy: Triso x fuel fabrication facility

The Framatome-TerraPower metal route is another version of the same interface

The Framatome-TerraPower metal route is another version of the same interface. In 2025, Framatome and TerraPower said they had produced uranium metal "pucks"using depleted uranium at Framatome's Richland nuclear fuel manufacturing facility. That milestone matters because TerraPower's Natrium design uses metallic fuel. But the press release is careful: the process was demonstrated with depleted uranium and can be used at higher enrichment. It is not yet public evidence of a licensed, commercial 19.75% HALEU metal deconversion line feeding Natrium fuel at scale. Source: Framatome: and terrapower achieve breakthrough in uranium metallization for advanced

The named reactor projects make the handoff concrete. TerraPower's Natrium demonstration in Kemmerer, Wyoming received its NRC construction permit in March 2026, according to Reuters and TerraPower, after the company filed its construction permit application in 2024. The design is a 345 MWe sodium-cooled reactor with thermal storage enabling a 500 MW boost. Reuters previously reported that TerraPower still treated fuel supply as a central concern and that the first Natrium and X-energy Xe-100 projects together would need roughly 20 tonnes of HALEU for startup, followed by more fuel within 12 to 18 months for refueling. Sources: Reuters: First terrapower advanced reactor schedule but fuel concern and Reuters: U.S. approves construction gates backed terrapower reactor wyoming

Dow's Long Mott project at Seadrift, Texas gives the TRISO pathway a named downstream customer. DOE said NRC accepted the construction permit application for four Xe-100 reactors at the Dow site, and NRC's project page describes the application as a four-module, 800 MWt / 320 MWe high-temperature gas-cooled plant using graphite pebbles with TRISO particle fuel. Sources: U.S. Department of Energy: Nrc dockets construction permit application dow advanced reactor project and Nrc: Long mott

A fabrication license at Oak Ridge, a deconversion route for feedstock, a transportation package

That customer does not only need fuel mass. It needs the correct fuel article at the correct schedule. A fabrication license at Oak Ridge, a deconversion route for feedstock, a transportation package, reactor licensing, and customer construction timing must align. Any one of those gates can convert "HALEU available"into "fuel unavailable." Transportation adds another control point. DOE's HALEU transportation page lists certified packages relevant to UF6, TRISO fuel, and limited fuel forms, and notes that packages originally designed for highly enriched uranium could move HALEU only in small quantities, which would increase containers and cost. PNNL's HALEU transportation work also frames the problem around a system historically built for uranium enrichments at or below 5%. Sources: U.S. Department of Energy: Finding better way transport haleu and Pnnl: 33999

The market may miss this because contract awards use the grammar of completion. "Selected," "awarded," "licensed," and "approved"each sound final. In this chain, each word often opens the next gate. A DOE deconversion IDIQ does not reveal which contractor will receive a task order, which site will be modified, which output form will dominate, which ASTM or customer acceptance specification will bind, which NRC license amendment will be contested, or which package will carry the material. A Part 70 license does not prove that the facility has passed its final operational readiness inspection. A reactor construction permit does not prove that first-core fuel exists in accepted form.

Enrichment could remain the binding constraint

There are serious counterarguments. Enrichment could remain the binding constraint. DOE awarded $2.7 billion in enrichment task orders in 2026, including $900 million each to American Centrifuge Operating and General Matter for domestic HALEU enrichment capacity and $900 million to Orano Federal Services for LEU enrichment capacity. If enriched UF6 production remains too small, deconversion and fabrication capacity can sit idle. Source: U.S. Department of Energy: U.S. department energy awards 27 billion restore american uranium enrichment

Reactor licensing, power-purchase economics, construction execution, or customer demand could also

Reactor licensing, power-purchase economics, construction execution, or customer demand could also dominate. A fuel bottleneck only binds if projects reach the point where they need fuel. Advanced reactor schedules have enough uncertainty that fuel-fabrication gates may move in and out of relevance as projects slip or accelerate.

The strongest workaround is fuel-design migration. Framatome's 2025 NRC application to amend its Richland license for TRISO fuel fabrication below 10% U-235 shows one direction: reduce enrichment intensity or alter the fuel/process route enough to ease Category II burdens, even if that means different reactor physics, larger cores, or lower performance. Source: Federal Register: Framatome inc framatome fuel fabrication facility license amendment application

A second workaround is international capacity. The United Kingdom's HALEU deconversion competition targeted an oxide deconversion facility operational by 2031 with initial capacity of 10,000 kgU per year, expandable to 30,000 kgU per year, plus detailed design for a metal line of at least 5,000 kgU per year. That would not automatically solve U.S. project fuel qualification or transport constraints, but it shows that governments are treating deconversion as its own industrial node rather than an afterthought. Source: Gov: Haleu deconversion competition scheme guidance

The signals worth monitoring are specific

The signals worth monitoring are specific. Watch for DOE task orders that name the deconversion site, form, annual volume, storage configuration, and customer route. Watch NRC ADAMS for license amendments tied to SNM-2011 at Piketon, SNM-1227 at Framatome Richland, SNM-7007 at TRISO-X, and any NFS, GNF, Orano, or Westinghouse fuel-cycle amendments. Watch for facility construction milestones, operational readiness inspection results, transportation certificate amendments, first HALEU UF6 receipts at deconversion sites, first accepted metal or oxide batches at fabricators, and fuel qualification data tied to named reactors.

The cleanest falsifier would be routine, multi-tonne movement of 19.75% HALEU from enrichment through deconversion into accepted metal or oxide feedstock, then into licensed fabrication facilities, with no project schedule slippage attributable to chemistry, licensing, packaging, QA, or fuel qualification. That would convert the interface from a control point into normal industrial plumbing.

Until that happens, the high-value question is not only "who can enrich HALEU"?It is "who can turn HALEU into the exact licensed fuel form that a named reactor customer can accept on schedule"?

HFC Phase-Down: Why the HVAC Refrigerant Transition Bottleneck Moved Downstream

In 2025, available bulk R-454B and new HVAC equipment could coexist with unfinished installations. Contractor-sized refrigerant packages were a separate constraint. Manufacturers responded by expanding that delivery channel and moving some field charging into the factory.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-09-24 · 12 min read

The refrigerant transition needed a second delivery system

The recurring system pattern is a mismatch between upstream availability and usable delivery. The question now is whether replenishment and recovery can become dependable routine operations.

Evidence checked through October 16, 2026.

In brief

  • EPA documented regional contractor-access problems despite sufficient upstream R-454B supply.
  • Packaging and extra factory charge addressed the same field requirement through different routes.
  • Established dealer recovery channels precede the newer collection pilots and separation projects.
  • The 2029 supply ceiling increases the incentive to recover material; shortages remain contingent.

A machine and a finished installation are different products

EPA's May 2026 final rule describes sufficient R-454B supply alongside difficulty getting service cylinders to contractors and technicians. Some regions experienced scarcity, interrupting access to the refrigerant needed for installation. That is direct support for a downstream delivery mismatch. EPA's account

The distinction matters because the chain has several owners. Chemical suppliers produce and blend refrigerant; packagers fill suitable containers; distributors finance branch stock; contractors combine that stock with equipment, labor and installation approval. The customer's purchase is a working installation. Bulk material becomes useful to that customer only when the other stages connect.

HARDI's sales observations show how quickly the equipment mix changed: A2L systems represented less than 5% of central-ducted distributor sales in November 2024, 51% in April 2025 and a preliminary 60% in May; September reached 91%. Sales composition measures substitution inside a channel. As that mix changed, distributors needed compatible refrigerant alongside the equipment. Early observations; September result

Two regulatory instruments shape that substitution. The AIM Act's allowance system restricts exchange-value-weighted new HFC supply, while end-use rules govern covered equipment. The residential/light-commercial provisions distinguish self-contained products from installed systems and preserve an eligible pre-2025-component route. Equipment restrictions EPA's compliance GWPs are 2,088 for R-410A, 465 for R-454B and 675 for R-32, against the covered categories' prohibition at 700 or greater. These values concern climate impact; equipment efficiency is a separate characteristic. EPA reference table

Two responses to the same field requirement

Carrier's May 19, 2025 response was to convert excess bulk R-454B into 20-pound cylinders for distributors and contractor dealers. It addressed the package and access point where field work was interrupted. Packaging announcement

Rheem chose an upstream workaround. Its May notice announced additional factory charge for 13.4 and 14.3 SEER2 air conditioners and 14.3 SEER2 heat pumps across its brands. Covered matches would support 30-foot line sets with a 3/8-inch liquid line, up from 15 feet, without higher unit prices. Some refrigerant that would otherwise need a separate field purchase could travel inside the equipment. Rheem's notice

The mechanisms are partly substitutable. More small packages expand the field-supply channel. More factory charge reduces the field requirement of a covered installation. Both still consume refrigerant, but they allocate handling and inventory responsibility differently.

By August 22, Carrier said it was fulfilling R-454B orders within two weeks after securing refrigerant, cylinders and down-packing capacity. Its stated terms removed minimum quantities and bundling with other refrigerants, with longer lead times possible above 5,000 units. The release leaves the unit-to-cylinder mapping unspecified. The operational change was coordinated access to complementary inputs. Carrier's fulfillment statement

Chemours' Texas project belongs to another stage. Announced in July 2022, the $80 million Opteon YF expansion, together with debottlenecking, targeted roughly 40% more site capacity for HFO-1234yf at Ingleside, near Corpus Christi. That is upstream chemical capacity serving several applications. The project addressed chemical manufacturing; the 2025 packaging response addressed field-ready delivery. Original project scope

The constraint can ease before every input becomes easy

A new refrigerant channel needs stock in many places at once. Initial distributor and contractor purchases can exceed subsequent consumption as both groups protect against missed jobs. That makes inventory formation different from recurring replenishment.

Worthington's September 23, 2026 earnings call describes lower A2L-cylinder demand as channel inventories adjusted and new-home sales remained muted. Management simultaneously reported tight steel availability and scheduling and shipment difficulties. Weaker orders and an upstream material constraint can coexist within the same supplier. Its account supports an inventory adjustment, rather than a simple continuation of the 2025 emergency. Worthington's call

The contemporary counterargument deserves equal weight. ACCA's June 2025 supplier Q&A records Worthington naming constraints in both gas and cylinders; Carrier names raw materials, global production, logistics and over-ordering. EPA's later aggregate assessment and these supplier-specific experiences describe different scopes. A supplier's specific constraint can differ from the national material balance; both records identify difficulties connecting supply to field work. Supplier testimony

Nor does the evidence support replacing a permanent-shortage story with a uniform demand-collapse story. AHRI reported 916,830 combined central-air-conditioner and air-source-heat-pump shipments in July 2026, up 20.1% year over year; year-to-date shipments rose 5.5%. HARDI subsequently reported August distributor sales revenue up 7.6%, helped by weather. The first series measures factory units; the second measures nominal sales. AHRI's July release; HARDI's October 13 update

Compatibility and approval also determine usable supply

Containment is an application-specific product. Worthington's current A2L family includes non-refillable, refillable and recovery cylinders, with distinguishing markings and valve configurations. A Lennox-listed legacy recovery cylinder has a 400-psi service rating yet expressly excludes R-454B and R-32 recovery. Pressure rating alone therefore cannot establish interchangeability. A2L portfolio; legacy restriction

Equipment substitution is equally specific. Greentech reports completing a Fort Worth replacement on September 10, 2026: legacy R-22 equipment gave way to a Daikin FIT R-32 heat pump and communicating air handler, with controls and other installation work integrated into the job. The purchased object was a replacement system and service package, rather than a different fluid for the old circuit. Fort Worth case

Repeat field activity also exists. Laminar's Mitsubishi review now says it has installed dozens of R-454B heat pumps in 2026 and marks an earlier missing-sensor problem resolved. Its body explicitly refers to October 2026 while the publication date remains September 12, 2025. The revised account adds reported repeat installation experience to an earlier description of component difficulties. Revised installer account

Local permission is another distinct requirement. San Antonio's March 6, 2025 interpretation provides a refrigerant-pipe shaft exception conditional on refrigerant quantity and the smallest occupied space through which piping passes. Engineers must demonstrate the applicable concentration limit. The official's conditional design route makes calculations and project approval part of the delivery process. CI-2025-002, questions 3-4

The reverse delivery chain already has a business

Reclamation connects a different set of transactions: a contractor returns recovered material, a reclaimer tests and processes it, and a service buyer purchases qualified refrigerant.

Hudson and Lennox announced their aftermarket alliance in August 2022, with Hudson designated exclusive supplier of certified reclaimed refrigerants for Lennox's residential aftermarket support. Lennox's 2024 report records 285 participating dealer locations and 174,875 pounds reclaimed. This operating channel predates the cylinder disruption. Alliance; Lennox's operating disclosure, p.46

The collection offer includes logistics. Hudson's Lennox-program Q&A says Hudson covers pickup freight, refurbishment and mixed-refrigerant disposal, with no minimum pickup. Payment is issued 30 days after invoice receipt. Replacement 30- and 50-pound cylinders are to be dispatched within 48 hours of receiving recovered material. These terms make small returns easier to organize and specify which party carries handling costs. Program terms

California's REFRESH pilot adds collection to equipment-replacement workflows. Administrator NASRC identifies A-Gas and Hudson as selected reclaimers and links contractor enrollment to the CEC's Equitable Building Decarbonization direct-install program. Program structure Hudson's posted R-410A buyback offer is $5 per pound at intake purity of at least 95%, and $3 below that level, subject to change. Those are offered payments for recovered feedstock. Posted terms

In Washington, DC, DCSEU reports that Hugee returned more than 200 pounds through its Hudson pilot. That is a named material-flow observation. Accepted yield, payment and repeat returns are the next commercial milestones. DCSEU's account

These relationships explain the proposed mechanism for expansion: combining return logistics, compensation and an existing replacement visit can reduce the effort needed to collect usable feedstock. Its economics depend on how much additional accepted material reaches processing.

More collected pounds must become more saleable pounds

EPA requires reclaimed material resold to a new owner to meet specified purity and laboratory-verification requirements. That separates returned stock from marketable product. Reclamation framework

RMI and OTS tested a three-ton split heat pump and five-ton rooftop unit with an intentionally formulated specification-limit R-410A sample. Normal-charging performance differences from virgin refrigerant stayed within measurement uncertainty. Buyers gain concrete evidence about specification-grade fluid under the tested short-term conditions. Amended technical appendix, pp.4-9

Hudson's established business is visible in recognized revenue: $246.614 million in 2025, including $7.151 million in RefrigerantSide services. Its acquisition record also contains a shortfall: the company reversed a $1.6 million USA Refrigerants earn-out liability after determining the criteria would not be met. Existing operations and missed commercial expectations belong in the same account. 2025 annual filing

The September 17, 2026 Icorium agreement is a separate development branch. Hudson announced access to engineering resources and separation-related intellectual property, seeking faster conversion of complex mixtures into saleable material. Icorium targets first deployment in 2027. That is a development schedule, with commissioned equipment and customer-accepted output still ahead. Development agreement; Icorium's target

The economic test is contribution per saleable pound: realized receipts minus yield-adjusted feedstock, collection, testing, processing, rejected material, packaging, delivery and financing. Subsidies and royalties should be identified separately. Extra separation capacity helps only where difficult intake is the obstacle; extra collection helps only where recoverable material is available.

Hudson's Q2 2026 filing illustrates the distinction between activity and returns. Revenue rose to $78.3 million with higher volume, while gross margin fell to 26% from 31%, reflecting lower selling prices and higher freight costs. June 30 net inventory was $128.112 million; its undrawn $40 million revolving facility was due March 2, 2027. These company-wide figures show pricing and working-capital exposure. Quarterly filing

R-22 challenges the inevitability of scarcity

U.S. production and import of new R-22 ended in 2020, while existing and reclaimed supply continued supporting servicing. EPA credits reclamation with helping maintain adequate supply through that phaseout. Homeowner guidance; EPA retrospective

R-22 reclaimed output nevertheless fell from 7.82 million pounds in 2019 to 5.68 million in 2022. Fleet retirement can reduce material requirements even as virgin supply disappears. The comparison directs attention to demand against delivered supply, rather than to a presumption that reclamation must grow indefinitely. Historical EPA tables

The scheduled HFC ceiling declines from 60% to 30% of baseline in 2029. Because the ceiling is exchange-value-weighted, physical availability depends on refrigerant mix, inventories and adaptation. Separately, the 2029 reclaimed-only servicing requirement covers supermarket systems, refrigerated transport and automatic commercial ice makers. Residential HVAC service buyers face changing supply economics, rather than that blanket purchasing mandate. Allowance schedule; servicing scope

Three outcomes to distinguish

If field replenishment becomes routine, the historical packaging constraint has eased. Emergency capacity then competes on ordinary handling cost and service reliability.

If refrigerant is available but owners decline replacement quotes, affordability or demand may determine job completion. Expanding filling capacity would address the wrong immediate problem.

If legacy servicing encounters delays despite collected stocks, the location of those stocks matters: unaccepted feedstock, processing queues and finished regional inventory imply different remedies. A national recovered-pound total cannot identify which one is stopping a repair.

Limits: national delay rankings, a complete refrigerant-specific service balance and subsidy-adjusted collection economics remain unavailable. Supplier and installer accounts are attributed operating evidence, and laboratory tests leave lifetime reliability open. The public call measures national output direction, not program causation, profitable scale or a servicing shortage.

  • By December 31, 2027: look for REFRESH disclosure of accepted returns, payments and repeat contractor participation. Observation base October 16, 2026. Program administrator
  • By December 31, 2028: settle the 2027-versus-2026 reclaimed-HFC output call using a comparable public series. Forecast base September 29, 2026. EPA records
  • By December 31, 2029: check for an unaffiliated buyer's acceptance and payment for a traceable Hudson-Icorium processed lot. Forecast base September 29, 2026. Starting agreement

The next constraint should be located where an otherwise executable job stops. The transition has already changed who packages, charges, collects and finances refrigerant. Its next achievement would be dependable usable supply through those connected operations.

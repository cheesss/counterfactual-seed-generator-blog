The refrigerant transition needed a second delivery system

EPA documented sufficient bulk R-454B during the 2025 transition alongside regional difficulty obtaining service cylinders. Carrier later reported delivery relief after securing cylinders and down-packing capacity; Chemours subsequently described stabilizing availability.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-09-24 · 13 min read

Published 2026-09-24; updated 2026-09-29.

In brief

  • EPA documented a 2025 bottleneck in contractor-ready R-454B cylinders.
  • HARDI reported 91% A2L share of central-ducted distributor sales in September 2025.
  • Carrier reported two-week R-454B fulfillment after assembling the necessary supply chain.
  • Worthington reported weaker A2L-cylinder demand alongside tight steel supply.
  • 2029 HFC allowance ceilings fall to 30% of baseline.

Equipment and field inventory moved on different clocks

The U.S. HVAC refrigerant transition is usually measured by the equipment leaving factories. HARDI reported that A2L equipment rose from less than 5% of central-ducted distributor sales in November 2024 to 51% in April 2025, a preliminary 60% in May and 91% in September. Manufacturers and distributors changed their product mix quickly. Finishing installations required a supporting inventory network that followed a different schedule. (HARDI early sales series) (HARDI September sales share)

EPA's May 26, 2026 final rule describes sufficient R-454B supply during the 2025 transition alongside difficulty obtaining service cylinders and scarcity in some regions. Bulk chemical availability could coexist with a contractor unable to obtain refrigerant in the package needed to complete a job. The rule documents a material historical delivery bottleneck; it does not establish that packaging caused most installation delays nationwide. (EPA Technology Transitions final rule)

The operating chain explains the mismatch. Producers supply bulk refrigerant; filling and down-packing operations place it in suitable cylinders; distributors position inventory; contractors obtain it for installation and top-offs. Each handoff must work. Extra production upstream cannot immediately complete an installation when the missing input is a filled cylinder at the local branch.

By September 29, 2026, supplier accounts supported a different picture: delivery relief and inventory adjustment, with other inputs still constrained. The useful lesson is how participants repaired the handoff and how to identify a recurrence. Carrying the original shortage diagnosis forward indefinitely would miss the industry's adaptation.

Factory charging offered a route around the cylinder shortage

Carrier and Rheem approached the same field requirement from opposite ends. On May 19, 2025, Carrier announced conversion of excess bulk R-454B into 20-pound cylinders. Rheem's May 22 announcement instead increased the refrigerant already inside specified equipment, reducing its dependence on field top-offs.

Rheem's temporary measure covered specified 13.4- and 14.3-SEER2 air conditioners and 14.3-SEER2 heat pumps, with the line-set allowance based on a 3/8-inch liquid line. The company said the extra charge would not increase unit prices. The chemical requirement remained; more inventory and charging responsibility moved into the factory. This provides a concrete mechanism for relieving field demand without assuming additional bulk output solves every problem.

Carrier's sequence runs from a May packaging announcement to reported two-week fulfillment in August. That is a dated supplier response, rather than a measured national recovery time. Orders above 5,000 units could take longer, and the announcement supplies no independent distribution of customer receipt times. Its delivery claim nevertheless provides evidence against an uninterrupted shortage.

Chemours' expansion illustrates a separate clock. The approximately $80 million project was planned before the 2025 disruption and concerned HFO-1234yf feedstock. Treating its 40% addition as emergency finished-R-454B capacity would confuse upstream investment with the filling and distribution work that contractors needed.

Initial stocking can turn into inventory adjustment

Chemours' September 9, 2026 presentation says R-454B availability began stabilizing in Q2 2026. It describes elevated distributor inventory after initial channel stocking and additional OEM factory charging. Part of that account rests on company estimates. It supports an inventory-cycle explanation: establishing a new distribution network can require more purchases than maintaining it once the shelves are stocked. (Chemours September presentation)

Worthington's September 23 earnings call, furnished to the SEC on September 25, adds a useful complication. Management reported lower year-over-year A2L-cylinder demand as channel inventories adjusted and new-home sales remained muted. It also reported tight steel availability and extended lead times disrupting production and shipment scheduling. Weaker cylinder orders can coexist with an upstream material constraint. (Worthington September earnings call)

An exclusively downstream explanation also overstates the earlier evidence. ACCA's supplier-response page records Worthington discussing both gas and cylinder constraints, while Carrier identified production, materials, logistics and over-ordering among the problems. These participant accounts do not establish a national molecule deficit, but they prevent ruling out supplier-specific production limitations. (ACCA supplier responses)

The market has reached repeatable procurement in the observed equipment and refrigerant channels. Dependable national operation remains a separate test: customer deliveries and completed jobs across regions through a peak cooling season. Neither a supplier announcement nor an equipment-sales share measures that outcome. AHRI's July 2026 combined air-conditioner and heat-pump shipments rose 20.1% year over year, with year-to-date shipments up 5.5%; those figures also caution against replacing a sweeping shortage story with a sweeping demand-collapse story. (AHRI July shipment release)

The first missing input varies by installation

A cylinder can be available while another requirement stops a project. San Antonio's March 6, 2025 interpretation CI-2025-002 created a conditional exception to the A2L refrigerant-pipe shaft requirement. Engineers must show that refrigerant concentrations satisfy the applicable limit for the smallest occupied space traversed by the piping. The interpretation provides an effective local compliance route, although the available record identifies no named multifamily project receiving final mechanical approval through it. (San Antonio interpretation)

Federal inventory relief follows another route. Effective July 27, 2026, EPA removed the installation deadline for eligible systems whose specified components were all manufactured or imported before January 1, 2025. That protects a finite stock of legacy equipment. It does not permit unrestricted new R-410A installations using later-vintage components. (EPA Technology Transitions final rule)

Field execution brings its own requirements. Section 608 technician credentials do not expire, and ACCA reported more than 8,200 technicians and contractors trained through its A2L program by August 2025. Training participation establishes activity, rather than a national count of competent, equipped technicians available for jobs. (EPA technician certification) (ACCA training participation)

Individual contractor accounts show why job records matter. Laminar's account of its first Mitsubishi R-454B installation described refrigerant scarcity as mostly a non-factor while identifying detection-component issues as a complication. Greentech separately reported a September 2026 Fort Worth conversion from R-22 equipment to a Daikin R-32 heat pump. These are bounded examples of different installation experiences and equipment pathways; neither establishes a representative success rate or proves that an R-454B project could simply switch refrigerants. (Laminar installation account) (Greentech Fort Worth conversion)

Recovery starts with an existing service network

Servicing the R-410A installed base is a separate, longer transition. Existing systems can continue operating and being repaired, and R-454B is not a drop-in replacement. Replacement-equipment sales can therefore change quickly while legacy servicing continues. (Carrier refrigerant compatibility guidance)

Recovery already has operating channels. Lennox's 2024 sustainability data report 285 dealer locations and 174,875 pounds reclaimed through its Hudson program. Those company totals are neither R-410A-only volumes nor counts of contractors making repeat paid returns, but they show an established collection relationship. (Lennox sustainability data)

California's REFRESH program connects recovery to building-decarbonization replacements. Its solicitation provided up to $5 million at the program level, and Hudson and A-Gas were selected as reclaimers. Hudson's posted terms offer $5 per pound for recovered R-410A at at least 95% purity and $3 below that threshold, subject to change. These are intake incentives; finished-product specifications, resale prices and contractor profit are different measures. (REFRESH program) (Hudson recovery-payment terms)

In Washington, DC, DCSEU reported that Hugee Corporation recovered and returned more than 200 pounds in a pilot. Return is a concrete milestone, but reclamation and subsequent delivery remain separate steps. RMI and OTS R&D's 2026 tests likewise provide bounded technical evidence: specification-compliant reclaimed R-410A produced no measurable short-term performance penalty in one three-ton split heat pump and one five-ton rooftop unit. Neither a return milestone nor this small test establishes fleet reliability or repeat customer purchasing. (DCSEU recovery pilot) (RMI and OTS technical tests)

The R-22 precedent changes the burden of proof for 2029

The closest historical comparison is the R-22 servicing transition. EPA credits reclamation of ozone-depleting refrigerants, including HCFC-22, with helping maintain adequate service supply through the phaseout. That is an agency retrospective assessment, rather than proof that every repair was cheap or timely. It nevertheless shows that tighter virgin supply can be accommodated by an operating recovery system. (EPA reclamation trends)

EPA's historical table records R-22 reclamation falling from 7.82 million pounds in 2019 to 5.68 million in 2022. Declining output alone establishes neither scarcity nor abundance: equipment retirement, inventories and servicing demand also change. R-410A faces a different policy regime and installed base, so the precedent cannot supply a numerical shortage probability. It does show why the physical service balance matters. (EPA historical reclamation table)

In 2029, HFC production and consumption allowance ceilings fall from 60% to 30% of their respective baselines. These exchange-value-weighted limits govern regulated new supply; they do not halve all contractor-ready R-410A. Inventory withdrawals, refrigerant mix, reclamation, retirement and leakage affect what remains available for repairs. The separate reclaimed-only servicing requirement covers specified commercial refrigeration subsectors, rather than residential HVAC generally. (HFC allowance schedule) (Servicing requirement scope)

EPA reports approximately 10% growth in HFC reclamation during 2025. (EPA reclamation trends) Aggregate growth cannot locate a future R-410A constraint. During 2027-2028, the useful preparation is a refrigerant-specific balance: recovered intake, processing queues, saleable stock, deliveries and repair requirements. Too little recovered feedstock would implicate collection; accumulating unprocessed material would implicate processing; finished stock alongside contractor shortages would implicate distribution.

The 2029-2030 cooling seasons test whether that system covers legacy demand. Sustained refrigerant-attributable repair delays would support a servicing bottleneck. Adequate replenishment and timely repairs would demonstrate adaptation even under tighter allowances. Rising reclaimed share alone can accompany either outcome.

Moving responsibility does not guarantee moving margin

OEMs control factory charging and equipment configuration. Producers control bulk supply and blending inputs. Cylinder makers, down-packers and distributors turn that material into regional field inventory. Contractors decide whether recovered refrigerant enters the reverse chain, while reclaimers control acceptance, processing and finished-product delivery. Those actors can relieve different constraints, and established suppliers can retain customer relationships while changing how they serve them.

Public-company results show why activity and retained economics must be separated. Hudson reported second-quarter 2026 revenue rising 8% to $78.3 million while gross margin fell to 26% from 31%, citing lower refrigerant pricing and higher operating costs among the factors. These are company-wide results, rather than reclaimed-R-410A unit economics. Worthington estimated an approximately $5 million adverse Q4 FY2026 EBITDA comparison because the prior year's unusually strong A2L inventory build did not repeat. Initial stocking can create revenue that does not recur at the same rate. (Hudson quarterly results) (Worthington FY2026 earnings discussion)

Hudson also held about $128.1 million of net inventory at June 30, 2026 and an undrawn $40 million revolving facility then scheduled to mature March 2, 2027. Owning inventory improves the ability to serve customers while exposing the owner to price and financing risk. Chemours' feedstock exposure, Carrier's charging and fulfillment role, Worthington's cylinders and Lennox's recovery relationship therefore represent different operating positions, with different risks. (Hudson June filing)

The payer and cost path matter. Factory pre-charge moves work into manufacturing; recovery incentives compensate intake; distributors finance local availability. A pound sold through several intermediaries remains one pound of final demand. Durable additional earnings must survive collection losses, rejected material, testing, packaging, freight and financing, with subsidy kept distinct. More recovery could be useful while costs absorb the added revenue. The evidence establishes changing responsibilities more firmly than a new downstream profit pool.

What to watch

The linked sources document the starting conditions. Future operating records and EPA releases are needed to settle these tests; inaccessible records leave a result unresolved.

  • R-454B replenishment, base September 29, 2026; review November 30, 2027: track May-September 2027 orders and linked jobs in a fixed panel of three unrelated distributors, retaining rejected orders. Dependable deliveries and completions support relief in those channels; recurrent cylinder-attributable stoppages despite available equipment and bulk refrigerant weaken it. (Carrier fulfillment statement)
  • REFRESH repeat payments, base September 29, 2026; by December 31, 2027: three distinct contractors each receiving payment for the second of two separately accepted R-410A returns made after the base date would demonstrate repeat paid recovery. One-off returns or acceptance without payment do not meet that threshold. (REFRESH program) (Hudson recovery-payment terms)
  • San Antonio execution, base September 29, 2026; by December 31, 2028: one named multifamily A2L project receiving final mechanical approval with plans expressly using CI-2025-002 would demonstrate execution of the local route. Documented refusals would weaken its practical usefulness. (San Antonio interpretation)
  • HFC reclamation output, base September 29, 2026; review December 31, 2028: compare unrounded 2027 and 2026 U.S. totals using one EPA data vintage. Higher 2027 output establishes growth; flat or lower output rejects that proposition. Neither result alone settles R-410A scarcity. (EPA reclamation trends)
  • R-410A servicing, base September 29, 2026; review June 30, 2031: compare the 2029-2030 cooling seasons with 2028 in a fixed distributor panel. Sustained refrigerant-attributable repair queues support a service constraint; reconciled supply and timely repairs reject its dominance. Rising reclaimed share alone is insufficient. (HFC allowance schedule)

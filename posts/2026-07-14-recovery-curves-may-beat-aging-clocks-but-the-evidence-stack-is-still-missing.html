Recovery curves may become endpoints only when they stop trying to explain all of aging

Recovery measurements become more interesting when resting-state biological-age scores rise during surgery or infection and then fall again during recovery. But that does not validate a universal aging curve; it points to a narrower endpoint question tied to a specific stressor, population, and outcome.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-07-14 · 13 min read

In brief

  • 203 Duke patients split into outcome-specific recovery classes.
  • 4,337 TILDA participants linked slower stand recovery to frailty.
  • 91% versus 76% Radboud completeness exposed a frailty-data cliff.
  • 2019 to 2026 CHF endpoint work shows digital review can grind slowly.

The universal healthspan story is where the evidence starts to break

That state-sensitivity point is not hypothetical. Several biological-age estimates rose during acute stress and later reversed during recovery, which implies at least partial state sensitivity rather than a pure stock-of-aging signal. (Cell Metabolism stress-response study) (NIA summary)

If that is true, the better question is not "how old is this person today?" It is "what does this person do after a defined challenge?" That sounds like a larger opportunity, but the evidence points the other way. PRIME-KNEE found weak concordance outside the pain domains, which argues against one universal recovery score and toward several narrower endpoint families. (PRIME-KNEE results)

That changes the thesis. Recovery kinetics does not look ready to replace biological-age scores as a universal healthspan measure. It looks more plausible as a set of narrow endpoints that become credible only after developers abandon the universal-score ambition and validate one stressor, one population, and one patient-relevant outcome at a time.

Once the measure is narrowed, the bottleneck changes shape. The problem is not a shortage of sensors, assays, or dashboards. It is the evidence package: repeatability, missing-data handling, incremental value beyond static reserve, treatment responsiveness, and a defined context of use. FDA and EMA both organize acceptance around that kind of bounded use, not a category-level blessing for "recovery" or "wearables." (FDA context of use) (EMA methodology guidance)

The named cases already show signal, but they stop short of endpoint status

The evidence base is no longer empty. It is just stubbornly one layer short of what an endpoint needs. The named cases now span surgery, fracture, dialysis, active standing, safe provocations, and adjacent regulatory programs. They show real physiology, real heterogeneity, and real operational friction. They do not yet show a reusable, treatment-sensitive endpoint. (SPRING RESTORE) (PRIME-KNEE registry) (Baltimore Hip Studies) (CHOICE) (TILDA active stand) (Radboud preoperative study)

The pattern across the table is consistent. Recovery trajectories can be prognostic. They can even be mechanistically rich. The missing bridge is still the same one: a prespecified recovery feature that survives reliability testing, adds value beyond ordinary reserve measures, and changes with treatment in a way that tracks a recognized outcome.

Surgery supplies the cleanest first context, and it still looks messy

Surgery keeps reappearing because it solves one of the hardest design problems: it gives a known time zero. It also produces meaningful deterioration and familiar follow-up windows. That is why SPRING RESTORE, PRIME-KNEE, Baltimore Hip Studies, and the All of Us perioperative work matter more than abstract talk about resilience. (All of Us perioperative preprint)

PRIME-KNEE is the most useful named case because it looks the most like an endpoint program. The study started on 2020-02-27, reached primary completion on 2023-10-30, and published its main clinical-predictor results in 2026. That realized clock matters. Even a single-center observational program needed roughly 44 months to get from launch to primary completion before anyone could ask whether treatment actually shifts the curve. (PRIME-KNEE registry)

The results are narrower than the category story. Only about 7% of participants occupied the highest-resilience position across all four domains, while about 20% occupied the lowest across all four. Strong agreement appeared mainly between pain intensity and pain interference. Activity and disability behaved more independently. That is evidence for indication-specific recovery measures, not for one cross-system healthspan factor. (PRIME-KNEE results)

The perioperative preprint makes the model risk even clearer. In that analysis, recovery moved from about 13 weeks to 22 weeks depending on whether baseline was immediate or remote. The All of Us result is a preprint, so it is best read as a modeling warning rather than settled endpoint evidence. A useful endpoint cannot swing that much because of a baseline choice most readers never see.

Active stand is the best low-burden candidate, and it still looks fragile

If surgery is too heavy for broad geroscience use, active stand is the clearest low-risk alternative in the report. TILDA linked slower systolic blood-pressure recovery after standing to frailty in 4,337 participants, and the paper reported a -4.12 mmHg interaction in the 10-to-20-second window. The appeal is obvious: the challenge is brief, mechanistic, and already legible to autonomic laboratories. (TILDA active stand)

The same line of evidence also carries one of the sharpest warnings. Repeat-visit orthostatic beat-to-beat blood-pressure work reported low-to-moderate reliability, with intraclass correlations ranging from 0.47 to 0.80. If within-person variability is that large, more sites and more documentation will not rescue the endpoint by themselves. (orthostatic reliability)

Radboud then showed the operational version of the problem. In 373 preoperative patients, continuous blood-pressure data were available for 91% of the cardiothoracic cohort but only 76% of the older, frailer general-surgery cohort. The best association weakened after adjustment for frailty and comorbidity and did not reproduce cleanly in the second cohort. That is not a side issue. The people the endpoint is supposed to help are the same people most likely to break the data pipeline. (Radboud preoperative study)

The more exploratory challenge papers do not solve that. A 50-person multi-challenge study found age associations in cold immersion, vascular occlusion, and balance recovery, but not in heart-rate or strength recovery. A 69-person Nebraska balance study found age and recovery-status effects, yet little support for a clean dose-response story. The safe-challenge menu is getting richer. The case for one portable, high-utility measure is not. (multi-challenge study) (balance perturbation)

The strongest base rates are narrow, and the clocks are slow

The nearest positive precedent is not in aging. It is SV95C in Duchenne muscular dystrophy. EMA qualified the stride-velocity measure as a secondary endpoint in 2019 and as a primary endpoint in 2023. That four-year interval is encouraging only if the analogy is used carefully. SV95C measures disease-specific ambulation. It does not ask the sponsor to standardize a stressor, defend a universal resilience construct, or reconcile several physiological subsystems. (EMA SV95C 2019) (EMA SV95C 2023)

The harder base rate is the C-Path chronic-heart-failure activity program. Public materials show letters of intent accepted in April 2019, an informal FDA meeting in October 2022, a step-count qualification-plan submission in December 2024, and active work still being reported in 2026. If familiar step data can occupy a seven-year public evidence cycle, recovery kinetics starts with a harder job. (C-Path 2022 deck) (C-Path 2025 poster) (C-Path 2026 update)

But this is also where the first-pass thesis had to be narrowed. Formal qualification is not mandatory for first deployment. FDA says a drug-development tool can still be reviewed inside one IND, NDA, or BLA without entering the full qualification program. That means the first useful recovery endpoint could emerge as a program-specific secondary endpoint before any reusable public qualification exists. It does not lower the science bar. It separates the adoption clock from the qualification clock. Taken together, the adjacent SV95C and C-Path analogues suggest narrow, indication-specific recovery measures are likely to remain evidence-constrained through the late 2020s, while reusable FDA or EMA qualification looks more like a 2029-2031-or-later analogy than a published agency timetable. (FDA DDT process)

As of 2026-07-14, the cited FDA biomarker-qualification materials and EMA qualification-opinion materials did not show a public geroscience recovery-kinetics endpoint record. (FDA biomarker qualification program) (EMA SV95C 2019) (EMA SV95C 2023) A negative public-record search is not proof that no confidential sponsor interaction exists. That matters because the field still lacks even a named public context-of-use fight.

The spend sits in the evidence package, not in the device box

If this category grows, the first money is unlikely to sit with whoever sells the prettiest watch or dashboard. PRIME-KNEE already needed Garmin devices and Greenlight integration. Radboud used Finapres, Artinis, BIOPAC, and MathWorks. The completed NCT04815824 exercise protocol, for which no final public results were located, used Abbott, Siemens, and Beckman Coulter platforms. The adjacent SV95C precedent sat on a specific Sysnav device-and-algorithm package. (Garmin research trials) (Greenlight PRIME-KNEE) (NCT04815824 protocol) (Sysnav qualification context)

That stack already looks crowded because the real scarce asset is somewhere else. What sponsors keep having to rebuild is the package that ties one defined challenge to raw data, calibration, missingness analysis, prespecified recovery features, outcomes, and regulator correspondence across sites. Until that package exists, the recurring spend sits in clinical sites, assay runs, data integration, quality control, biostatistics, and submission documentation. This looks more like service revenue than like a clean monopoly rent.

That distinction matters for public-company language. The report found no direct listed-company exposure. Garmin, Abbott, Danaher, and Siemens show up only as partial or indirect chain exposures, with no disclosed recovery-endpoint revenue, pricing power, backlog, or qualification ownership. The best control points identified here are private, nonprofit, governmental, or sponsor-owned.

The strongest counterargument is that dynamic measures may simply lose

The hardest argument against the thesis is not regulatory delay. It is that the new measures may add burden without adding enough utility. PRIME-KNEE tested dual-task gait and functional near-infrared spectroscopy before surgery, and conventional physical, cognitive, and psychological reserve measures still predicted recovery better. That is not a footnote. It is direct evidence that a more physiological test can still be the less useful one. (PRIME-KNEE results)

The same pattern appears elsewhere. Radboud's best association weakened after adjustment. The All of Us perioperative preprint changed the recovery window by nine weeks when baseline definition changed. A 2025 static comparator, the CHEES resilience scale, reported an ICC of 0.915 in a 1,934-person validation cohort. Dynamic measures do not compete against a vacuum. They compete against cheaper, easier, and sometimes more stable alternatives. (All of Us perioperative preprint) (CHEES validation)

The intervention pipeline is weaker than the first-pass narrative implied. NCT04815824 is listed as completed, but no final public results were located. RECOVER, NCT04057677, terminated after enrolling 12 participants. That leaves the field without a public randomized result that closes the treatment-responsiveness gap. The strongest version of the thesis therefore has to stay narrow: validation-evidence is likely binding only after the field proves that the construct is coherent and worth validating. (NCT04815824 record) (RECOVER record)

The plausible win condition is specific, and so is the failure mode

The credible success case in this report is no longer a universal healthspan number. It is a narrow endpoint sequence. Perioperative recovery curves could become indication-specific secondary endpoints first because surgery provides a known time zero, meaningful deterioration, and established follow-up windows. A low-risk active-stand or submaximal-exercise battery could follow only if repeatability, missingness, and incremental value clear a much higher bar.

The decisive study design is also specific. One multicenter trial would have to use the same challenge at three or more sites, show repeat-visit reliability strong enough to define minimal detectable change, keep primary-endpoint missingness below 10% in frail older adults or prove robustness to informative missingness, add value beyond frailty and baseline function, and show a treatment-by-recovery interaction aligned with a recognized clinical outcome. If that happens, the bottleneck migrates from clinical validity to standardization and operating economics.

The failure condition is just as clear. A technically clean multicenter study could still show that recovery curves add nothing beyond static reserve measures, or that each stressor produces its own isolated endpoint family. In that world, recovery kinetics remains useful as a mechanistic research tool and never becomes a reusable healthspan endpoint.

What to watch

  • A peer-reviewed paper or registry results posting for NCT04815824 with repeatable post-exercise recovery parameters.
  • A public disclosure explaining whether any analyzable data survive RECOVER's termination at 12 participants.
  • A study across at least 3 sites reporting less than 10% missing primary recovery data in frail older adults.
  • A sponsor or agency document naming a recovery-derived measure and its context of use.
  • An FDA or consortium update resolving the C-Path CHF step-endpoint review after the December 2024 plan submission.

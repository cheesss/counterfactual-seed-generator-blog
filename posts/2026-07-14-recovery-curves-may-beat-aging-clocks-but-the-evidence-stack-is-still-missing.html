Recovery curves will become credible endpoints only after they stop trying to explain all of aging

Longevity research has spent years compressing aging into a resting-state number. Epigenetic clocks, proteomic models, frailty indices, inflammatory panels, and organ-age estimates all try to locate a person on an aging trajectory. Some of those estimates rise during surgery, infection, pregnancy, or other acute stress and then decline during recovery.

The natural alternative is to measure what happens after a defined disturbance: the magnitude of displacement, the speed of recovery, overshoot, failure to return to baseline, and whether different physiological systems recover together.

That does not create a universal healthspan endpoint. It creates a sequence of narrower engineering and clinical questions:

defined challenge → reliable recovery feature → incremental clinical utility → randomized treatment responsiveness → program-specific regulatory acceptance → reusable qualification

The first gate is not regulation. Developers must first identify a recovery construct that is coherent for one stressor, one population, and one patient-relevant outcome. Only then does the validation package become the pacing constraint.

This distinction matters because FDA qualification is voluntary. A sponsor may propose an unqualified measure inside an individual IND, NDA, or BLA. Formal qualification becomes valuable when the same context of use will be reused across drugs, indications, or organizations. It is a scaling route, not a prerequisite for the first trial deployment. (FDA DDT process)

The most plausible path is therefore not a single recovery score that replaces biological age. It is a collection of indication-specific endpoint families: postoperative activity recovery, orthostatic blood-pressure recovery, immune response after vaccination, or molecular recovery after standardized exercise.

Surgery gives the cleanest clock—and still fragments

Surgery repeatedly appears in recovery research because it solves a difficult measurement problem: it provides a known time zero. The insult is clinically important, deterioration is visible, and established outcomes such as pain, disability, complications, activity, and length of stay can be followed over months.

The NIA-supported SPRING program organized physical-resilience research around knee replacement, bone-marrow transplantation, and dialysis initiation. Its RESTORE feasibility cohort moved from 23 participants at baseline to 21 undergoing knee replacement and 19 completing the protocol. That showed that multisystem testing around a major stressor was feasible in older adults. It did not establish multisite reliability, treatment responsiveness, or a reusable endpoint. (SPRING RESTORE)

PRIME-KNEE moved closer to an endpoint-shaped study. Duke launched the study in February 2020 and reached primary completion in October 2023—roughly 44 months later. Its 2026 main analysis included 203 adults aged 60 or older, with pain, disability, and Garmin-derived activity measured before knee replacement and through six months of recovery.

The study’s most important result was fragmentation. About 7% of participants occupied the highest-resilience position across all four outcome domains, while roughly 20% occupied the lowest. Agreement was strongest between the two pain measures. Activity and disability behaved more independently. Only 152 of the 203 analyzed participants had usable step data. (PRIME-KNEE results)

That evidence supports several recovery measures, not one latent healthspan factor. A treatment might improve walking recovery without changing pain, or reduce pain without restoring activity. Combining those effects into a universal score could erase the information that makes the trajectories clinically useful.

Other clinical-stressor studies reinforce the same boundary. The Baltimore Hip Studies followed older adults after hip fracture and found that broad and reduced biomarker sets explained 37% and 27% of variation in an enhanced recovery phenotype. The CHOICE dialysis cohort followed 394 older adults after dialysis initiation and linked declining physical function and improving vitality trajectories with subsequent mortality. These are meaningful prognostic associations. Neither shows that treatment-induced movement in a recovery feature predicts treatment benefit. (Baltimore Hip Studies) (CHOICE study)

Active stand exposes the frailty-data cliff

Major surgery cannot become a routine screening challenge. The search for a lower-burden perturbation therefore moves toward active standing, submaximal exercise, balance perturbation, vascular occlusion, cold exposure, or vaccination.

Active standing is the strongest low-burden candidate in the current evidence. In the TILDA analysis, 4,334 participants underwent beat-to-beat cardiovascular measurement during the transition from lying to standing. Frailty was associated with slower early systolic blood-pressure recovery, including a reported −4.12 mmHg interaction during the 10-to-20-second window.

The attraction is clear. The challenge takes seconds, produces a dense physiological curve, and has an interpretable autonomic mechanism. But repeat-visit orthostatic studies have reported only low-to-moderate reliability for some measures, with intraclass correlations ranging from 0.47 to 0.80. A treatment effect cannot be distinguished from measurement noise until the target feature has a credible minimal detectable change.

Radboud University Medical Center demonstrated the operational problem. Its 2025 study tested active standing, cerebral oxygenation, and grip fatigue in 373 older surgical patients. Beat-to-beat blood-pressure data were available for 91% of the cardiothoracic cohort but only 76% of the older, frailer general-surgery cohort.

The strongest blood-pressure association weakened after adjustment for frailty and comorbidity and did not generalize cleanly across cohorts. The same people for whom a resilience endpoint should be most valuable were the most likely to generate incomplete data. (Radboud study)

Smaller challenge studies have expanded the menu without closing the validation gap. A 50-person study found age associations in cold-immersion, vascular-occlusion, and balance recovery, but not in heart-rate or strength recovery. A 69-person Nebraska study found age and recovery-status differences during repeated platform perturbations, without test-retest or clinical-outcome validation.

These results imply that “safe challenge” is not a product category. Each perturbation recruits different physiological systems, creates different measurement problems, and may map to a different clinical outcome.

Molecular challenges may commercialize before they become endpoints

Exercise and vaccination produce denser molecular responses than active standing, but they also increase burden and cost.

The completed NCT04815824 Veterans Affairs study enrolled 38 participants and compared two standardized exercise modes using repeated blood sampling during exercise, through five hours of recovery, and again after 24 hours. Results are posted publicly. The study shows that randomized crossover collection of molecular recovery data is feasible in older adults.

It does not show that a bone-marker recovery curve measures healthspan, disability, or treatment benefit. Its nearer commercial role is pharmacodynamic: confirming mechanism, selecting dose, or identifying biological responders. (NCT04815824)

RECOVER attempted a stronger perturbation: ten days of bed rest followed by structured rehabilitation or ambulatory recovery. The study terminated after enrolling 12 participants because of insufficient funding. Rather than serving as a near-term evidence catalyst, it illustrates the financing and execution risk of intensive challenge protocols. (RECOVER registry)

Vaccination offers a more standardized immune challenge. Systems-immunology studies have collected transcriptomic, metabolomic, epigenomic, cellular, and antibody measurements before and after influenza vaccination. These programs can generate reproducible pharmacodynamic signatures. Their clinical meaning still centers on immunogenicity, not broad healthspan.

The likely near-term outcome is therefore a reduced assay panel tied to one biological mechanism, not a multi-omic universal resilience score.

Regulatory precedent rewards narrow contexts

The closest successful digital-endpoint precedent is SV95C in Duchenne muscular dystrophy. EMA qualified the wearable-derived stride-velocity measure as a secondary endpoint in 2019 and as a primary endpoint in 2023.

That four-year progression matters, but the analogy has limits. SV95C measures disease-specific ambulation in a defined ambulatory Duchenne population using a specified device and measurement procedure. It does not require a sponsor to administer and standardize a stressor, reconcile several physiological systems, or defend a universal resilience construct. (EMA SV95C opinion)

The C-Path chronic-heart-failure activity program provides a harder base rate. Its letters of intent were accepted in April 2019. Public materials subsequently described concept-of-interest work, an FDA meeting in 2022, a ground-truth collaboration, and submission of a step-count qualification plan in December 2024. In April 2026, the program was still reporting reviewability work.

If familiar step data can occupy a seven-year public evidence cycle, a challenge-derived recovery measure starts with a more difficult task. The challenge dose, baseline, raw signals, algorithm, missingness policy, recovery feature, patient interpretation, and treatment interaction all become part of the measurement system. (C-Path 2026 update)

FDA’s public record also shows the importance of scope. An Actibelt proposal for real-world walking speed in sarcopenia patients recovering from hip-fracture surgery had its letter of intent accepted in 2019. A broader PostopQRS proposal covering recovery after all forms of surgery and anesthesia was not accepted at that stage.

The lesson is not that narrow proposals always succeed. It is that regulators can evaluate a specific population, concept, procedure, and decision. “Recovery after anything” is not a context of use.

Narrow measures are likely to remain evidence-constrained through the late 2020s. A program-specific exploratory or secondary endpoint could appear sooner. Reusable FDA or EMA qualification looks more like a 2029–2031-or-later development under the SV95C and C-Path base rates, not an agency timetable.

The money sits in evidence operations

The visible product is a wearable, assay panel, or dashboard. The scarce asset is the evidence package that connects a defined challenge to raw data, calibration records, missingness analysis, prespecified recovery features, treatment assignment, clinical outcomes, and regulator correspondence.

Current spending is therefore research-service spending. Sponsors pay for clinical sites, participant support, device provisioning, assay consumables, integration, quality control, biostatistics, and submission documentation. Formal qualification may eventually support licensing, but no recovery-kinetics package has demonstrated repeat procurement across unrelated sponsors.

Value could migrate through the following sequence:

commodity sensor or assay → configured measurement service → evidence engineering → outcome-linked dataset → reusable implementation rights

Garmin supplied activity data to PRIME-KNEE, while Pattern Health’s predecessor Greenlight integrated the study workflow. Finapres and Artinis provided instrumentation in the Radboud program. Abbott, Siemens Healthineers, and Beckman Coulter platforms appeared in the exercise-study protocol. Sysnav controls the adjacent SV95C device-and-algorithm package.

These names represent exposure to parts of the chain, not direct recovery-endpoint franchises. Garmin, Abbott, Danaher, and Siemens Healthineers have not disclosed material revenue, backlog, pricing power, or qualification ownership tied to recovery kinetics. Hardware may gain volume while remaining replaceable and competitively priced.

The stronger control points would be a regulator-accepted device–algorithm–protocol package or a multisite dataset connecting standardized challenges, treatment effects, and patient outcomes. Those assets may belong to private endpoint vendors, trial sponsors, or precompetitive consortia. They may also remain sponsor-owned, leaving platforms, laboratories, and CROs with recurring but labor-intensive service revenue.

Standards could expand the market while weakening proprietary rents. CDISC’s digital-health-technology work may reduce bespoke data transformations and improve submission interoperability. That would increase demand for validated implementation and conformance while making custom field mappings less defensible.

The strongest counterargument is that dynamic measures may simply lose

The central commercial risk is not regulatory delay. It is that recovery curves may add burden without adding enough decision value.

PRIME-KNEE tested dual-task gait and functional near-infrared spectroscopy before surgery. Conventional physical, cognitive, and psychological reserve measures predicted postoperative trajectories better than the provocative tests. Radboud’s strongest association weakened after conventional covariate adjustment. These are not documentation failures. They are evidence that a sophisticated physiological test can be less useful than a cheaper baseline measure.

Dynamic endpoints also compete against established frailty scores, functional tests, patient-reported outcomes, and static biomarkers. Those alternatives are easier to collect, easier to interpret, and often more complete in frail populations.

Model dependence adds another risk. An All of Us perioperative wearable preprint estimated recovery at roughly 22 weeks using a remote preoperative baseline and 13 weeks using an immediate baseline. The All of Us analysis is a preprint and is best treated as a modeling warning rather than settled endpoint evidence. A measure that changes materially with an unobvious baseline choice is not ready for treatment-effect interpretation.

The decisive falsifier is straightforward: a technically reliable multicenter recovery measure adds no prognostic information, treatment interaction, sample-size efficiency, or clinical decision value beyond frailty, baseline function, and conventional biomarkers.

In that outcome, the sensors and data pipelines still work. The endpoint thesis does not.

The likely paths through the late 2020s

The first credible success case is perioperative and indication-specific. A multicenter prehabilitation or rehabilitation trial could prespecify one activity, disability, autonomic, or molecular recovery feature and negotiate its use as an exploratory or secondary endpoint within a single development program.

A low-risk active-stand or submaximal-exercise package could follow, but only after a dedicated repeat-visit program demonstrates reliability in the target population, small site effects, acceptable device interchangeability, and primary-measure missingness below 10% or robust handling of informative missingness.

Molecular challenge panels may advance earlier as pharmacodynamic tools. Their success criterion is narrower: a reduced panel reproduces across sites and assay lots, changes predictably after an intervention, and supports dose selection or mechanism confirmation. It does not need to serve as a healthspan surrogate.

The universal-score path has no defensible relief date. It requires evidence that orthostatic, exercise, immune, metabolic, and clinical-stressor recovery share a stable latent factor after measurement error and baseline reserve are removed. Current evidence points in the opposite direction.

The most likely outcome is fragmentation: orthopedic trials use activity and disability recovery, autonomic studies use standing blood pressure, immune-aging studies use vaccination response, and metabolic programs use exercise or meal challenges. That would create several service and evidence markets without producing one dominant healthspan platform.

What to watch

  • By 2027-12-31, FDA or EMA publishes a recovery-specific qualification decision, accepted plan, or letter of support.
  • A three-site study reports repeat-visit reliability of at least ICC 0.75 for its primary recovery feature.
  • A frail-older-adult trial keeps primary recovery-measure missingness below 10% or validates informative-missingness corrections.
  • By 2029-12-31, a randomized multicenter study reports a prespecified treatment-by-recovery interaction tied to patient benefit.
  • By 2028-12-31, two unrelated sponsors license the same recovery algorithm, manual, and change-control package.

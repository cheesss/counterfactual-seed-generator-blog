The real bottleneck in genomic screening starts after diagnosis

Genomic screening can clear the diagnostic gate and still miss the treatment gate. A positive result has to survive confirmatory testing, current phenotype, label fit, payer approval, center acceptance, caregiver logistics, and a dated slot.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-07-14 · 13 min read

In brief

  • 50,000+ Generation participants still do not yield a public treatment-conversion clock.
  • 34 Medicaid programs now test the first shared sickle-cell access corridor.
  • 64 CASGEVY infusions trailed more than 75 activated treatment centers in 2025.
  • 8 LENMELDY centers keep pediatric gene-therapy delivery geographically tight.
  • 15 years of follow-up keeps the treatment episode open after infusion.

The bottleneck sits between diagnosis and reservation

That sequence creates a reservation-grade treatment state. The patient, product, payer, and center have to line up at the same time. The evidence can expire when disease status changes, coverage turns over, a label narrows, a manufacturing slot moves, or the hospital loses capacity.

CMS's sickle-cell pathway shows how long that alignment can take. The federal materials sketch about 30 days of evaluation, 60 to 90 days of washout or chronic transfusion, 3 to 9 inpatient harvest days, 42 to 112 days of manufacturing, 7 to 9 days of conditioning, and a 35-to-45-day admission before 15 years of follow-up begin (CMS state RFA). CMS presents those durations as pathway estimates, not observed medians.

Each row offers strong front-end metrics and weak transition metrics. GUARDIAN disclosed screen-positive and diagnosed counts, yet it did not publish a standard treatment funnel. Generation scaled past 50,000 participants, yet it has not published median confirmation-to-treatment times or an intervention-class breakdown.

Generation also shows why eligibility stays versioned. Genomics England added 97 genes linked to 48 conditions in April 2026 and said it would not reanalyse earlier babies when the list changed (Generation condition policy). A genomic result is a dated entry into a policy regime, not a permanent treatment ticket.

The public clock runs longest outside the lab

The lab phase is the part the public sees best. GUARDIAN reported 72.0% parental consent, 99.6% sequencing success, and a 3.7% positive-screen rate in its interim cohort (JAMA GUARDIAN). The treatment phase remains opaque. The public record does not show how long those cases spent in confirmation, specialist routing, payer review, or treatment preparation.

Florida's pilot shows the same split. The statute created the governance, consent, and reporting shell. Florida State University then assigned the operator a wider role: sequence about 900 genes, arrange confirmatory testing and counseling, connect families to specialized care, train hospitals, and coordinate follow-up (Florida statute) (FSU launch update). FSU described about 900 genes; the statute establishes the pilot but does not set that target.

Illinois makes the paperwork gate concrete. Its Medicaid notice requires a manufacturer agreement and CIBMTR participation before a provider can treat CGT model patients (Illinois Medicaid notice). CMS then pushes the same idea to a multistate scale through standardized prior authorization, managed-care alignment, and out-of-state provider access (CMS FAQ).

That is why the tightness horizon runs through at least the first full 2027 reporting cycle. Sunshine Genetics does not owe its first consortium report until October 15, 2026. CMS does not expect public model evaluations until 2027. Until those datasets arrive, enrollment counts and center counts will grow faster than proof of who owns the delay.

Center counts still hide the real queue

CASGEVY is the clearest warning against simple network metrics. Vertex disclosed 147 first cell collections, 64 infusions, 30 fourth-quarter infusions, and $116 million of 2025 revenue, after saying earlier that more than 75 treatment centers had been activated (Vertex FY2025) (Vertex Q2 2025). Those figures do not identify the failing step, but they do show that nominal coverage and realized throughput can diverge.

The July 1, 2026 label expansion sharpens that point. FDA cleared CASGEVY for patients ages two years and older, and Vertex said the change adds about 5,500 eligible U.S. children while the company now has more than 75 activated U.S. centers (Vertex CASGEVY expansion). Full myeloablative conditioning remains in the pathway. The question shifts from "How many centers exist?" to "How many younger patients can complete phenotyping, counseling, authorization, collection, conditioning, and admission?"

The former bluebird portfolio told the same story before privatization. The company disclosed 70 first cell collections in 2024 across ZYNTEGLO, LYFGENIA, and SKYSONA, more than 70 qualified treatment centers for ZYNTEGLO and LYFGENIA, six for SKYSONA, and an approximate two-quarter lag from collection to infusion (bluebird 2024 10-K). The bottleneck did not disappear when the locator filled out.

LENMELDY and KEBILIDI narrow the route even more. LENMELDY runs through eight U.S. centers, six to eight weeks of manufacturing, a four-to-twelve-week center stay, and 15 years of monitoring (LENMELDY centers) (LENMELDY journey). KEBILIDI requires a confirmed biallelic DDC diagnosis, a named cannula, and a stereotactic-neurosurgery team; Boston Children's describes delivery in a real-time MRI operating room (FDA KEBILIDI) (Boston Children's KEBILIDI). These corridors depend on named teams and rooms, not generic infusion capacity.

The base rate says this buildout takes years

The closest operating base rate is commercial CAR-T. CAR-T already depended on qualified hospitals, chain-of-identity controls, patient-specific manufacturing, and long-term registries. CIBMTR said by May 2024 that more than 13,000 commercial CAR-T recipients had entered its registry orbit, while reporting completeness still sat around 50% to 65% by product (CIBMTR newsletter). Even a mature corridor leaves evidence gaps behind it.

The closest public-health base rate is spinal muscular atrophy screening. SMA joined the Recommended Uniform Screening Panel in 2018. Thirty-four states had implemented by June 2021, and all 50 states plus the District of Columbia were screening by 2024 (SMA rollout study) (Cure SMA). That path still took years, and it pointed to one disease family with a cleaner treatment map than a broad genomic panel.

Genomics England's first 100,000 Genomes buildout offers the institutional base rate. The system spent about five years building sample, consent, lab, and return-of-results infrastructure before it hit its sequencing target (Genomics England origins). Broad newborn genomic screening now asks the health system to add treatment routing on top of that base. No public record in this packet shows a universal scheduler that already solves both jobs.

Who controls the route

The strongest value-capture evidence in this report sits inside product-specific corridors, not inside a neutral national platform (TrakCel) (Cencora) (Cryoport IR) (MaxCyte history) (LENMELDY centers).

That split matters because the economics stay split. Cencora says it handles more than 30,000 cryogenic shipments a year and supports more than 200 CGT manufacturers. Cryoport reports about 50 strategic locations. Those figures show infrastructure depth, yet the record does not show contract values, margins, switching costs, or which vendor shortens cycle time (Cencora) (Cryoport IR). The current evidence supports control-point exposure. It does not close the case for a large neutral orchestration market.

The counterargument has real evidence behind it

The payer-relief layer is not hypothetical. CMS has 34 participating Medicaid programs that represent 84% of Medicaid beneficiaries with sickle-cell disease, and the model requires standardized access criteria, managed-care alignment, and access to qualified providers across state lines (CMS CGT model) (CMS FAQ). If those rules cut authorization and contracting friction, the bottleneck ranking will shift.

The record also shows working screen-to-treatment examples without a neutral scheduler. The Generation Study has described rapid referral in a retinoblastoma case and early growth-hormone treatment after genomic screening (Generation retinoblastoma case) (Generation growth case). SMA screening reached national coverage through state programs and specialist-care networks, not through a new cross-sponsor exchange (Cure SMA).

Treatment burden can filter demand before documentation does. CASGEVY's slow ramp can reflect conditioning, infertility risk, long admissions, and family choice as much as payer friction or center readiness. Vendors also benefit from telling a complexity story. Their product pages prove that coordination infrastructure exists. They do not prove that evidence fragmentation sets the pace of treatment completion.

The narrower thesis survives that attack. A current cross-party evidence bundle is a necessary admission-control layer for the hardest genetic medicines. Public evidence in this packet still leaves demand conversion, treatment burden, manufacturing, and physical capacity as equally credible gates.

The missing dataset still decides the argument

The public record still lacks the product-eligible fraction, a patient-level transition clock, effective center capacity, a consolidated KEBILIDI center roster, and contract economics for the orchestration layer. That absence keeps the thesis in comparative territory. The architecture is visible. The ranking of bottlenecks is not.

The thesis breaks if the 2027 CMS results show no coordination advantage, if 100,000-screen cohorts yield few advanced-therapy-eligible patients, if integrated orchestration performs no better than ordinary sponsor case management, or if center expansions fail to create persistent waits for manufacturing, operating rooms, or beds. In that case, the story moves downstream. Patient choice, conditioning burden, and physical capacity would own the clock.

What to watch

  • The October 15, 2026 Sunshine Genetics consortium report for site lists, budget lines, and referral targets.
  • The first 2027 CMS CGT model evaluation for authorization days, resubmissions, and completed treatments.
  • A GUARDIAN or Generation release that sorts screen-positive cases by intervention class, not total flags.
  • Any sponsor or center disclosure that links CASGEVY collections, releases, and infusions by cohort.
  • A public KEBILIDI center roster or first commercial procedure announcement.

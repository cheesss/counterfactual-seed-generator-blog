The completed renovation occupies roughly 116,000 gross square feet, but ARCO describes 59,000 square feet of dry warehouse alongside freezer, convertible cooler, cold dock and production space.

Cold Storage's Refrigerant Shift Is Changing The Operating Work

The central mechanism is complement formation: changing refrigeration architectures changes the equipment, technical evidence, controls and skills needed to make a plant operate. Integrators connect those requirements; maintenance organizations keep the installation useful. The recurring pattern is standards interoperability, where permissible refrigerants, rated components, local acceptance and operating procedures have to work together.

That mechanism supports an ecosystem-expansion argument, but not yet a measured market boom. Refrigeration already has substantial equipment and service businesses. The question is how much additional work the transition creates, who performs it, and whether owners pay separately for it.

Several Routes Through A Long Transition

EPA's current sector table sets a 700-GWP installation threshold for new cold-storage warehouse systems from July 27, 2026. From January 1, 2032, the threshold becomes 150 for charges of at least 200 pounds, excluding high-temperature cascade sides, and 300 for smaller charges and high-temperature cascade sides. Repair components for existing systems are distinguished from new installations. Construction start alone does not settle installation compliance. EPA sector table

The rule prohibits refrigerants at or above the applicable threshold; the numbers are not inclusive allowances. Its reconsideration also reflects safety, code, feasibility and availability concerns. R-513A provides an interim option in some applications, but comments recorded by EPA identify limitations for freezer duty. Regulatory permission does not establish suitability at every temperature. May 2026 final rule

The industry's starting point matters. EPA cites a 2023 GCCA survey reporting ammonia use among 91% of respondents, synthetic F-gas among 10%, and CO2 among 5%. Multiple responses were possible. These are not exclusive market shares, a census, or the mix of new projects. The evidence points toward substantial incumbent natural-refrigerant capability rather than an industry awaiting its first viable alternative.

The timetable is current policy, not an assured six-year revenue trajectory. EPA lists active judicial challenges, including ALTA Refrigeration/CUSER, industry associations, states and NRDC. A challenge is not itself a suspension or repeal, but it reinforces the need to treat future compliance dates as legally contingent. EPA judicial-review register

The Installed Package Reveals The Business

Classic Refrigeration's Alpine account identifies an EVAPCO LMP Purity rack and evaporators, Guntner coils and gas coolers, and Pride floor-heating panels. Classic describes engineering through commissioning and controls startup, and advertises 24/7 maintenance support. It reports approximately 40,000 square feet of freezer/cooler space within a 116,766-square-foot building. Classic's project account

That is concrete supplier and integration activity. It is not a disclosed annual service payment, independently measured reliability result or refrigeration-only contract price. The visible customer is Alpine; the contractor coordinates the package. How the purchase price divides among equipment, engineering and continuing support remains undisclosed.

Nor did the product family originate with the latest federal rule. EVAPCO's 2023 brochure already documents Purity CO2 racks, gas coolers, evaporators and controls integration. Its emphasis on simplified installation and rapid commissioning also supplies a counterargument: product development can reduce field complexity rather than continually increase specialist work. EVAPCO's historical product brochure

The commercial distinction is between an established family gaining deployments and a genuinely new branch attracting its first customers. Confusing the two exaggerates novelty and can misidentify who owns the customer relationship.

Seven Projects, Different Kinds Of Evidence

The named cases illustrate breadth, not a representative adoption sample.

Valley Fine Foods in Yuba City is a food-processing conversion, not a whole-warehouse replacement. The CEC's June 2025 publication describes two R-22 compressor systems replaced by one transcritical CO2 rack, together with changes to spiral freezers. It reports emissions reductions exceeding the grant application's expectation. Equipment renewal and process changes occurred together, so this cannot isolate a refrigerant-only performance effect. CEC project publication

A successful subsidized project does not establish universal payback or a fleet-wide failure rate. Its commercial relevance is the completed integration of refrigeration into production; generalizing its economics requires comparable operating boundaries and repair records.

At Arcadia Cold's Joliet facility, LJC identifies a roughly 294,000-square-foot building equipped with transcritical CO2. LJC and Clayco delivered design-build work for Saxum, and LJC describes structural engineering, permit drawings and coordination with refrigeration systems. Those responsibilities do not establish that LJC holds the refrigeration maintenance contract. LJC project record

Shoreline Freezers' current operator page advertises 188,000 square feet of cold storage in southern New Jersey and transcritical CO2 refrigeration. This is an operating-company description, not a dated commissioning certificate or audited capacity series. It supports the presence of a customer-facing installation without revealing its equipment or service economics. Shoreline Freezers

CORE X Complete in Sturbridge provides a useful historical/current distinction. The operator announced that its approximately 83,000-square-foot warehouse opened to customers on October 18, 2022, with Ti Cold participating. That opening announcement did not name the refrigerant. October 2022 announcement A later operator description explicitly identifies CO2 refrigeration at Complete. That supports current architecture, not a separately documented original equipment schedule. CORE X network description

RL Cold in Ridgeville contributes a different observation. Primus says it employed a third-party commissioning authority on the completed LEED Gold project. This confirms identifiable verification work, but not that a refrigerant change caused the commission or its price. Whole-building sustainability programs can purchase similar services independently of low-GWP substitution. Primus project account

Alpine, Valley, Arcadia, Shoreline, Sturbridge and Ridgeville therefore show different parts of the operating chain. Their footprints, energy duties and procurement histories should not be collapsed into one capacity or cost comparison.

The seventh case, Lineage Hutchins, remains a plan rather than operating evidence. Texas's architectural-barriers registration lists 606,375 gross square feet, including offices, $162 million estimated construction cost, a September 2025 start and June 2027 completion. Its review status concerns accessibility, not refrigeration commissioning. Texas project registration

TGW's June 9, 2026 announcement identifies warehouse automation and anticipated end-2027 go-live. Neither record establishes the refrigeration architecture or its service package. The construction and go-live dates cannot be used to measure a commissioning delay, and the pre-July-2026 start makes this an unsuitable clean example of post-rule project origination. TGW announcement

Who Controls The Transition

The control points are distributed. EPA sets federal restrictions. Equipment manufacturers develop compatible families. Laboratories supply testing and certification evidence within applicable scopes. Designers and local authorities address project acceptance. Integrators connect hardware and controls; owner teams and service contractors retain operating responsibility.

UL's low-GWP offering includes component certification, pressure-fatigue testing, refrigerant-release and leakage work, and training. These are commercially offered capabilities, not evidence of a cold-storage certification queue or an unusual fee premium. A service catalog demonstrates supply; paid volume is a separate question. UL services

Training makes the same distinction visible. IIAR posts nonmember prices of $1,590 for IIAR 2, $850 for IIAR 4, $1,350 for PSM/RMP and $1,390 for IIAR 6. Member and institutional discounts apply. These prices establish purchasable instruction, not realized tuition revenue, enrollment growth or a national technician shortage. IIAR course catalog

The owner ultimately needs responsibility to remain clear when equipment crosses organizational boundaries. A rack manufacturer, controls provider and installer can each fulfill its own scope while leaving an unresolved operational problem between them. The economic hypothesis is that coordinating those interfaces becomes valuable enough to sustain a continuing customer relationship. Whether that value is separately invoiced remains open.

An Earlier Deadline For Parts Of The Legacy Fleet

New-system restrictions are not the only source of operating work. EPA's January 2026 fact sheet describes automatic leak detection for covered commercial and industrial-process appliances with at least 1,500 pounds of charge, the specified HFC/substitute criterion above GWP or exchange value 53, and installation from 2017 onward.

Covered 2017-2025 appliances face a January 1, 2027 deadline. For newer appliances, installation-related deadlines apply. Annual auditing and calibration continue; direct sensing and indirect analysis are available approaches. This is not a blanket mandate for pure ammonia or CO2. EPA automatic leak-detection fact sheet

Consequently, monitoring and compliance work can grow while an owner retains an existing refrigerant architecture. That is a different causal pathway from selling a new low-GWP rack.

The R-22 precedent supports coexistence. EPA's historical guidance explained that the 2020 end of new U.S. production and import would still permit existing systems to use previously produced, recovered and reclaimed material. Its lesson is asset persistence, not that its old leak-repair provisions are current law. EPA's 2014 phaseout guidance

Operating Data And The Limits Of Lock-In

Dover's 2025 filing describes a DFR cloud product spanning CO2 deployment, diagnostics, warranty management and maintenance. Production data are intended to shorten startup and reduce errors such as incorrect high-pressure settings. This establishes product formation, not a disclosed warehouse subscription business. Dover 10-K

Configuration and diagnostic history could help an equipment supplier remain involved after installation. But continuing influence is not necessarily exclusive control. EVAPCO LMP's training manual identifies compatibility with several controls suppliers, including Danfoss, CPC and Micro Thermo. Interoperability is an explicit part of the system offering. EVAPCO LMP manual

The decisive commercial questions are who can access operating records, who authorizes changes, how warranty responsibility is allocated, and whether software is paid, bundled or owner-controlled. More data alone settles none of them.

Durable Need Does Not Mean Accelerating Expenditure

Lineage's first-half 2026 recurring maintenance capital was $64 million versus $74 million a year earlier. Repairs and maintenance expense rose to $105 million from $100 million. Refrigeration appears within broader categories; Freon-to-ammonia conversions can appear in integration capital. These totals neither isolate refrigeration nor equal independent contractors' revenue. Lineage quarterly filing

Chemours supplies another counterexample to straight-line substitution. Q2 2026 Opteon sales were $337 million versus $375 million in Q2 2025, while Freon rose to $150 million from $123 million. These are global product-family sales across applications, affected by price and mix, not warehouse refrigerant volumes. Chemours sales table

Three Outcomes To Watch

From an October 11, 2026 vantage point, three conditional outcomes remain plausible, without evidence sufficient to assign numerical probabilities.

Natural refrigerants may absorb most procurement. That would support component and service continuity while weakening expectations of a newly formed A2L warehouse economy.

Incumbents may absorb the complements. Standardized packages, internal training and bundled diagnostics could improve operation without creating separate suppliers or higher service margins.

Lifecycle coordination may become a more explicit purchase. Named commissioning scopes, renewed service agreements and paying diagnostics customers would support expansion beyond the initial equipment transaction.

The comparison must control for cooling duty, building scope, asset age and inflation. Missing public contracts do not disprove activity; merely listing technical requirements does not prove incremental expenditure.

The refrigeration transition is changing the choices that operating organizations must connect. The strongest supported finding is an established service ecosystem adapting around those choices. A larger paid ecosystem remains a proposition to test in customer contracts, responsibility splits and operating results, not infer from a GWP deadline alone.

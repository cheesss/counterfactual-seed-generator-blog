Forming the Gearbox: Schaeffler’s Attempt to Move Humanoid Strain-Wave Drives from Precision Machining to High-Volume Forming

A cheaper way to shape a precision reducer could shift manufacturing work from repeated cutting into reusable tooling and process control. Schaeffler has the relevant industrial history. The commercial test is whether its new humanoid product wins stable designs, accepted production and repeat orders.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-10-04 · Updated 2026-10-23 · 11 min read

Schaeffler's formed gearbox faces a repeat-production test

This is a process-substitution story: engineering moves into the tool upfront, and recurring work falls if enough good parts follow. The recurring manufacturing-tooling pattern makes customer acceptance and design stability as important as the speed of the forming operation.

In brief

  • Schaeffler described formed robotic flexsplines in 2021, well before its new humanoid offer.
  • Broader humanoid production orders establish demand; component selection determines which manufacturing route benefits.
  • Dedicated tooling earns its return through stable designs, repeat volume and dependable field performance.

Precision moves into the process

A strain-wave reducer provides compact reduction through a flexible toothed member, the flexspline. Repeated deformation is part of its operation. Tooth accuracy, wall geometry and fatigue resistance therefore belong to the same engineering problem. Original strain-wave study

Schaeffler's August 13 announcement claims manufacturing steps for key components take seconds instead of minutes, material consumption falls by more than 75%, and torque and efficiency remain comparable to conventionally manufactured components. It reports completed validation and plans Germany-first mass production in 2027. The company's automotive lineage includes more than two million formed strain-wave gearboxes supplied over the preceding decade. Product announcement

The economic mechanism is to encode more geometry in a reusable tool. The supplier commits process engineering and capital earlier, then recovers that commitment through less recurring work. Precision still has to survive material flow, treatment, finishing and inspection.

For the buyer, the denominator is an accepted unit. Scrap consumes inputs without creating a saleable part; corrective finishing can restore quality while giving back the conversion saving. A faster forming step increases complete-line output only when that step was pacing production.

The purchasing comparison consequently extends from blank to field service. It includes tool preparation, residual machining, metrology, assembly, qualification and expected interventions. Material-input savings sit within that overall comparison rather than becoming a second benefit to add to the manufacturing-cost claim.

The older business strengthens the case

Schaeffler's April 13, 2021 DuraWave RTWH release already described a precision-formed flexspline for lightweight robots and cobots. It linked forming to material properties, alloy selection, and heat and surface treatment, with bearings and motors offered alongside the gearbox. Hannover Messe release

The operating history goes beyond an exhibition offer. In January 2022, Schaeffler said its cobot strain-wave gearbox with motor and sensors had reached the point of volume production. That supports historical industrialization of the family, with a milestone more concrete than a catalogue listing. January 24, 2022 release

Older patents also show why the distinction between forming and machining needs care. A 1990 flexspline patent combines deep drawing with machining and allows formed or machined teeth. Schaeffler's 2017 application describes moving material outward into a toothed die.

The new offer is thus an application and industrialization proposition with an established manufacturing ancestry. Historical product-family activity makes execution more plausible; the particular humanoid design still has its own qualification clock. Procurement teams are evaluating a repeatable process-and-part package under a particular load spectrum.

Read the order book at component level

On October 13, Schaeffler reported a humanoid order book of approximately EUR400 million following two customer additions in Greater China. This is evidence of broader component demand. Its commercial importance depends on conversion into delivered and paid-for components. Preliminary results

Slide 7 supplies two essential details. Production across multiple products had started for two customers; three others were scheduled within six months. Its footnote says the order book excludes launches with announced partnerships. The reciprocal robot partnerships therefore cannot simply explain away the disclosed total. October presentation

Product mapping is the next useful observation. Different actuator parts and gearbox architectures can contribute to the same corporate order book. A disclosure naming the selected formed reducer, platform and delivery status would connect demand to this manufacturing route. A repeat lot would show that acceptance had survived another production cycle.

Two purchases create different incentives

Humanoid's May 13 agreement places Schaeffler on both sides of a commercial relationship. The factory buys robot service; the robot developer buys actuators. Humanoid describes a binding phased deployment under a Robot-as-a-Service model, with maintenance, fleet software, updates, support and performance management included. A separate five-year actuator agreement covers more than half of wheeled-platform joint-actuator demand through 2031, with a seven-digit quantity expected. Humanoid's announcement

The service obligation gives reliability a payer. Lower component conversion cost can be overwhelmed by replacement labor, disrupted work and repeated site visits. A dependable joint can consequently command value above its immediate material or machining saving.

The relationship also offers a learning channel. Factory experience can inform component revisions, and component expertise can improve integration. That is an economic inference: its value will appear in fewer interventions, quicker deployment or better productive availability.

The actuator arrangement establishes a purchasing channel. The formed variant's actual part selection and repeat call-offs would establish its participation in that channel.

Assembly, training and operation follow separate clocks

Taicang provides a concrete manufacturing milestone. Schaeffler's October 20 announcement describes a roughly 7,500-square-meter facility, including an assembly line for joint modules such as linear and rotary actuators. Six humanoids at five stations handle, join, inspect, label and package parts. The reported mass-production launch concerns those components; assembly is a different operation from forming precision teeth. Taicang announcement

AEON follows a deployment progression. Hexagon's April 22 agreement describes a completed 2025 pilot loading, unloading and inspecting parts at a multi-machine station, followed by a plan for at least 1,000 robots across Schaeffler's network by 2032. Hexagon's rollout record

The October 15 Erlangen Humanoid Gym case concerns teams training and validating AEON capabilities before factory-floor deployment. That work can reduce integration risk and build site expertise. Gym case

Cheraw supplies a more direct operating observation. Schaeffler's April AGM address identifies Digit transporting heavy components at its South Carolina plant. Agility's September 29 release names Schaeffler among Digit's commercial-environment users. These are reported task operations, a distinct milestone from training or a future fleet target. CEO address, Agility release

Taken together, the cases establish industrial activity around the component business. Identifying the gearbox inside any one robot requires a component-specific record.

Who controls the transition

Schaeffler's Höchstadt tool center already makes punches and dies and combines development, production and toolmaking. Its 2022 opening record describes automated machining and a servo press used to represent customer-press requirements. Tool Technology Center

The first return from substitution could remain inside Schaeffler as productivity or process knowledge. It need not create a new outside equipment market. Die compensation, material control, tool maintenance and measurement can be the capabilities that preserve the saving.

The simple break-even relationship is additional fixed cost divided by recurring saving per accepted unit, assuming comparable output and a stable design. Frequent revisions shorten the useful life of the tool; repeated approved lots spread its cost.

Price then decides who captures the benefit. The supplier can retain savings, pass them through or exchange them for a larger supply position. A service operator can accept a higher piece price for lower downtime. Those outcomes distribute one economic benefit rather than creating independent pools of revenue.

Competitors can win elsewhere in the joint

Harmonic Drive Systems reported in November 2024 that mass production had begun for some humanoid customers while prototype supply continued. Existing qualification and application experience give an incumbent something tangible to defend. HDS presentation, slide 29

Green Harmonic's corrected FY2025 report records RMB570.7 million in revenue and the reducer sales shown above. Its reporting combines industrial and embodied-robotics applications. That operating base can fund customer support and further development while serving demand across several markets. Corrected filing

Other suppliers compete on integration. Nidec's December 2024 Smart-FLEXWAVE BD launch combines a reducer with a safety torque sensor and reports functional-safety certification for that sensor. Nabtesco markets compact torque capacity and longer service life against its selected conventional comparators. Nidec release, Nabtesco specifications

These propositions can change the purchase decision even when forming reduces factory conversion cost. Existing acceptance reduces switching work; integrated sensing can simplify system development; another architecture can better suit a joint's requirements.

Schaeffler itself pursues planetary gearboxes with VinDynamics, including joint application-data collection for actuator optimization and prospective maintenance services. Its portfolio reflects the diversity of joint requirements. April 22 partnership

Commercial history cuts both ways

The Melior Motion business Schaeffler agreed to acquire in 2022 had real operating activity: approximately EUR23 million of 2021 revenue and planetary gearboxes installed in Kuka's KR Cybertech and KR Iontec. Those are named industrial applications, distinct from the new humanoid strain-wave branch. Acquisition announcement

Technical acceptance still leaves customer economics exposed. Schaeffler's FY2024 accounts record EUR39 million of intangible impairment in a precision-gearbox automation unit, citing difficult markets and a considerable revenue decline with one main customer. Annual report, note 4.1

In August 2026, GAM completed a separate acquisition of PSC production technology and assets from Schaeffler and formed GAM-Melior in Hameln. PSC technology already underpinned GAM's GPL product. This is a documented transfer of manufacturing control toward an existing downstream user. GAM's completion record

These are adjacent-business records. Their lesson is that customer concentration and control of production can determine the commercial outcome even after a precision product reaches the market.

Alternative manufacturing research adds a technical boundary. The 2016 JPL/Caltech metallic-glass study demonstrated working flexsplines and observed cracking in fatigue tests that lacked the outer spline's normal support. In 2022, Amorphology and AddiTec reported a six-inch steel prototype combining additive manufacture and CNC finishing. NASA separately identifies research suppliers for metallic-glass feedstock, flexsplines and planetary components. Original study, hybrid prototype, NASA project

These routes reinforce the same distinction: making geometry successfully and delivering a qualified lifetime are separate achievements.

Three possible outcomes

In the substitution case, a stable joint wins customer acceptance, repeat lots preserve durability and yield, and production work moves toward the formed route. Manufacturing and procurement records would connect the technical claim to commercial displacement.

In the coexistence case, forming becomes a useful portfolio option while established suppliers and other joint architectures retain substantial roles. Schaeffler gains a product without rewriting the market.

In the reversal case, redesign, tool replacement, rework or field interventions consume the recurring saving. A customer reverting to a machined route because of precision or durability would directly challenge the proposed adoption mechanism.

Limits: public records checked through October 23, 2026 do not establish this branch's named customer acceptance, production yield, die life, representative fatigue distribution, selling price or field returns. Cost and performance comparisons remain supplier claims; the redistribution of manufacturing value is an analytical inference.

What to watch

  • By June 30, 2027; base October 23, 2026: a public record of accepted live-production box handling at Herzogenaurach under the Humanoid phased plan. This tests deployment, separately from gearbox selection.
  • By December 31, 2027; base October 23, 2026: German series output explicitly identifying the formed humanoid reducer against the product roadmap.
  • By December 31, 2028; base October 23, 2026: a named OEM/platform series nomination for this formed product in a supplier record or corresponding customer announcement.

These are disclosure forecasts: each requires a dated qualifying public record by its deadline. The decisive economic transition is repeated procurement of accepted joints. That is when a reusable tool can replace recurring cutting work as a demonstrated advantage.

Aging biomarkers still fail the transfer test

Aging-biomarker developers already know how to publish predictive models. Large cohorts and high-plex assays can produce strong organ-age and mortality associations. The field's problem starts when that result has to travel across laboratories, matrices, software versions and sponsor programs.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-07-14 · 11 min read

The field can already get a signal; it cannot yet move that signal safely

In 2025, the UK Biobank Olink study used 2,916 proteins from 44,498 participants to build 11 organ-age models, and mortality hazard rose from 2.3 for people with two to four aged organs to 8.3 for people with eight or more. The direct Olink-SomaScan comparison, however, came from 601 dual-platform samples with 1,823 overlapping proteins, and organ-age correlations ranged roughly from 0.3 to 0.8. That supports biological signal. It does not prove that one platform can replace the other in a drug program or a pooled evidence base. UK Biobank organ age earlier SomaScan study

FDA's own process makes the distinction plain. A sponsor can use a biomarker inside one IND, NDA or BLA path without formal qualification if it locks the assay, software and laboratory. The harder gate appears later, when the same result must survive laboratory transfer, assay substitution, version changes or reuse across programs. Portability is the issue here, not the right to run one closed study. FDA context of use FDA qualification process

FDA's first usable path is narrow

The clearest aging-adjacent regulatory case remains circulating progerin in Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome. The 2018 submission drew on one 25-participant treatment dataset and one 36-participant dataset, then proposed progerin as a pharmacodynamic or response biomarker with possible surrogate potential. FDA accepted the project into the Biomarker Qualification Program, but it pushed the intended use toward proof of concept, dose-response characterization and confirmation of mechanism. Progerin LOI FDA decision letter

FDA's questions went straight at transfer and interpretation. The agency asked for sample inventories, collection and storage procedures, stability, precision, reproducibility, linearity, reference ranges, within-subject and between-subject variability, and standardized clinical outcomes. It also stated that qualifying a biomarker would not qualify one proprietary assay. That is the operating lesson for normal-aging tools: the first credible win is likely to be one matrix, one assay version, one population and one bounded pharmacodynamic or enrichment claim. FDA decision letter

The public record reviewed here did not surface a later Qualification Plan or final qualification decision for the progerin project. That absence does not prove the program stopped. It does show how much evidence FDA can demand even when the biology is direct and the disease context is narrow.

NIST can build a ladder, but not the last mile

The most concrete adjacent build comes from NIST's cell-free-DNA methylation work with the National Cancer Institute's Early Detection Research Network and LGC Clinical Diagnostics. NIST posted the invitation in April 2023, the manuscript was received in May 2025, and the version of record appeared in July 2026. By then the team had built five nominal methylation levels from 0% to 100%, tested them across six laboratories, produced 100 sets, placed 40 sets in an NIH repository, and shown at least 30 months of frozen stability. NIST cfDNA exercise NIST describes these as candidate research-use materials, not certified aging standards. NIST project page

That is real infrastructure. It also stops short of the thing aging-clock developers actually need. The materials did not fully reproduce clinical cell-free DNA, and whole-genome bisulfite sequencing did not provide sufficient results in the first round. A ladder can expose assay-specific bias. It cannot by itself prove that a small within-person aging signal survives specimen handling, extraction differences, software updates and platform changes. NIST cfDNA exercise

The missing asset is a transfer protocol, not a single vial. Someone has to pair assigned controls with retained human bridge aliquots, version-lock rules and follow-up outcomes that show the same signal still means the same thing after a handoff.

The named platform cases fail when the assay has to travel

The methylation workflow already shows why that handoff is hard. A 2022 study across five datasets and 2,308 Illumina arrays found processing-day, slide and array-position effects, and it identified 4,649 probes that repeatedly required substantial correction. The same paper warned that aggressive correction can also erase biology when study design and batch structure are confounded. array batch study

That leaves sponsors with a familiar choice. They can lock one core laboratory and one preprocessing stack, or they can pay for retained controls and repeat bridging each time the platform changes. There is no public relief date for that second problem. Every array revision, reagent change and pipeline update can reopen it.

Proteomics shows the same pattern in a more commercial setting. The Olink bridge study carried six to eight retained bridge samples across seven studies, 36 plates, three assay versions and about 2.5 years. Aggregate inter-study variation fell from about 42% to 26.2% after normalization, yet 36 of 80 evaluable proteins still failed the separate serum-versus-EDTA-plasma comparability test. The field already has a proprietary relief valve. It does not yet have full replacement-level portability. Olink bridge study

Shared materials solve pieces of the problem, not the whole chain

Metabolomics is the cleanest warning against overclaiming. SRM 1950 gives laboratories one shared frozen-plasma material, and the current NIST listing prices it at $1,302 per unit. In the 31-laboratory lipidomics round robin, participants reported 1,527 unique lipids and reached consensus values for only 339. Later work expanded the high-confidence SRM 1950 catalogue to 1,058 metabolites, which is useful progress, but it still looks like analyte-by-analyte harmonization rather than one transferable metabolomic-age unit. SRM 1950 listing SRM 1950 lipidomics SRM 1950 database expansion

Glycomics and immune-age work land in the same place. The 2024 glycan-clock precision paper ran 312 technical replicates over 26 days and kept 23 of 27 peaks below 10% coefficient of variation, with a key intermediate trait between 1.24% and 1.59%. NISTmAb showed how large an interlaboratory glycosylation program can get, with 103 reports from 76 laboratories and a later set of 13 purified glycan materials. EQAPOL did something similar for complex immune assays through common specimens, SOPs and recurring proficiency rounds. None of those precedents created an aging-specific transfer system on their own. glycan precision NISTmAb NIST glycan standards EQAPOL

The earliest visible public relief horizon still appears to be 2028

The hardest missing input is still longitudinal human evidence. Synthetic ladders can be remade. A finite pool of consented, intervention-linked specimens with repeat measures and later outcomes cannot. CALERIE Legacy is the clearest live case in the public record: the original trial enrolled 220 participants, the follow-up study allows up to 216 participants, and the protocol links biomarker work to clinical assessments, blood, urine and optional specimen banking 10 to 15 years after the original intervention. CALERIE Legacy CALERIE consent

The schedule matters. ClinicalTrials.gov lists an estimated primary completion date of January 31, 2027 and an estimated study completion date of January 31, 2028. The public record reviewed here also did not surface a normal-aging Letter of Intent, Qualification Plan or final qualification action. That combination makes the portability gate look binding through at least 2028 for any sponsor that wants a reusable normal-aging tool rather than one closed program. CALERIE Legacy FDA context of use

The base rate points to layered standardization

The most optimistic historical analogue is HbA1c. NGSP began in 1996 and made routine measurements portable by linking methods back to DCCT values through reference laboratories, split-sample comparisons and continuing certification. That is what a durable cross-program measurement system looks like when the field has one disease context and one widely accepted clinical interpretation. HbA1c base rate

The less comforting analogue is troponin. Clinical practice adopted troponin assays even though manufacturers retained method-specific thresholds and epitope effects. Flow-cytometry proficiency and NISTmAb sit somewhere between those two poles: common specimens and repeated rounds improve interlaboratory behavior, but they do not erase biology or create one universal decision rule. Aging biomarkers probably resolve in layers for the same reason. Closed-platform uses can arrive first, reusable platform-specific tools can follow, and only a few high-value contexts may justify neutral cross-platform qualification. troponin harmonization review

The rent sits with whoever keeps the assay moving

If portability matters, the spending does not stop at the clock developer. It moves into bridge-sample inventories, lot and software requalification, central-laboratory validation, sample logistics, assay-version equivalence work and controlled access to longitudinal specimens. Some of the strongest control points sit with actors no investor can buy: FDA defines the acceptable context of use, NIST anchors public measurement infrastructure, and cohort custodians hold specimen rights and outcome linkage.

The clearest public-company exposure sits one layer lower, inside platform control. Thermo Fisher paid about $3.1 billion for Olink in July 2024, and Illumina agreed to pay $350 million plus up to $75 million in contingent consideration for SomaLogic before completing the acquisition in January 2026. Those deals prove that version control, assay ecosystems and vendor normalization can hold value. They do not prove that a neutral biological-age unit will emerge, and the public record reviewed here did not surface aging-specific revenue, margins or sponsor-contract visibility for broader instrument names such as Waters, Agilent, Bio-Rad, Labcorp or Quest. Thermo Fisher-Olink Illumina-SomaLogic

The counterargument, and what would prove this wrong

That counterargument is strong. FDA already provides a program-specific bypass around formal qualification, and the Olink bridge study proves that a vendor can manage part of the drift problem inside one ecosystem. A sponsor that keeps one assay, one matrix, one software build and one central laboratory may never need a neutral reference hierarchy to run a useful pharmacodynamic or enrichment study. FDA qualification process Olink bridge study

The deeper objection is clinical, not analytical. Functional measures such as frailty, gait, grip strength, fitness and clinical events may carry more decision value than molecular clocks. A 2025 commentary argued that molecular aging clocks remain largely associative and should be benchmarked against functional measures, and a 2026 clinical review found limited evidence on deployment, responsiveness and meaningful change. If that judgment holds, metrology becomes a secondary service problem rather than the main bottleneck. functional-measure commentary JCI review

That critique narrows the thesis instead of killing it. The claim is not that every aging biomarker needs a universal standard before anyone can use it. The claim is that portability, pooling and reusable qualification demand more transfer infrastructure than the field's public story usually admits.

What would disprove the thesis is not another commentary piece but one of three public outcomes:

  • A prospective intervention study changes laboratories or major platforms and still preserves prespecified classifications, treatment-response estimates and later outcomes without retained bridge samples or external proficiency testing.
  • FDA accepts a normal-aging biomarker for consequential program-specific use, and later sponsors reproduce the same decision with ordinary platform QC rather than shared reference infrastructure.
  • Standardized molecular aging scores fail to improve enrichment, dose selection or outcome prediction once conventional biomarkers and functional measures are included.

What to watch

  • A public FDA Letter of Intent or Qualification Plan for a normal-aging pharmacodynamic or enrichment biomarker.
  • A blinded multisite ring trial that uses shared human aliquots across at least two aging-biomarker technologies.
  • A vendor or sponsor paper showing assay-version or platform substitution with stable participant classifications and treatment-response estimates.
  • CALERIE Legacy results released after the estimated January 31, 2027 and January 31, 2028 milestones.
  • A recurring aging-specific proficiency catalog from CAP-, EQAPOL- or consortium-style operators.

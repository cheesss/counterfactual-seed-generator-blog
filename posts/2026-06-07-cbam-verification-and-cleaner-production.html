CBAM's verification problem is the price of proving a cleaner plant

A producer selling into Europe can lose the benefit of cleaner production at an unexpected point: the handoff of its records. Under the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, installation-specific emissions must become acceptable, independently verified information before an importer can use them as actual values. The physical improvement and the evidence supporting it are separate pieces of work.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-06-07 · 10 min read

CBAM's Cleaner-Plant Advantage Needs Proof

That is the constraint worth watching. Defaults provide another compliance route, so missing plant evidence does not by itself establish a universal barrier to importing. Instead, it can prevent a producer from demonstrating an emissions advantage. The recurring system pattern is evidence gating: technical performance gains commercial recognition through a chain of records, qualified scrutiny and accountable reporting. Commission framework

Research and forecast base: November 17, 2026.

In brief

  • Verified actuals can distinguish a cleaner producer from the applicable default.
  • Existing assurance firms are extending their services into CBAM.
  • Supplier relationships determine whether usable factory records reach the importer.
  • Repeated evidence work creates a service opportunity; persistent scarcity remains unproven.

Two Different Gates

Authorisation determines who can act as the CBAM declarant. Accreditation qualifies the independent verifier examining an installation's emissions. Employing a filing agent does not automatically transfer the declarant's responsibility. These distinctions matter when an importer buys a package described simply as compliance support. Amending law

A producer choosing the actual-emissions route has to monitor the relevant production processes, allocate emissions under the prescribed methodology and supply records that withstand examination. The verifier needs competence in the relevant activities; an independent reviewer examines the verification before the report is issued. A generic footprint calculation cannot perform all those jobs. Accreditation and verification rules

The commercial decision is a comparison between complete liabilities under lawful methods. An importer and producer should compare the default route with accepted actuals, then subtract incremental monitoring, assurance, software and coordination costs from the benefit they can retain. Free-allocation adjustments and carbon-price deductions must follow the conditions applicable to each route. This is an economic decision rule, rather than evidence that every plant will save money.

Even defaults require maintained infrastructure. July's correcting regulation replaced default-value annexes after identifying missing or incorrect production-route indicators and other errors. Those indicators connect emissions defaults to the free-allocation calculation. Keeping classification and legal parameters current is useful work whichever method a customer chooses. Regulation 2026/1740

The Factory Behind The Trader

Inogen describes an Italian kitchenware importer whose Chinese traders stood between it and the factories holding the required emissions data. Anew's response was practical: bring traders and factories into joint meetings, establish data owners, provide collection forms and review the returns. The importer was the service client; the purchased work addressed coordination and documentation. Inogen case

The initial failure was access. A calculation engine could not answer questions that had never reached the producing installation. The case illustrates why the constraint may sit in a commercial relationship before it reaches an auditor's desk. It also suggests where a service provider can earn its place: organizing a repeatable exchange between parties with different information and incentives.

Complex goods add another dependency. A processor's own operating records may be usable while an upstream precursor remains poorly documented. Article 15 allows actual emissions for the production processes within the installation to be combined with defaults for one or more precursors. For upstream actuals, the verification rules require a satisfactory report with appropriate accreditation scope and reporting period. Calculation rules, precursor assurance requirements

That mixed route changes the economics of an imperfect supply chain. Improving one part can become useful before every supplier is ready. It also makes careful process boundaries important: the service must establish which records support which part of the goods' embedded emissions.

Assurance Supply Is Adapting

The response is already visible among established firms. Accredia's September 28, 2026 decision extended the validation and verification accreditations of Bureau Veritas Italia, RINA Services and SGS ICS Italia into CBAM. Their activity-group scopes differ; the revised decision lists a narrower set for SGS than for RINA. Customers need the applicable scope, rather than a group-wide assurance brand. Accredia announcement, revised decision

Swedac's October announcement explicitly identifies Intertek Certification AB as an existing EU ETS verifier adding CBAM accreditation. SGS Belgium and DNV separately announced further accreditations on November 6. Taken at their respective evidential levels, these records show familiar service businesses extending into a new regulatory branch. Swedac: Forsta svenska organet ackrediterat for cbam verifiering, SGS announcement, DNV announcement

ISSC offers a different route. Its RvA annex identifies Damietta, Egypt, and a CBAM grant dated October 22, 2026 under temporary restrictive conditions. RvA's policy requires attendance at the first activity, examination of its file and a positive additional-assessment decision for conversion to regular accreditation. That is the reason to watch conversion, rather than count the announcement as completed customer work. ISSC scope, RvA policy, Article 74

Assessment work can also travel without moving the accreditation decision. Accredia's October agreement with Korea's NIER envisages training and individual qualification of local technicians, including hybrid assessments under Accredia's control. Swedac's UKAS agreement allows British assessment work while Swedac retains responsibility for accreditation. The institutional design could reduce travel and coordination burdens as it becomes operational. Korean cooperation, British cooperation

Software Has An Operating History

Steelforce's customer disclosures establish activity before the definitive regime. Its 2023 report says it already used CarbonChain for product-footprint tracking and successfully submitted its first transitional report, covering five EU entities. Its 2024 report identifies internal reporting staff, data flows to CarbonChain and successful submissions for October 2023-December 2024. This is operating use of accounting and reporting support across successive periods. 2023 report, 2024 report

CarbonChain's product history reinforces that sequence. The April 2024 Comply release described customs-data validation, XML generation and supplier engagement; October added improvements to supplier communication and audit trails. CBAM was becoming an additional workflow within an existing accounting product family. Spring release, October release

There is a useful commercial counterweight in the same history: the summer release offered eligible companies free access until October 2024. Adoption therefore cannot be translated directly into paid subscriptions. The stronger proposition is that software and associated support were being used to perform a repeated task. Summer release

Public infrastructure competes with part of that proposition. Ireland's EPA explains that operators can share installation data through the Registry with the declarants importing their goods. Private providers have to earn their role through integration, reconciliation, supplier access and service. EPA guidance

The exemption also changes the addressable workflow. The Commission expected approximately 182,000 importers, mainly SMEs and individuals, to leave scope. The commercial inference is a shift away from treating every previously affected small importer as an unchanged filing prospect, toward the more complicated relationships that remain. Commission simplification announcement

Who Controls The Constraint

The parties purchase different things and retain different responsibilities.

PartyPurchased or funded workControl point
Installation operatorMonitoring, data preparation and assuranceAccess to production and precursor records
Accredited verifierAccreditation, competent teams and independent reviewThe verification opinion within its scope
EU importer or declarantFiling support, integration and certificatesMethod selection and declaration responsibility
Software or advisory providerProduct development and customer supportReliable exchange and reconciliation of records
Material buyerPhysical goods meeting procurement requirementsCommercial acceptance and negotiated price

Accreditation charges are costs borne by the verification business; customer engagement fees are a separate transaction. Similarly, an importer's avoided liability is a pool of potential value, not automatically the provider's revenue. Contract terms determine how that value is shared.

Procurement Has Its Own Acceptance Test

CarbonChain attributes a seven-figure steel award for Spaeter to footprint scenarios that met a manufacturer's green-steel criteria. The case names Spaeter executive Max Benger and describes buyer demands for third-party-validated product footprints. It provides a named procurement use for the accounting service. Spaeter case

The physical product has its own milestones. SSAB's June 12 release reports a 39-metre pedestrian and bicycle bridge installed over the E22 in Lund, delivered with Peab and Hjalmarssons, using approximately 95% SSAB Zero steel. Material supply, fabrication and construction had reached a delivered application. SSAB delivery record

Both cases help explain demand for credible emissions information. Procurement footprints and statutory CBAM calculations nevertheless have different boundaries and acceptance requirements. The Commission's definitive-period FAQ rejects LCA and life-cycle inventory database emission factors as a substitute for the prescribed embedded-emissions calculation. FAQ, section 4.24

Three Ways The Constraint Could Move

If supplier records improve and assurance expands, the constraint can move toward integration: matching accepted installation information to the importer's goods and declaration. Transitional reporting offers evidence of learning. The Commission reports actual-value use rising from 8% to 53% overall between Q4 2023 and Q2 2025, and from 25% to 93% for imports above 1,000 tonnes. Commission review

If audit capacity expands but factory access remains weak, supplier coordination can remain the harder job. Anew's intervention illustrates that mechanism; mixed precursor treatment gives processors a way to proceed selectively.

If accredited teams are difficult to obtain in a particular geography or activity group, defaults preserve an alternative while the producer loses the opportunity to establish its individual advantage. Swedac estimates six to ten months from a complete accreditation application, potentially three to six for related extensions, and explicitly reports resource limits outside its priority countries. That supports local friction alongside an expanding supply response. Swedac guidance

Better recognition can also change trade allocation before it changes aggregate production. A producer could direct existing cleaner output toward Europe. The OECD identifies resource shuffling as an environmental-effectiveness risk, making portfolio-wide decarbonisation a separate question from the quality of a particular export flow's evidence. OECD analysis

Limits: transitional reports were unverified; the provider cases establish neither customer payments nor CBAM-caused material premiums. Public evidence does not supply a matched EU-wide demand/capacity series or representative assurance margins. SGS Belgium and DNV are company announcements rather than inspected grants here. ISSC's annex has inconsistent expiry wording, so its precise expiry is left unresolved.

What To Watch

By September 30, 2027, the decisive ISSC record is a dated RvA conversion decision or replacement annex establishing regular CBAM accreditation. By the same date, completed assessments under the Korean and British arrangements would show whether cooperation is becoming operational supply.

For customer value, the useful next record is a named operator and declarant connecting accepted 2026 actuals with their 2027 declaration and quantified liability. The Commission schedules the first verification reports from January 2027 and certificate purchasing from February; those milestones explain why 2026 qualifications and reference prices are preparation rather than completed annual settlement. Verification timetable, certificate calendar

Operators can preserve traceable records and arrange assurance within the relevant scope. Importers can compare complete liabilities and agree who funds the evidence. Service providers can turn those arrangements into repeatable delivery. The supported opportunity is making the evidence handoff reliable; enduring auditor scarcity is only one possible first-cycle outcome.

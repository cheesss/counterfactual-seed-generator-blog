The AI Power Trade Is Becoming a Turbine Slot Trade

AI power is starting to look less like a grid story and more like a delivery-slot story. Demand can be forecast years ahead, but a project still needs a turbine delivery window in the right year and a build schedule that can line up around it.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-06-21 · 9 min read

The bottleneck is moving from generic power demand to dated turbine access

The standard story about AI power is that data centers are growing faster than the grid. That is true, but it is still too broad to explain who holds leverage. A utility can announce generation, and a developer can secure land and fuel access, yet the project can still miss its window if a large-frame turbine is not available in the right year.

That is why "more power" is not the right unit of analysis. The narrower bottleneck is dated access to large-frame gas turbines and the project-delivery windows built around them. EPC labor, hot-section parts, controls, and long-term service still matter, but the cited reporting points first to turbine availability as the main gate.

The demand backdrop explains why that narrower market suddenly matters. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory estimated that U.S. data-center electricity use rose from 58 TWh in 2014 to about 176 TWh in 2023, with a possible range of 325 to 580 TWh by 2028. The IEA put global data-center demand on a path from roughly 415 TWh in 2024 to about 945 TWh by 2030 in its base case (LBNL U.S. data-center load report) (IEA Energy and AI).

Those forecasts do not settle the fuel mix. They do explain why schedule certainty and firm delivery now command a premium.

Reservation is turning turbine supply into a queue market

GE Vernova said its Gas Power equipment backlog and slot reservation agreements rose from 83 GW to 100 GW in the first quarter of 2026. In that quarter alone it signed 21 GW of new gas equipment contracts, including 19 GW of slot reservation agreements, converted 6 GW of prior reservations into orders, and shipped 4 GW of equipment. The 19 GW of reservations sat inside that 21 GW total, not on top of it (GE Vernova Q1 2026 results).

That is not the language of a commodity market. It is the language of a queue market in which position in line can matter almost as much as the machine itself.

Reuters and Wood Mackenzie describe the same pressure from the outside. Reuters said planned and under-development U.S. gas-fired capacity more than tripled in 2025 to roughly 252 GW, while combined-cycle project costs more than doubled over 12 to 24 months to at least $2,400 per kW (Reuters on gas-plant costs and lead times).

The same reporting cited Wood Mackenzie estimates that the U.S. turbine order pipeline rose from 7 GW in 2020 to 44 GW in 2025, with large-turbine lead times above five years. Wood Mackenzie separately said gas turbine prices could reach about $600 per kW by the end of 2027, up 195% from 2019 (Wood Mackenzie on turbine pricing).

The named projects show what "reserved" really means

The strongest evidence sits at the project layer. Once the constraint moves from abstract megawatts to specific delivery years, project announcements start to read like claims on scarce industrial timing rather than generic growth plans.

Alternative architectures matter when schedule certainty becomes part of the product

The evidence here does not prove that every engine-based or hybrid project exists because a turbine slot was unavailable. It does show the menu of firm-power architectures developers can use when delivery timing matters and large-frame lead times are long.

Wärtsilä announced multiple U.S. data-center power orders using gas engines, including a 412 MW Ohio project and a 790 MW Texas project. Cummins announced a multi-year agreement with Circe Energy to supply natural-gas generator sets for high-performance-computing data centers in West Texas, with deliveries from 2026 through 2030 (Wärtsilä Ohio 34SG order) (Wärtsilä Texas engine order) (Cummins-Circe West Texas generators).

Pacifico Energy's GW Ranch describes a private-grid campus built around gas generation, battery storage, and solar to serve AI infrastructure (Pacifico GW Ranch). Taken together, these examples are consistent with a market in which dated turbine access has become more valuable. They are not proof that turbine-slot scarcity forced each choice.

Markets often miss this distinction because megawatts look fungible from far away. A planner can put solar, batteries, gas, and load growth on one chart. A developer has to care about the delivery year, the machine class, the site interface, and the contractor schedule. The rent tends to collect at the narrowest dated step in that chain.

The counterargument is that this is a late-cycle squeeze, not a new regime

There is a real chance that this becomes an over-ordering story rather than a durable industrial choke point. AI load forecasts could prove too high. Some announced campuses may slip or never reach scale. OEMs are also expanding. GE Vernova is investing in Greenville, Siemens Energy has described a U.S. investment program for grid and gas-turbine equipment, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries says it is increasing capacity, and Wärtsilä said it is expanding technical production capacity in Vaasa by 35% (GE Vernova Greenville expansion) (Reuters on Siemens U.S. investment) (MHI GTCC Q&A) (Wärtsilä Ohio 34SG order).

That caution should narrow the claim, not erase it. The point is not that every adjacent layer is already proven tight, and not that every announced project will be built. The point is that once developers start treating a delivery year as scarce property, leverage moves upstream toward the firms that can commit turbine output on a dated schedule.

What to watch

  • Slot reservation agreements and how often they convert into firm orders.
  • Quoted delivery years for large-frame turbines and engine packages.
  • EPC awards that bundle OEM equipment with construction responsibility.
  • Service and parts backlog disclosures that would show the constraint spreading.
  • Project cancellations or delays that would show the reservation queue was overbuilt.

The AI power trade is usually framed as a contest for electrons. The more precise version is a contest for dated turbine access and the project window it anchors.

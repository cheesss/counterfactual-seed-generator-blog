The fuel test for methanol ships

Claim: Limited repeat purchases of qualifying fuel at a price carriers or their customers will pay will keep Rotterdam's calendar-2027 Bio Methanol bunker sales below 25,000 metric tonnes.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-11-19 · 11 min read

In brief

  • Stena Germanica began methanol sailings between Gothenburg and Kiel in late March 2015 after its retrofit.
  • CMA CGM Osmium took biomethanol at Shanghai in both March and August 2026.
  • Ørsted halted FlagshipONE in August 2024, citing higher costs and an inability to secure long-term offtake at sustainable prices.

Two deliveries to the same ship

In August 2026, at the Lyuhuashan anchorage off Shanghai, a bunker vessel transferred 8,016 tonnes of biomethanol to CMA CGM Osmium. The same 13,000-TEU ship had taken 3,643 tonnes at Shanghai in March. The carrier announced the March bunker before the ship joined its scheduled Asia–Mexico service; by August it listed the ship as operating on that route.

Most of the August fuel came from Shanghai Electric's Taonan plant, travelled to Dalian for consolidation and storage, and went by sea to Shanghai, where SIPG Energy handled the transfer. Jilin records first-phase production starting in July 2025; Shanghai Electric puts that phase's design at 50,000 tonnes a year. March delivery · August delivery · Taonan project update · Plant design

What made that possible was a March 2025 supply agreement that set out who would make the fuel, how it would reach the coast and who would put it aboard. For a liner that already has the engine, that is the constraint: securing enough fuel with documented lifecycle emissions, at a port the ship actually calls, at a price someone will cover again on the next rotation.

The recurring pattern is that once equipment can do the job, routine use depends on a qualifying input delivered reliably. A tonne of commodity methanol and a tonne with the fuel attributes a particular buyer needs are different purchases. Carrier supply agreement · ISCC EU certification

What shipowners already know how to buy

The Stena Germanica ferry began methanol sailings between Gothenburg and Kiel in 2015 after a Wärtsilä retrofit. In 2016 the first seven Waterfront Shipping dual-fuel tankers went into service with MAN two-stroke engines. The ferry returned to the same ports; Waterfront was already in the business of moving methanol.

By 2023 Maersk had a different customer problem: keep a container liner supplied. It contracted OCI biomethanol for Laura Mærsk's 21,500 km maiden voyage from Ulsan to Copenhagen, then arranged fuel from Equinor's existing facilities for Rotterdam bunkering on the Baltic service. The sourcing changed with the route. Wärtsilä ferry case · Methanex operating history · Maersk sourcing report

Ship work remains substantial. Zhoushan Xinya spent 88 days converting Maersk Halifax in 2024, including tanks and fuel preparation and supply systems. In June 2026 Everllence's PrimeServ completed the main-engine upgrade of Seaspan Yangtze at COSCO Shipping Heavy Industry's Shanghai yard. The first of five ships in the joint retrofit program returned to service for Hapag-Lloyd, which charters it from Seaspan.

Engine work and ship integration are distinct jobs; Hapag-Lloyd reports retrofit-related commitments in its November accounts. The vessel can still run on conventional fuel, giving the carrier a way to keep its schedule while leaving the fuel producer with demand to contract. Halifax conversion · PrimeServ completion · Hapag-Lloyd filing

Maersk reported 19 methanol dual-fuel ships at the end of 2025. It is also buying other options: its November 2026 filing records orders for 26 large liquefied-gas-capable ships for 2029–30 delivery. DNV's global counts show methanol orders falling during a broader newbuild slowdown, even as total containership orders rose. Methanol represented about 6% of 2025 container ordering by gross tonnage, versus 58% for LNG. The order mix shows that suppliers face competition for the next ship as well as for the next bunker. What keeps a plant running

Kassø in southern Denmark shows how many businesses have to line up. European Energy integrated the plant, Siemens Energy supplied electrolysers, and Tønder Biogas supplied biogenic carbon dioxide. The 2022 equipment announcement targeted commercial production in the second half of 2023. The developer announced its first industry-grade batch in April 2025 and delivered fuel to Laura Mærsk at the May inauguration.

The European Energy–Mitsui venture reported supplying contracted customers in the first quarter of 2026. It also secured bridge financing, which supports the plant's operations but is separate from fuel sales. The developer cites 42,000 tonnes a year of design capacity and, in its half-year update, describes work to raise capacity from the original 32,000-tonne plan to over 40,000. Equipment order · First grade-qualified batch · Ship delivery and bridge financing · 2026 capacity update

Shipping is one customer for that output. OMV says it received its first Kassø delivery on July 14, 2026 for use in a German road-biodiesel ingredient; the venture announced a multiyear deal in August, alongside existing offtake arrangements with Maersk, LEGO and Novo Nordisk. A plant with several outlets has more ways to sell its output. A liner can end up competing with industrial and road-fuel buyers for tonnes from the same facility. OMV receipt · Kassø buyer agreement

FlagshipONE shows the other side of the bargain. Ørsted took a final investment decision in 2022 and stopped work on the Swedish project in August 2024. It cited higher costs and an inability to secure long-term purchase contracts at sustainable prices, and recorded a DKK 1.5 billion impairment. The commercial problem was a price both sides could live with for years. Technical progress on its own could not close that gap. Ørsted interim financial report

Goldwind starts with named anchor buyers. It already sells wind turbines and services at scale; fuel production is a newer business. Its Hinggan League first phase was designed for 250,000 tonnes a year, and the engineering contractor dates the first dispatch to September 10, 2026.

Maersk targets 500,000 tonnes a year under its deal, while Hapag-Lloyd announced 250,000 a year under a separate deal and identified an adjacent planned factory. The engineering contractor puts the wider three-phase capacity plan at 1.45 million tonnes a year by the end of 2027. Hapag-Lloyd's earlier full-rate timetable depended on a final investment decision. Its November filing still presents the volumes as a purchase obligation. The milestone that matters next is a named carrier confirming receipt under its contract. Who controls the constraint

Producers sell fuel, storage operators make inventory available where it is needed, and bunker operators sell delivery in a ship's port window. X-Press Feeders put Rotterdam at the centre of its methanol feeder network because it could source fuel there. The port names OCI as the supplier, UniBarge and TankMatch for last-mile delivery, and Evergreen as a customer for the feeder service. Methanex later acquired OCI's international methanol business, including low-carbon production and marketing alongside large natural-gas-based assets. A useful trading capability can sit inside a much bigger commodity business. Rotterdam feeder network · Methanex closing announcement

The carrier compares delivered cost per unit of energy: at 19.9 MJ/kg versus 42.7 for marine diesel or gas oil, methanol takes about 2.15 times the mass to provide the same fuel energy, before engine efficiency and pilot fuel. For an emissions purchase the relevant comparison is the additional cost per lifecycle tonne avoided. The bunker delivery note records what went aboard; sustainability documentation establishes the fuel pathway and emissions attributes.

FuelEU Maritime creates a reason to pay for reductions by tightening the permitted annual greenhouse gas intensity of covered energy, 2% below its reference from 2025 and 6% from 2030. Pooling and other fuel choices set competing prices for compliance. The proposed global IMO measures were still under discussion as of November 19. FuelEU law · Commission guidance · IMO agenda

Cargo owners can help pay for the reduction even when their containers are booked on another voyage. Under ZEMBA's first tender, Hapag-Lloyd reports issuing biomethane-based transport certificates in 2025. The second tender commits buyers to the emissions attributes of e-methanol service from Hapag-Lloyd and e-ammonia service from North Sea Container Line beginning in 2027. The award projects approximately 120,000 tonnes CO₂e in combined abatement over the deployments. The first tender shows that carriers can deliver a product freight buyers can claim; the second tests whether those buyers can also underpin regular purchases of newer fuels. Carrier's 2025 record · ZEMBA award

What could change the result

One port can supply a whole service. A carrier need not find a supplier at every stop. Shanghai concentrated inland output on a scheduled service, and Rotterdam's feeder network already uses a hub. Rotterdam's sales reached 11,819 tonnes in 2025, up from 3,946 in 2024, then fell 32% year on year to 8,007 tonnes through September 2026.

Rotterdam also reported shifts in bunkering to other ports across its broader market. At the same time, it agreed to build a quay for Evos's planned methanol and ethanol storage expansion, with quay completion expected in early 2028. A large customer landing recurring stems in Rotterdam could lift the total above the call; the same service bunkering elsewhere would ease the carrier's constraint without moving the Rotterdam figure. Port bunker series · Port volume update · Evos expansion

A methanol retrofit can open a second fuel route. WinGD reports Tangier Mærsk sailed from Houston to New York on ethanol supplied by POET. And in October 2026 Singapore's port authority confirmed that Golden Island pumped about 5,000 tonnes of certified bioethanol into the retrofitted Maersk Halifax while it worked cargo at PSA. Both operations show why engine flexibility can matter after a ship enters service. If a substitute proves easier or cheaper to buy for the relevant emissions benefit, it can relieve a carrier's problem while keeping methanol volumes lower. WinGD voyage report · Singapore transfer

Public records don't give a comparable series for delivered prices, plant utilization or ship-level use of qualifying fuel. Rotterdam settles the call on volume; it cannot by itself tell us whether price, routing or a substitute drove the result.

What to watch

  • By February 11, 2027 (base November 19, 2026): Look for Maersk's confirmation of a dated 2026 receipt under its Goldwind contract. Maersk's November interim report gives that publication date for its full-year report. A documented deferral would miss the announced 2026 start; a silent report leaves it unresolved. Maersk financial reports
  • By December 31, 2027 (base December 2025): Look for the first documented eligible e-methanol and e-ammonia deployments under ZEMBA's second award. A delay into 2028 would miss the published start window. The evidence to look for is fuel used in service and the resulting verified freight attributes. Tender and verification requirements

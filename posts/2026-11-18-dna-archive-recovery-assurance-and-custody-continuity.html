DNA archives need an exit plan

The emerging product is a recoverable file: a durable sample accompanied by the information, rights and practical arrangements needed to read it after its original supplier has gone.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-11-18 · Updated 2026-11-19 · 12 min read

DNA archives need an exit plan

A sealed capsule is designed to outlast today's storage equipment. Turning it into an institutional archive requires several kinds of continuity: someone must identify the sample, reconstruct its contents and establish who may receive them. The surrounding software, laboratories and custodians become part of what the buyer purchases.

That is the ecosystem-expansion mechanism. A new medium creates demand for complementary capabilities, while their design determines who keeps the customer. DNA protection could become an operated service, a portable recovery package or a tier administered by an existing archive platform. The evidence currently shows offers and qualification work around these arrangements.

In brief

  • DNA Shield offers operated protection for selected records through early access.
  • Outside recovery has a documented experimental precedent in the Goldman-Wuyts challenge.
  • Incumbent integration offers another route through Scality's planned DNA archive tier.

Three deliverables, three responsibilities

Biomemory's October 15 launch announcement presents DNA Shield as a managed protection service: customers encrypt selected information, Biomemory encodes it into DNA, and recovery follows a controlled process. The intended objects include key-recovery material, industrial knowledge and important records. Broader storage expansion is dated from 2029 in the company's roadmap.

A buyer should distinguish three deliverables. The first is the sample: identified material containing the selected information. The second is recovered data: files checked against an agreed reference. The third is an exit package: technical instructions, permissions and arrangements that let recovery continue after a laboratory, custodian or supplier changes.

These deliverables test different things. Possession establishes custody. A matching readback demonstrates that a particular chain worked. An exit exercise establishes whether another team can operate the chain using the materials and rights delivered to the owner. That last test turns replaceability into something a procurement team can inspect.

Shield's pilot offer makes the second deliverable explicit. Biomemory operates the technical stages; the customer checks the returned file against the original and receives its container. The subsequent trial uses portal requests and write/read credits. This is a concrete proposed acceptance procedure. Its next observable transition is a customer reporting that the procedure worked on its data. Pilot workflow.

The business has a history

Shield follows earlier work within the same product family. Biomemory's December 2023 DNA Card offer named Eurofins Genomics as the retrieval-service provider. The release also projected a 100-PB Biomemory Prime system for 2026. The card, the projected system and today's selected-record service have different scopes; the historical target remains a target in that record. Original announcement.

There is customer-side evaluation as well. France's defence ministry described experiments with its digital organization and Future Combat Command in June 2026, covering strategic infrastructure and tactical settings. The task under examination is operational evaluation of DNA storage. The ministry frames the initiative around identifying uses and testing potential. Ministry record.

Biomemory's own partner descriptions distinguish CNES-funded radiation tests, a Bordeaux demonstrator and SICPA anti-counterfeiting exploration that helped redirect its focus toward storage. These are useful operating relationships with distinct purposes. Separating them helps identify what Shield must add: an accepted protection service with defined continuing responsibilities. Project descriptions.

The decoder has to survive too

A sequencer produces DNA reads. Recovering files also requires ordering, error correction and interpretation of archive structure. SNIA's Sector Zero identifies the writer and the codec needed to reach archive metadata; Sector One describes the logical archive and its contents. Together they provide a discovery path for a future reader. Sector One.

Section 6 of Sector Zero exposes the organizational dependency. Vendors are encouraged to supply codec details to the governing body, while disclosure remains optional. The specification warns that missing details can make the archive unreadable. A surviving identifier therefore needs a surviving destination: useful information that an authorized reader can obtain. Sector Zero.

SNIA's June 2025 codec white paper recommends detailed format specifications covering layout, error correction and media parameters so future implementations can recover the archive. Preserving that knowledge lets readers change as technology changes. Codec guidance, printed pages 22-23.

The procurement implication is straightforward: give a competent second team the recovery package and an agreed verification reference. Record the preparation steps, software environment and undocumented assumptions it encounters. The result measures whether the knowledge travels with the sample. It also reveals which continuing work deserves a service contract.

Outside recovery can work

In January 2018, University of Antwerp researcher Sander Wuyts recovered the key in Nick Goldman's DNA-storage Bitcoin challenge, launched in January 2015. This supplies a concrete experimental example of a reader outside the writing organization. The three-year interval describes the challenge's calendar, rather than retrieval time. EMBL-EBI account.

Wuyts's technical account is particularly useful because it preserves the friction. He initially followed an older description, discovered an additional transformation in the challenge version, extracted a Perl script embedded in a PDF and changed his assembly approach. The successful recovery depended on documentation, interpretation and help from a colleague as well as sequencing. Reader's account.

This precedent supports a business model that can work against permanent dependency. A supplier can sell an archive whose documentation lets other laboratories compete for subsequent reads. Better portability could enlarge adoption while reducing exclusive recovery income. The recurring pattern is complement formation; ownership of those complements remains contestable.

Atlas outlined a similar division of labor in March 2026: return a capsule to Atlas, process it with a desktop sequencer or use a sequencing center, with an open-source script for the external routes. Its presentation reported small end-to-end proofs of concept and expected terabyte datasets by year-end. Those are proposed recovery paths and a dated scale-up target. Product presentation.

A reader can travel with an archive

France's Archives nationales registered two DNA-encoded declarations of rights in November 2021. Its account identifies CNRS/Sorbonne researchers, Twist-synthesized fragments and Imagene metal capsules, and reports repeated complete file recoveries by the project team. This joins named physical custody to an exercised technical process. Custodian record.

MARKOS adds an explicit reconstruction layer. Its August 2026 repository lists sequencing data, decoding scripts and printed pages intended to let a future reader rebuild the transcription program for Greek and French versions of the Gospel of Mark. That makes the recovery knowledge an archival object in its own right. Deposited materials.

CNRS reports that Nice's Romain-Gary heritage library has held the print-and-DNA edition since September 2026. It describes the fragments being verified by sequencing before containment. The institutional contribution is the combination of an object, a location and its explanatory material. CNRS account.

The related work by Eupalia and Andra, France's radioactive-waste agency, offers a conventional alternative. Their 2020-21 tests transcribed a database onto permanent paper, producing the reported 464-page document. A 2024 paper extends the same preservation family to PDF preparation, drawing on late-2023 tests. Its approach converts content into simpler representations and prints digital data as two-dimensional barcodes. 2022 account, 2024 paper.

The economic lesson runs across the media. Specialists can create value by delivering enough knowledge to reconstruct access without retaining the original provider. A one-time portable package and a continuing managed service solve the same institutional problem through different allocations of work.

A supplier exit has already happened

Catalog's company notice says it began winding down, transferred intellectual property and assigned other assets for creditors. Biomemory announced its acquisition of Catalog assets on March 5, 2026. The pair of records shows corporate discontinuity alongside technological continuation. Catalog notice, acquisition announcement.

For an archive owner, continuity depends on which obligations follow the assets. An agreement must specify who answers recovery requests, provides usable documentation and pays for successor work. A transferred patent and an executable recovery obligation serve different purposes.

Biomemory now publicly proposes an exit response. In a November 13 interview, product executive Vincent Franceschini described future escrow of reading information, customer-held cards or outside secure custody, and a commercial guarantee being developed. He said Shield pilots should start in January 2027. Those statements identify commitments that buyers can ask to see in an agreement. Executive interview.

Escrow also needs an operational destination. The depositary must release usable material under defined conditions, and a successor needs rights and resources to exercise it. Encryption-key succession belongs beside decoder continuity: reconstructing an encrypted file and authorizing someone to decrypt it are separate steps.

Continuity is already an operating service

Portico illustrates the organizational design on conventional infrastructure. Its terms provide verification access and define release triggers including publisher cessation and sustained platform failure. Its preservation agreements include rights to transform content and name a nonprofit successor. Access terms, organizational provisions.

There are exercised outcomes. Portico's August 19, 2026 alert lists six journal titles made accessible. Its March 2025 announcement records a signed agreement to preserve Clarivate's Ebook Central books, including future additions. These are, respectively, an access outcome and a preservation agreement. Access alert, Clarivate agreement.

Libraries and publishers fund the operation; library contributions are tied to materials expenditure in Portico's published schedule. The purchased responsibility is maintained, legally exercisable access. This supplies an organizational precedent for DNA procurement teams considering custody, verification and succession. Contribution schedule.

The BBC Domesday project supplies an interpretation precedent. Published in 1986, its specialized reading environment survives through working equipment and browser emulation. Original equipment also supported the BBC's 2010-11 revisit. The preservation achievement includes a continuing way to understand the object. Equipment loan, museum emulator.

Who keeps the customer

Scality's June 2, 2026 partnership announcement positions DNA alongside flash, disk and tape within Scality-managed environments. It describes a jointly developed integration roadmap. The commercial implication is an incumbent route: a DNA supplier may gain distribution while the existing platform retains policy, workflow and the account relationship. Counterparty announcement.

SNIA's Swordfish working draft similarly maps DNA cards, containers and objects into a storage-management model and mockups. Its contribution is interface design that could make a new medium administratively familiar. Working draft.

These are analytical alternatives; one contract can combine them. Counting each role as a separate revenue pool would duplicate spending. A recurring fee earns its interpretation when it funds specified continuing work. It may instead recover the cost of the initial write over time.

Manufacturing and conventional substitutes matter

Atlas announced Eon 100 in December 2025, before Shield. In September 2026, AtlasBase chief executive Jeff Treuhaft said cartridges had shipped to early-access customers. Useful payload and accepted readback are the measurements needed to connect that shipment milestone to archive qualification. Eon announcement, CEO's answers.

The manufacturing pathway is concrete too: imec's March 2026 record describes chip development, fabrication, integration, prototyping and investment with Atlas. That work addresses production capability. Manufacturing partner.

Writing economics provides a strong competing explanation for the pace of adoption. An August 2026 preprint by Alex El-Shaikh, Bernhard Seeger and Thomas Heinis finds synthesis dominates its modeled bulk-archive costs. Its baseline treats DNA running costs as negligible relative to synthesis and sequencing. The result explains why manufacturing improvement and institutional recovery arrangements are separate tasks. Economic model.

The conventional option is improving. Fujifilm lists native capacities of 12 TB for LTO-8, 18 TB for LTO-9 and 30-TB and 40-TB LTO-10 variants. Its 40-TB launch describes an advance over the 30-TB version. Buyers can compare complete offline arrangements across media: compatible readers, custody, keys, verification and eventual migration. Specifications, 40-TB release.

Three outcomes would reshape value capture. Routine, inexpensive outside recovery would favor portable packages and competing laboratories. Successful platform integration could leave archive incumbents controlling the customer. Repeated buyer preference for conventional offline copies would limit DNA's protection niche. Each scenario tests a different link between technical progress and continuing revenue.

Limits: As of November 19, 2026, the checked records establish offers, evaluations, experimental recoveries and executive-reported shipments, rather than a disclosed cohort of paid Shield renewals or an executed, funded successor arrangement. Deposited software was not run, and the historical recovery examples provide no commercial success-rate denominator.

What to watch

  • By October 16, 2027; base October 16, 2026: the original, stronger forecast requires public documentation by an organization other than Biomemory of a named Shield customer's successful outside-operated recovery; the program record remains a monitoring source.
  • By December 31, 2026; base March 10, 2026: Atlas's vendor target is tested by a public record identifying useful terabyte input and the completed operation's scope against its presentation.

The next advance becomes tangible when a buyer can name the files, the reader and the obligation that survives a supplier's departure. Durable molecules create the opportunity. Exercisable recovery makes an archive.

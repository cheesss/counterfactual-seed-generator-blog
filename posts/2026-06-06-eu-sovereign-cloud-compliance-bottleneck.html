EU Sovereign Cloud Compliance Bottleneck

Europe's sovereign-cloud business is advancing when a buyer can move an application into an acceptable operating environment, rather than merely select a provider with the right label. Qualification opens a purchasing route. The next commercial steps are supplying the required functions, integrating the application, securing customer acceptance and earning repeat operating income.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-06-06 · 12 min read

Europe's Sovereign Clouds Still Have To Deliver The Application

That is a commercialization story with several beneficiaries. European operators can gain infrastructure and support business while American technology remains inside the service. Application suppliers and integrators can retain important customer relationships. Italy's PSN demonstrates that substantial delivery revenue can coexist with thin operating margins and a net loss.

The recurring pattern is the passage from eligibility to repeatable delivery. Its economics depend on turning demanding first installations into services that subsequent customers can use.

In brief

  • European procurement accommodates both native technology and locally controlled operation of foreign software.
  • Qualified infrastructure becomes commercially useful through application functions, integration and customer acceptance.
  • PSN demonstrates operating activity; French programmes reveal the transitions still being completed.

Buying Control Without Replacing Every Technology

The Commission's April 17 award establishes four parallel purchasing routes. POST Telecom leads an offer with OVHcloud and Clever Cloud; the other selected providers are STACKIT, Scaleway and a Proximus-led partnership using S3NS, Clarence and Mistral. The Google-derived environment operated by EU companies reached the competition's minimum SEAL-2 level. The award opens access to services under a combined ceiling rather than recording customer consumption. Award record.

The mechanism is a different allocation of authority over administration, access and support. A customer can purchase European-controlled operation while retaining familiar upstream technology. That can establish a local operating business without transferring ownership of the underlying software.

The Commission's implementation guidance makes dependencies explicit. SEAL-2 allows material dependencies; SEAL-3 describes meaningful but incomplete European influence. That more qualified description matters when interpreting the award announcement's stronger language about resilience. The assessment follows technical layers and suppliers, and differs from France's SecNumCloud qualification. Implementation guidance.

Demand also needs the right denominator. Union entities spent EUR 87 million on public cloud in 2025, according to a March parliamentary answer covering all such providers. Framework purchasing authority and annual expenditure measure different things. Commission answer.

Policy is still developing. The Commission's roadmap, updated October 14, lists the Cloud and AI Development Act as a June 2026 proposal awaiting final adoption, with agreement targeted for Q4 2027. Its proposed framework should be distinguished from purchasing arrangements already in operation. Legislative roadmap.

A Platform Certificate Has Edges

S3NS's PREMI3NS shows what continuing local operation entails. Its August explanation describes installing Google updates in a quarantine region, examining binaries and configurations, and conducting behavioural checks before production. Exceptional Google support requires an invitation, manual authorization and a supervised read-only session controlled by S3NS. These are continuing operating responsibilities requiring expertise and accountability. S3NS operating explanation.

Within that arrangement, the catalogue still distinguishes individual functions. At the October 27 review, Cloud Run and Secret Manager carried qualification-pending labels. Cloud Run supplies a managed environment for containerized applications. An application built around that function presents a different delivery task from one whose requirements fit the existing qualified catalogue. Service catalogue.

Product-family history matters too. S3NS's December 2025 qualification announcement says Thales already uses PREMI3NS for internal IT and engineering, and describes roughly thirty pioneering users. That is attributed operating evidence for PREMI3NS itself. Earlier Local Controls or CRYPT3NS customer relationships should be kept separate from evidence about this qualified platform. Thales's position as controlling parent makes it an anchor user rather than an independent test of arm's-length demand. Qualification announcement.

The application layer is expanding. An April 27 SAP-S3NS announcement targeted RISE private cloud edition for H2 2026, with Thales as the first strategic customer. SAP would deploy the business software above S3NS's infrastructure, supporting an ERP transformation spanning finance, supply chain, production and purchasing. This creates a separate application-delivery relationship above cloud operation. SAP partnership.

G7 supplies a different milestone. Its October 8 announcement describes AI applications under test before deployment on PREMI3NS, including staff assistance and driver-facing tools. Named external testing is progress from a general product announcement toward a specific application programme. G7 announcement.

Bleu Has More Than One Qualification Clock

Bleu, owned by Orange and Capgemini, offers a Microsoft-based route. Its April 17, 2025 announcement targeted qualification of its IaaS, PaaS and CaaS services in H1 2026. That original window should remain visible when assessing delivery. Original target.

A narrower operator update changes the interpretation of later expectations. Bleu's July account targets Azure qualification for end-2026 and Microsoft 365 for H1 2027. Thinkproject's June statement had used the broader end-2026 formulation. The two product lanes therefore need separate follow-up dates. Bleu update, Thinkproject announcement.

Bleu reported general availability of roughly one hundred services in October, following ISO and health-data-hosting certifications. Its cloud offering remained on ANSSI's public in-progress list at the cutoff. Availability, those certifications and final SecNumCloud qualification identify different commercial states. Bleu news, ANSSI pending projects.

Dassault Aviation's December 16, 2025 selection concerned collaborative services based on Azure and Microsoft 365. That identifies the customer and purchased application category, keeping the milestone distinct from aircraft-design workload migration. Customer release.

Other suppliers are at different stages. OVHcloud's September announcement identifies SNC Cloud Platform as its third qualified offering, following Bare Metal Pod and VMware on OVHcloud. Its consumption-based public-cloud branch expands an established product family. OVHcloud announcement.

AWS, meanwhile, launched its European Sovereign Cloud in Brandenburg on January 14. It describes physically and logically separate infrastructure and dedicated European operating arrangements. The incumbent is adapting its own service structure to compete for these customers. AWS launch.

The Health Data Hub Is Buying A Destination It Must Help Build

France's Plateforme des donnees de sante, or PDS, selected Scaleway in April through UGAP's Nuage public framework. Its explanation says participating offers covered parts of its requirements and that remaining security options would be implemented with the selected provider. The purchase combines a destination platform with further engineering. PDS selection.

The qualification dependency is explicit in Scaleway's own release: existing HDS certification and a credible SecNumCloud trajectory supported its selection, with the latter described as indispensable for hosting sensitive PDS data. Its IaaS/PaaS qualification project remained publicly in progress. Scaleway release, ANSSI projects.

DINUM describes technical and methodological support alongside Inria and health specialists. Decision responsibility remained with PDS and the competent authorities; tests and technical validation were still ahead in its June account. The customer's May 7 assembly record expects the first main-SNDS hosting stage in late 2026 or early 2027. DINUM account, assembly decisions.

If the implementation creates supported functions that later customers can reuse, this demanding first project can improve subsequent delivery economics. A bespoke solution creates a different business: one requiring another substantial engineering effort for each sensitive workload.

Scaleway is also expanding above infrastructure. Its acquisition of Saagie adds DataOps orchestration, linking ingestion, processing, machine learning and visualization. SFEIR's March 2026 partnership brings migration, cloud-native development, governance and training. These are concrete complements to cloud infrastructure, with distinct application and integration responsibilities. Saagie acquisition, SFEIR partnership.

Italy Makes The Economics Visible

PSN's operating history extends beyond recent announcements. Its 2023 accounts record EUR 78.3 million in revenue; the customer-revenue note is dominated by migration, rearchitecting, replatforming and associated infrastructure operations. Revenue reached EUR 255.5 million in 2024. Public administrations buy the work of moving and running services as well as infrastructure. 2023 accounts, 2024 accounts.

In 2025, the combined implementation-and-operations category contributed EUR 360.68 million. Operating cash flow was EUR 51.05 million against EUR 79.51 million of investment outflow. The business has scale and operating cash generation, while its reported loss makes sustained profitability the next economic test. 2025 report.

Purchaser records expose collection work. A March 16 reconstruction-commissioner decree authorizes settlement of EUR 338,953.73 before VAT for earlier invoices. The associated agreement conditions PSN's waiver of late-payment interest on timely payment. Delivery, invoicing and collection are separate parts of the operating cycle. Purchaser decree.

PSN reported more than 280 central and health-sector administrations migrated for its PNRR target in July. That adds a reported workload milestone to the accounts. Repeat operating demand after the initial publicly supported transition will determine how this delivery business matures. Operating milestone.

Delivery Could Become Routine

The strongest counterargument is that qualification, integration and procurement become standardized enough for migration to become ordinary.

Telekom reports a completed Moodle migration in Baden-Wuerttemberg, serving more than 1,600 schools, and access to T Cloud Public through Bechtle's multi-cloud procurement channel. It is an example of an operating application and an established purchasing route reinforcing each other. Telekom account.

Delos and adesso illustrate complement formation at an earlier stage. Their September partnership assigns advice, architecture and implementation to the service partner; managed services and resale are contemplated later. It connects the Microsoft-based German cloud offer to delivery teams familiar with public-sector customers. adesso announcement.

Law also leaves room for competing arrangements. On March 20, the Conseil d'Etat upheld a specific three-year health-data processing authorization involving Microsoft Ireland hosting, distinguishing study data from platform-user data and considering contractual safeguards. That case demonstrates why procurement preferences and GDPR legality need separate analysis. Court explanation.

History supplies failure paths. Microsoft stopped admitting new customers and deploying new services to its isolated German trustee cloud in 2018, citing the flexibility and consistency customers wanted. Existing services continued with security updates. France's Senate recounts weak commercialization and eventual retreat at Cloudwatt and Numergy. Today's broader application catalogues and purchasing channels address problems those earlier projects encountered. Microsoft history, Senate account.

Who Controls The Transition

Several businesses sit behind the customer bill. The operator controls infrastructure administration and support. Software owners retain technology and application relationships. Integrators carry conversion and implementation work. Procurement brokers connect buyers to admissible offers. Security operations, facilities, power and connectivity support continuing service.

The important economic question is which relationships persist after migration. Reusable security configurations, supported managed functions and known application architectures can turn engineering effort into a repeatable product. A local invoice can coexist with continuing foreign software income.

Three outcomes follow from this mechanism. Standardized deployments could lower integration effort and accelerate repeat purchases. Bespoke installations could raise delivery revenue while leaving operating margins thin. Expanding catalogues without accepted workloads would point toward customer readiness or demand, rather than qualification, as the stronger explanation for slow commercialization.

Limits: the evidence establishes named transitions, not an EU-wide capacity shortage, comparable qualification lead times or standalone margins for the new French offers. Operator customer counts are attributed disclosures; payment orders establish authorization rather than bank settlement, and combined service categories leave recurring income partly obscured.

What To Watch

By December 31, 2026, check Bleu's Azure qualification against its July target; by June 30, 2027, check its separate Microsoft 365 target, both base July 2026. The settling records are final ANSSI decisions for the relevant service perimeter, rather than general availability announcements. Operator targets.

By March 31, 2027, review PDS's public confirmation of its first accepted main-SNDS hosting stage, base May 7, 2026. This is an editorial checkpoint for the stated late-2026/early-2027 window. Customer silence would leave delivery unverified. By December 31, 2026, also check for SAP-S3NS deployment confirmation against the April 27, 2026 H2 target. PDS record, SAP roadmap.

Broader market forecasts require their own test. Gartner's February 9, 2026 vintage projects European sovereign-IaaS spending of USD 23.118 billion in 2027. A same-definition update by June 30, 2028 would permit comparison; this spending category is different from Synergy's broad infrastructure-provider share. Gartner forecast.

Covered switching charges end on January 12, 2027 under the Data Act, changing one part of migration economics. Application conversion, security implementation and acceptance still require delivery work. Commission guidance.

For buyers, the useful unit of comparison is an application delivered under specified controls with accountable support. For suppliers, it is the customer relationship and repeat operating work retained after conversion. Those working services are the commercial substance behind Europe's sovereign-cloud label.

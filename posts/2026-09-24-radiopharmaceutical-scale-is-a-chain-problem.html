Cancer's Next Supply Chain Is a Radioisotope Clock

Cancer medicines that decay expose a familiar industrial constraint: announced capacity becomes useful only when a qualified chain can convert it into dependable output. For radioligand therapy, that output is a usable dose arriving at a staffed treatment appointment. Increasing isotope production helps; expanding accepted processing, manufacturing and delivery routes can matter just as much.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-06-22 · 13 min read

A Medicine Must Meet an Appointment

In February 2023, Novartis explained a supply failure in unusually concrete terms. Pluvicto was being manufactured in Italy in small batches, with five days for a dose to reach its intended patient. Production interruptions, weather and import difficulties could force doses to be remade. Those remakes displaced later appointments, while the factory lacked usable backup stock. FDA-hosted patient letter

The failure propagated through a queue: a missed shipment consumed another manufacturing slot as well as another appointment. Extra irradiation upstream would help only if the remaining stages could turn it into usable medicine.

Recovery supplies the essential comparison. On October 26, 2023, Novartis announced that FDA had classified the shortage as resolved following production expansion. Its financial reporting subsequently confirmed January 2024 FDA approval for commercial Pluvicto manufacturing in Indianapolis. The radioactive clock stayed the same while the supply configuration changed. Resolution announcement; Q1 2024 financial report

This is evidence that qualified expansion can relieve a constraint. It is also a reason to measure the remaining scheduling margin across the whole pathway: source, processing, manufacture, release, transport and treatment.

Pluvicto Has an Operating Market

Two clocks govern the product. Lu-177's physical half-life is 6.647 days; Pluvicto's labeled shelf life is 120 hours from calibration. The label specifies activity at administration and prohibits use after expiration. Clinics and factories therefore coordinate around a product specification and an appointment, as well as physical decay. Prescribing information

Novartis identifies Millburn, Indianapolis and Carlsbad as operating US RLT sites. Denton and Winter Park are planned additions, with stated opening targets of 2028 and 2029 respectively. The company's Carlsbad account dates its opening to 2025. Counting operating sites separately from construction projects gives a meaningful baseline for further expansion. Current roster; Carlsbad history

Realized business is visible in Pluvicto's $1.293 billion of first-half 2026 net sales. FDA then expanded its indication on July 31 to PSMA-positive metastatic androgen pathway modulation-naive or -sensitive prostate cancer, previously called metastatic hormone-sensitive disease, in combination with androgen receptor pathway inhibitor therapy. Selection requires an approved PSMA PET product. The expansion enlarges the potential market and increases the importance of imaging, referral and treatment capacity. Financial report; FDA approval

Commercial Lu-177 therapy is thus an established operating business. Ac-225 input sales for clinical development represent a different stage, with finished-drug demand still dependent on therapeutic and regulatory outcomes.

Processing Can Be the Binding Step

Brookhaven shows why reactor or accelerator capacity alone is an incomplete measure.

DOE's isotope program had established accelerator production of Ac-225, but available hot-cell processing space limited expansion. An $8.5 million refurbishment restored dormant facilities. After completion in March 2023, Brookhaven processed its first irradiated thorium target and shipped Ac-225 to customers, adding a second DOE processing site. The useful investment was shielded separation capability, connecting an existing irradiation route to customer material. NIDC operating account

Lu-177 offers a successful specialized network. Bruce Power's Unit 7 in Ontario began commercial isotope production in October 2022 using a system developed with Isogen and a partnership involving the Saugeen Ojibway Nation and ITM. In October 2024, the partners reported two years without a missed isotope shipment to ITM, which processes and distributes the material. Joint operating record

Irradiation and pharmaceutical processing can work across organizational and national boundaries. Their interfaces require dependable schedules, agreed material characteristics and repeatable processing. Ownership of every stage is one way to coordinate those tasks; contracts and specialized partners are another.

Ac-225 Customers Buy More Than an Isotope

NorthStar reported weekly millicurie-level Ac-225 production at Beloit, Wisconsin, in January 2026. Its expanded relationship with Convergent is a more concrete demand signal than an anonymous capacity forecast: the companies announced dedicated manufacturing suites for the investigational CONV01-alpha program, extending a 2024 arrangement covering isotope supply, drug manufacture and development work. Production statement; Customer agreement

Convergent purchases an isotope-and-manufacturing service; NorthStar connects its material to clinical drug batches. Dedicated suites can shorten transfers and align production schedules.

Documentation is another purchased capability. Nucleus RadioPharma's June 2026 agreement with BWXT covers Ac-225 chloride and diagnostic I-123. BWXT undertakes to maintain product Drug Master Files and provide letters of authorization supporting customer submissions. Supply framework

NorthStar separately reported FDA acceptance of an Ac-225 DMF in April. FDA explains that DMFs provide confidential manufacturing information for review with referencing applications; they are optional and receive neither approval nor disapproval themselves. Qualification therefore remains connected to the customer's application and process. NorthStar milestone; FDA policy

TerraPower draws a further boundary: its present Ac-225 is research-grade starting material made outside cGMP, intended for further manufacturing. Its planned Bellwether facility represents a separate cGMP expansion. A compliant finished medicine depends on the downstream process and controls appropriate to that starting material. Supplier description

First Supply and Industrial Expansion Have Different Histories

PanTera's two production branches make this distinction tangible.

In June 2025, the Belgian company reported its first Ac-225 delivery from a Th-229 generator using material supplied by TerraPower. That early-supply route already had a shipped-product milestone. Delivery announcement

The Actinium Production Center in Mol uses a separate Ra-226 photonuclear route. Belgium's nuclear regulator confirms a construction and operation license granted on June 17, 2025. PanTera's October groundbreaking release distinguishes building operation in 2028 from expected first commercial APC supply in 2029. Its older boilerplate retains a 2028 production reference; the differentiated milestones provide the clearer schedule. FANC record; Project schedule

Existing generator deliveries and future photonuclear expansion should be tracked separately. TerraPower's precursor contribution to early supply also means these relationships cannot automatically be counted as wholly independent upstream sources.

Nusano's Utah platform sits at another transition. Its June 25, 2026 demonstration reached a target through the accelerator's front end at 28% of full energy. The October 14 Primo agreement identifies a Taiwanese developer seeking several isotopes, including Ac-225 and Lu-177. Together these establish hardware progress and a customer relationship. Accepted customer lots would establish the next operating milestone. Technical demonstration; Primo agreement

A Supplier's Business and Its Drug Can Diverge

ITM provides a particularly useful comparison. Telix's September filing reports $273 million of ITM company revenue in 2025 and describes its isotope manufacturing business as profitable. Telix agreed to acquire ITM for $1.65 billion upfront on a cash-free, debt-free basis, subject to closing conditions. The transaction seeks control of an existing platform alongside a therapeutic pipeline. Transaction filing

Yet ITM-11, its Lu-177-edotreotide drug candidate, received an August 7 Complete Response Letter. The September transaction presentation attributes the unresolved items to chemistry, manufacturing and controls and a third-party commercial facility. It also makes resubmission or an agreed path forward a closing condition. Regulatory and transaction update

An isotope supplier can generate revenue while its own drug awaits manufacturing-related remediation. Supply availability and approval readiness are distinct gates.

BMS approaches that boundary from clinical progress. On October 12, it reported that the planned interim analysis of Phase 3 ACTION-1 met its progression-free-survival endpoint for RYZ101 in patients with GEP-NETs progressing after Lu-177 therapy. RYZ101 remained investigational. Sponsor results

RayzeBio's Indianapolis facility already combines isotope and drug-product manufacture for clinical doses. BMS describes direct delivery to treatment facilities within three days of release. This internalizes important manufacturing handoffs while preserving the need for external transport and prepared clinical sites. RayzeBio manufacturing; BMS hub account

The Clinic Remains Part of the System

The delivery architecture varies by product. US medical-use rules permit several appropriately licensed supply and preparation routes, including licensed manufacturers, authorized professionals and specified investigational arrangements. External nuclear pharmacies are one option within that framework. 10 CFR 35.300

Cardinal Health's August 2025 account describes a functioning pharmacy-network model: on-site quality review precedes distribution, then central or regional pharmacies prepare and deliver patient-specific doses. Its manufacturing services connect developers to providers through an established distribution network. Operating workflow

Release rules also accommodate radioactive time. For specified short-lived radiopharmaceutical batches, US regulation allows release before sterility or pyrogen testing is completed, with testing completed as soon as possible. Identity, strength and the applicable quality requirements still govern release. 21 CFR 211.165

Patient release is a different event. NRC's August draft guidance concerns discharge after radioactive treatment; AAPM warned that its proposed calculations and assumptions could increase holding or inpatient burdens. A separate July licensing proposal seeks to reduce administrative barriers. These proposals show how downstream rules could change access. NRC draft; AAPM response; Licensing proposal

Payment follows its own timetable. CMS retained Pluvicto's A9607 reporting unit at one millicurie, rejecting a request for per-dose coding. The determination concerns administered activity. Coverage authorization can affect access before treatment; claim submission belongs outside the product-expiry sequence. CMS determination

Who Controls the Constraint

The evidence points toward spending on dependable conversion and delivery. Responsibilities differ at each transition.

Customer and controllerPurchased objectEvidence of economic control to watch
Developer pays isotope supplierAccepted material, activity and quality documentationRepeat accepted deliveries, renewals and second-source terms
Sponsor pays manufacturing partnerRadiolabeling, analytics and released drug batchesRelease yield, paid campaigns and utilization
Drug manufacturer uses irradiation partnersIrradiated targets on a reliable scheduleSustained fulfillment and contractual allocation of outages
Manufacturer or provider uses logistics and pharmacy servicesUsable medicine at the clinical handoffService levels and responsibility for replacement doses
Payer and patient fund treatment providerMedicine and administration servicesReimbursement relative to acquisition, staffing and facility costs

These are procurement relationships and analytical tests of durable control. Final-drug sales, upstream isotope purchases and manufacturing fees describe overlapping flows through the same patient pathway.

There is already some profitability evidence: Telix's presentation reports ITM gross margin above 40% using unaudited H1 2026 figures. Establishing lasting scarcity rents requires evidence about prices, alternatives and returns through the capacity cycle. Financial presentation

Integration can simplify coordination, as at RayzeBio. Specialization can also succeed, as Bruce–ITM demonstrates. The practical question for a sponsor is which configuration delivers accepted batches economically and reliably.

Three Ways the Next Constraint Could Move

Qualified relief. Repeated deliveries from additional sources and regional manufacturers create scheduling alternatives. The next limitation could become referrals, imaging access or staffed treatment slots. Pluvicto's recovery provides an operating precedent for this scenario.

Output ahead of qualification. New irradiation capacity arrives faster than accepted pharmaceutical processes. Bottlenecks remain in separation, release or particular customer applications. Brookhaven's processing history and ITM-11's regulatory setback illustrate different versions of this mechanism.

Capacity ahead of demand. Facilities expand while therapeutic approvals or adoption arrive slowly. Suppliers compete for fewer manufacturing campaigns than anticipated. NorthStar's earlier Mo-99 business provides a cautionary operating history: MURR records that production stopped in October 2023 after commercial activity had been established. Institutional account

The strongest current challenge to permanent Ac-225 scarcity comes from DOE. Its September 29 notice asked customers to identify 2027 needs commercial suppliers could not satisfy, to inform federal production and possible future scale-back. The request deadline was October 15. This is a market reassessment, with the production decision still ahead. DOE notice

Customer experience is also heterogeneous. Actinium Pharmaceuticals' Q2 2026 filing says existing DOE supply meets its current Ac-225 needs while describing a planned internal finished-drug manufacturing facility. Buying upstream material and building downstream capability can coexist within one strategy. Customer filing

Physics still sets different geographical boundaries. DOE explicitly links At-211's 7.214-hour half-life to regional production through university partners. A supply architecture that works for multi-day Lu-177 has a different scheduling budget for At-211. DOE regional model

Limits: Company operating statements are generally unaudited; the public record lacks comparable released-activity, batch-failure, late-dose, contract-price and clinic-margin data. The ITM-11 account is the sponsor's description rather than the FDA letter, and no completed September Nusano full-system milestone was located. The findings establish product-specific constraints and relief paths, rather than a measured market-wide supply balance.

HVDC Grid Superhighways: Factory Slots Are Becoming Part of the Critical Path

A grid project can reserve its cable years before it has permission to build the substation at the other end.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-09-24 · 13 min read

The Grid Buildout Has A Commitment Deadline

Scotland's Western Isles connection illustrates the problem. SSEN reserved supplier capacity in August 2023 and signed final cable contracts in January 2026. Yet, as of 10 October 2026, the Fanellan converter-station and substation refusal appeal remained on hold. Its November procedural target was not a promise of permission. Equipment and consent were advancing on different calendars. SSEN's reservations, Fanellan appeal record.

The emerging constraint is a latest feasible commitment date: owners must secure qualified equipment and installation resources before the time left in their construction programme becomes shorter than delivery requires. Waiting can improve project certainty while making the desired completion date harder to achieve.

This is a manufacturing-tooling problem, but not a claim that factories dominate every transmission project's critical path. A reservation protects one part of a sequence. It does not make the whole project executable.

What Makes A Lead Time Binding

A two-gigawatt high-voltage direct-current link is not a generic order for wire. Cable, insulation, joints and terminations must work as a qualified system. Converter valves, transformers and controls must meet civil works, installation campaigns and an AC network ready to receive the power.

The IEA's February 2025 assessment reported procurement periods of roughly two to three years for cables, up to four for large power transformers and beyond five for specialised DC cables. These are dated findings, not October 2026 quotations; the transformer category is not a separate converter-transformer survey. The same assessment identifies permitting as the primary transmission-delay source, particularly in advanced economies. IEA executive summary.

A newer signal comes from Ofgem's 2 October 2026 determination, which records transmission owners reporting six-to-eight-year HVDC equipment lead times. That broader, buyer-reported category cannot establish a like-for-like increase from the IEA's DC-cable range. Ofgem approved about GBP835 million of additional bespoke procurement allowances, in 2023/24 prices, covering several equipment classes and 22 projects, not HVDC alone or cash already paid. Ofgem's final determination.

The relevant comparison is delivery time against the time available before equipment is needed. Five years can be manageable if booked early. A shorter queue can bind a late-committing project. Neither figure alone measures a global shortage.

TenneT Made Competition For The Calendar Visible

TenneT's North Sea programme uses a 2 GW, 525 kV design. Its February 2021 procurement notice explicitly contrasted this with German 900 MW HVDC and Dutch 700 MW AC connections; TenneT engineers described the same distinction in April 2022. Larger repeat designs require fewer nominal systems per gigawatt; actual construction-time or cost savings still require delivered-project evidence. TenneT's procurement notice, TenneT engineers' technical account.

The 2023 procurement was correspondingly large. Hitachi Energy and Petrofac's framework covered six systems at approximately EUR13 billion. GE-led arrangements allocated five across two consortia, with the two German awards still expected when GE announced them in March. Siemens Energy and Dragados signed for three connections, nearly EUR7 billion. These were consortium delivery packages, not converter-OEM revenue or completed links. Hitachi's historical framework, GE's award announcement, Siemens' framework.

Repeated projects let suppliers organise engineering, production and fabrication over several years. That allocation can affect another buyer. Ofgem found that concentrated TenneT procurement contributed to the difficult tender conditions facing Eastern Green Link 1.

The regulator nevertheless allowed only 57 days of penalty exemption against 480 requested. It considered EGL1's original end-2027 schedule highly improbable and ultimately judged the joint venture's procurement conduct appropriate. The exemption is not a numerical decomposition of physical delay. The case establishes cross-buyer competition, not an explanation of every lost month. EGL1 determination.

A Reservation Is Not Completion Insurance

TenneT also exposes the limits of long frameworks. Petrofac disclosed immediate termination of its scope in October 2025. Hitachi's July 2026 announcement identified L&T as partner on four awarded projects. The execution partner changed; this did not add a new converter-technology supplier. Petrofac's disclosure, Hitachi and L&T's awards.

The change reached the fabrication chain. Malaysian builder MMHE disclosed a February 2026 novation transferring its subcontracts to L&T. Work continued, but the scope review excluded the second offshore substation's topside. That is a documented contractual workaround with changed scope, not proof that the original framework moved intact or that the complete second connection was cancelled. MHB's exchange announcement.

Factory access, contractor continuity and whole-system performance are different protections. Securing one does not automatically secure the others.

Similar Orders, Different Project Gates

Western Isles and Spittal-Peterhead progressed from reservations to approximately EUR2 billion of combined NKT cable contracts in January 2026. NKT's scope includes design, manufacture and installation. Western Isles is a 1.8 GW near-family case, not an exact 2 GW observation; Spittal-Peterhead is 2 GW. Both use 525 kV systems and retain 2030 commissioning targets. Orders and backlog are not accepted assets or collected cash. Final Scottish cable contracts.

Their consent positions differ. Fanellan remains a material Western Isles dependency. For Spittal-Peterhead, the owner reported marine approval and Banniskirk planning consent in November 2025, following Netherton consent. That progress does not establish that every condition is discharged or commissioning assured. Equipment similarity is no reason to assign both projects the same delivery confidence. Spittal-Peterhead's owner record.

Eastern Green Link 2 supplies another commercial progression. Prysmian's February 2024 firm cable order, approximately EUR1.9 billion, followed a June 2023 capacity commitment. The purchaser is the National Grid/SSEN project company; the cable package covers design through testing and commissioning for a 2 GW, 525 kV extruded system. Its stated operating target was 2029. EGL2 contract.

Dates require care. A September 2026 parliamentary answer gives EGL2 a licence delivery date of 31 December 2030. That is a distinct regulatory date, not evidence that the owner formally revised its operating forecast. Meeting the licence date would not establish delivery within 2029. Parliamentary answer.

EGL3 shows where package responsibility stops. Its March 2026 NKT cable contract exceeds EUR2.2 billion, with commissioning expected at end-2033. The National Grid/SSEN joint venture retains civil-works management. Calling the cable package turnkey does not make NKT responsible for every interface in an energised network. EGL3 scope and purchaser.

Earlier Funding, Not Unconditional Approval

National Grid's March 2025 frameworks announced GBP21.3 billion for cables and GBP24.6 billion for converters, within a wider GBP59 billion programme including civil works. These are framework opportunities, not fully placed orders or expenditure. Its subsequent annual results used an up-to-GBP26 billion converter figure; the differing disclosures should not be collapsed into one exact current ceiling. Framework announcement, Annual results.

Ofgem's Advanced Procurement Mechanism advances eligible supplier funding. Its current governance, effective 2 October 2026, treats HVDC cables, converters and converter transformers as bespoke procurement requiring specific approval. It does not waive planning permission or make the 2023 reservations APM-funded. Earlier procurement was already possible; this is an extension of overlapping procurement and consent, not a universal reversal of a permit-first industry. Current APM governance.

Shetland 2 demonstrates why commercial stages matter. The buyer assigns cable engineering and supply to Sumitomo, offshore transport and installation to Van Oord. SSEN's scope division. Sumitomo's July 2026 announcement anticipated a manufacturing call-off during 2026, subject to approvals, and project-cable production at Nigg in Q2 2027. The August contract notice describes initial works, not manufacturing authority. One award cannot be used to settle a different milestone. Sumitomo's timetable, Initial-works notice.

Relief Has Already Begun

A permanent factory-shortage story would overlook operating additions. Nexans reported a full year of expanded Halden operation in 2025. GE reported a 40% increase in Stafford valve-module production capacity in October 2025. These are company-reported improvements, not equivalent increases in completed transmission links, but they refute the claim that all relief awaits 2027. Nexans' operating results, Stafford's production improvements.

Further additions remain targets. NKT expects its new Karlskrona factory to enter commercial operation in Q2 2027, serving its transmission backlog. Siemens' EUR220 million Nuremberg expansion targets about 50% more transformer-factory capacity by 2028, not a separately measured 50% HVDC increment. New production can fulfil existing commitments without creating freely available slots. NKT's September update, Siemens' expansion plan.

Supplier choice is widening: Sumitomo's December 2025 Sea Link award is an actual National Grid 525 kV cable purchase, stronger evidence than framework membership. Yet a customer order, qualified technology and spare production capacity remain different things. Sea Link contract.

Qualification itself is not a uniform new delay. Prysmian documented a one-year prequalification test for its complete 525 kV submarine cable/accessory system in 2022. That does not mean every new line or substitution must repeat an identical year of testing. ENTSO-E's 2026 consultation, now closed, proposes voluntary common assessment while retaining independent TSO acceptance. It cannot create production capacity or allocate commercial slots. Prysmian's system test, ENTSO-E consultation.

Marine additions are staged too. Jan De Nul's September 2026 update places Fleeming Jenkin after first sea trials but still in final construction, not commercial cable laying. Contractor's status update.

Who Controls The Constraint

The control points extend beyond the manufacturer.

  • Owners set project requirements, commit procurement and coordinate interfaces.
  • Regulators decide eligible advance funding and allowed-cost recovery, not planning consent.
  • Planning authorities determine whether the proposed physical works can proceed.
  • Manufacturers and their partners supply accepted equipment and contracted installation scope.
  • Marine contractors must match vessels, protection equipment and crews to cable availability.
  • Owners and system operators must bring controls, AC connections and commissioning into alignment.

Simulation adds another advance commitment. The National HVDC Centre recommends starting hosting discussions about 18 months before replica delivery and signing agreements about 12 months ahead. Accepted simulation replicas are not accepted physical links; model hosting does not itself confer unrestricted intellectual-property or maintenance rights. Replica-hosting delivery plan.

Where The Money Moves

The economic change includes earlier owner commitments and broader delivery scope, not simply more cable factories. Prysmian completed its acquisition of ACSM in February 2026, bringing survey, seabed preparation and installation capabilities in-house. This transfers ownership of an existing business; it is not all newly created grid demand or proof of improved project returns. Acquisition rationale, Completed transaction.

Actual operating sales exist alongside backlog. Prysmian reported first-half 2026 Transmission revenue of EUR1.636 billion and adjusted EBITDA of EUR325 million. Its 20.7% margin uses standard-metal-price revenue, not the EUR1.636 billion denominator. The filing identifies manufacturing and installation activity on EGL1, Ijmuiden and other projects, but segment results do not isolate new-link profits or scarcity rents. Execution, capacity and project mix also matter. Issuer filing.

Lifecycle spending is already commercial. Hitachi describes its 2025 Caithness-Moray-Shetland support commencement as an extension of an earlier relationship, including maintenance and cybersecurity. This is existing installed-base business, not evidence of service revenue from unbuilt 525 kV links. Service commencement account.

Risk does not vanish when protection is purchased. Ofgem's Viking Link review excluded selected standby-vessel, additional warranty and delay-insurance costs from regulatory recovery. That is not an insurer refusing a valid claim; it shows that protection and consumer-funded recoverability are separate questions. Viking Link cost determination.

Frameworks, final orders, embedded materials, subcontracting, financing and acquisitions cannot be added as independent markets. Nor do early payments alone establish incremental fees or durable supplier margins.

Operating Precedent Has Configuration Limits

HVDC is an operating industry, not a laboratory proposition. Statnett took over the 1.4 GW NordLink interconnector in March 2021. SSEN completed final energisation of the 600 MW Shetland connection in August 2024. These are realised customer milestones for predecessor configurations. NordLink takeover, Shetland energisation.

New York's 1.25 GW CHPE entered commercial operation on 13 May 2026; NKT identifies its cable as 400 kV. It broadens the operating-family evidence without validating the exact European 2 GW/525 kV extruded cohort. Repeat procurement is not the same as repeated whole-link operating acceptance. NYSERDA's operating record, NKT's project disclosure.

A separate experimental branch also remains separate: SSEN's Aquila register records a rescope that put the physical Peterhead demonstration on hold while replica interoperability work continued. That is neither a failed electrical test nor proof of interchangeable production converters. SSEN's coordination register.

Three Ways The Constraint Can Change

Industrial relief: accepted output and installation capacity expand enough that buyers receive qualified offers within their required windows. Factory openings alone are insufficient; this outcome needs delivery evidence.

Constraint migration: equipment arrives as booked, but consent, civil works or AC integration delays normal service. The manufacturing exposure has then been managed for that project, even if the grid target is missed.

Demand retreat: postponed projects loosen booking calendars without a successful production ramp. Nexans said it was working with the Great Sea Interconnector customer on rescheduling. Hornsea 4's August 2026 update describes reconfiguration after its earlier pause, with seabed, connection and consent rights retained, not permanent abandonment. Neither record quantifies cable slots released. Nexans' scheduling statement, Hornsea 4's newer update.

These scenarios have different economic consequences. Extra accepted output can enlarge supply; a cancelled or deferred order can improve access while reducing suppliers' expected utilisation.

What To Watch

These are conditional milestone tests assessed on 10 October 2026, not calibrated probabilities. Component acceptance, ceremonies and trials do not settle whole-link service.

The strongest test of easing is repeated buyer evidence that qualified equipment and installation offers fit project-required dates without exceptional new years-ahead reservations. One delayed project cannot prove a market-wide shortage.

The relevant unit of expansion is not approved GW, or even GW with a cable order. It is capacity whose permission, qualified supply, installation and commissioning can still meet in an executable schedule.

Post-quantum migration needs an owner, not just an algorithm

Post-quantum protection is already reaching users through browser and cloud updates. In a mixed enterprise, the constraint can sit elsewhere: connecting a cryptographic finding to someone authorized to deliver a supported change. That handoff, rather than the inventory file itself, is the stronger candidate for a recurring migration business.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-06-07 · Updated 2026-10-24 · 12 min read

Post-quantum migration needs an owner, not just an inventory

In brief

  • Platform updates already distribute bounded post-quantum protection through existing customer relationships.
  • Mixed estates need findings connected to owners, dependencies and supported changes.
  • Free discovery and open tooling challenge standalone inventory pricing.
  • Trials, qualified modules, delivered orders and renewals establish different commercial milestones.

Two migrations with different control structures

A browser vendor controls a release channel. An enterprise service can depend on an application team, a rented platform, an external library and an inspection appliance managed by another department. The power to change cryptography is distributed differently.

Google's Q4 2024 operating update records default ML-KEM in Chrome 131 on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS and Android. Cloudflare's 2025 review reports its observed share rising from 29% at year-start to 52% in early December. Compatible browser and server software can deliver protection before the user commissions an enterprise-wide discovery project.

AWS illustrates the customer handoff within that managed route. Its April 7, 2025 launch covered hybrid ML-KEM key agreement at non-FIPS KMS, Certificate Manager and Secrets Manager endpoints in the commercial aws partition. AWS operates the service side; compatible client software must offer the corresponding key agreement.

The general implementation supply has also advanced. OpenSSL 3.5, released April 8, 2025, added ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA support. Application teams can now select maintained implementations and test their integration.

Centralized platforms can absorb discovery, testing and deployment inside an existing relationship. Fragmented estates must coordinate those tasks across people and contracts. Paid migration work has a clearer role where that coordination remains outside any one supplier's control.

A finding needs authority behind it

GAO's October 2026 report distinguishes a recent publication from an older measurement period. Across 24 CFO Act agencies, one fully addressed priority-system inventory activity, 22 partially addressed it and one did not address it. The underlying performance audit ran from February 2024 through September 2025; subsequent coordination concerned public release. GAO's inventory findings show missing systems, incomplete characteristics and weak supporting evidence.

An actionable record needs more than an algorithm name. It needs the service affected, its dependencies, the responsible operator, an available replacement and a way to test the change. Otherwise a technically correct finding can remain untouched.

The NCSC's March 2025 guidance begins with understanding systems and dependencies. Its initial exercise is expressly different from constructing a detailed register of every individual item. Commodity services may migrate through supplier updates; directly managed IT and operational technology require more customer involvement. Discovery serves a migration decision.

The June 24, 2026 OMB memorandum reinforces that governance model for covered federal systems. It distributes responsibility beyond CIOs and CISOs, requires clearly defined roles, and calls for automation where appropriate to maintain an updated cryptographic picture. Plans were due within 120 days. It also directs agencies to incorporate upgrades into cloud migrations, development lifecycles and hardware refreshes.

The recurring system pattern is a handoff from observation to authorized action. Better collection helps, but ownership, supplier support and acceptance determine whether a finding becomes an operating change.

Discovery cannot supply a missing verifier

NIST's migration project separates cryptographic discovery from interoperability testing. Its preliminary discovery architecture spans development pipelines, endpoint software and network observations, then considers how to normalize their reports. Each surface reveals a different part of an estate.

Compatibility can become the next constraint. Google's May 2024 rollout account describes existing TLS middlebox bugs exposed by enabling hybrid draft Kyber. Its September transition account explains that final ML-KEM was incompatible with the deployed experimental version. Chrome changed the offered mechanism and TLS codepoint, requiring server coordination.

GSA's June 2025 draft experiment report offers an identity-system example. Unifyia's platform issued credentials and performed lifecycle operations with ZTPass on NXP P71D600. Its tested Dilithium document-signing paths worked within the platform, while authentication could not be tested because supporting operating-system or middleware capability was absent. YubiKey 5.7 lacked Dilithium support altogether.

The experiment also tested classical RSA and Ed25519 paths, which must be kept separate from its post-quantum results. Its useful finding is the split between successful issuance and an incomplete application path. The next purchase or engineering task could be middleware, a supported device or application integration.

Signatures have their own acceptance path

ML-KEM establishes shared secrets. Certificate authentication and signed software require signature implementations and compatible trust paths. This creates more than one migration schedule inside the same organization.

Akamai's August 19, 2026 account reports general availability across its Enhanced TLS delivery chain. Client-to-Akamai and internal segments default to hybrid post-quantum key agreement; the origin segment requires customer opt-in and compatible support. Post-quantum authentication is a separate roadmap item.

Signature representation has become more concrete. RFC 9881, published in October 2025, specifies ML-DSA conventions for X.509 certificates and revocation lists. RFC 9964, published in May 2026, specifies JOSE and COSE serializations.

Raw public keys range from 1,312 to 2,592 bytes across ML-DSA's three parameter sets; signatures range from 2,420 to 4,627 bytes. Applications must accommodate the relevant encoded messages, buffers and framing. These requirements give integrators a concrete acceptance task beyond selecting a mathematical primitive.

Qualified hardware can preserve an incumbent

A hardware security module protects keys and performs cryptographic operations inside a defined boundary. Its supplier can therefore remain central to migration even as algorithms change.

NIST certificate 5450 records Luna T7's initial FIPS 140-3 Level 3 validation on July 29, 2026. The approved algorithm list includes ML-DSA and ML-KEM. Its security policy identifies firmware 7.15.1 and the tested T7/T7U module configurations.

Thales TCT's product-family history and upgrade statement traces pre-standard PQC support to 2023 and standardized-algorithm availability to September 2025. It offers existing customers an in-field firmware upgrade to the validated release.

That supports a plausible incumbent-retention mechanism: preserve key-management operations and supplier support while upgrading the cryptographic implementation. A hardware supplier may retain the relationship through firmware and integration work. The application owner still has to accept the enclosing service.

Customer names describe different milestones

HSBC's September 2024 announcement concerns a Quantinuum-supported trial around tokenised physical gold. The bank already offered institutional gold tokens and had launched a Hong Kong retail product on Orion. The PQC work tested interoperability across ledgers, including conversion into ERC-20 tokens. An existing operating product supplied the setting for the trial.

Vodafone and IBM's March 2025 announcement similarly placed new cryptography inside Secure Net, an established consumer security service. Vodafone supplied the service context, IBM the technology, and Akamai supported the feasibility demonstration in Barcelona. Vodafone's own account also records earlier cryptographic inventory testing.

Upstream, PQShield's June 2022 announcement distinguishes a Microchip licensing agreement from a Collins Aerospace proof-of-concept integration. Contracting for implementation IP gives an OEM a building block; product qualification and deployment remain separate work.

Commercial progress becomes clearer when the object and responsible actor are explicit. A customer logo cannot substitute for that description.

Free discovery changes where customers may pay

CycloneDX 1.6 introduced CBOM support in April 2024. ECMA-424's second edition, dated December 2025, specifies CycloneDX 1.7 as a machine-readable exchange format. Structured evidence can move between tools and organizations.

CBOMkit provides open tooling for generation, visualization and storage. Its Java scanning warning is instructive: without build outputs, unresolved symbols can reduce completeness and accuracy. A standardized output can still contain incomplete observations.

QuSecure's QuProtect R3 announcement, updated August 28, 2026, offers its Reconnaissance module free to qualifying organizations. Discovery can serve as an acquisition channel for a broader platform.

The economic inference is that customers may pay more readily for reconciliation, owner mapping, supported remediation and maintained evidence than for an exported list. Free collection increases pressure on the standalone file while leaving integration work to be done.

Who controls the constraint

DigiCert's Quantum Central release history documents July preview and September 24, 2026 general availability. Supported imports include certificate inventories and cloud key metadata. Policies identify violations; Jira tasks and synchronized status tracking connect findings to remediation.

Sectigo's September 17 announcement extends an existing certificate-management business into discovery and quantum-readiness assessment while inviting early access. The new branches inherit customer relationships and operational context.

There is an independent source of certificate-automation demand. Public TLS validity ceilings fall to 100 days in March 2027 and 47 days in March 2029 under the current requirements. Mozilla incorporates those requirements into its root policy. Shorter renewal cycles can drive automation spending before post-quantum authentication reaches a service.

Orders and accounting test the urgency story

SSH reports an approximately EUR160,000 NQX subscription-and-services order delivered to a European defence organization. The purchased object was network encryption and support. Its FY2024 release separately records recognition of an earlier cryptographic solution-and-services transaction during Q4.

These sit within an established security portfolio. SSH's FY2025 release reports EUR21.6 million consolidated sales against EUR22.2 million in 2024. Its September 2026 warning attributes reduced revenue contribution to delayed larger customer orders and revises expected full-year EBITDA to negative while retaining growth and positive operating-cash-flow guidance.

Government development funding is another channel. The SBIR registry records QuSecure's USD74,529 Phase I USAF research contract. Its March 2026 announcement describes a USD3.9 million TACFI award and a Q3 2026 classified-cloud deployment target tied to pursuing IL6 authorization. Its current company page still describes sponsorship toward that authorization. Funding supports technical maturation and access to prospective users.

Limits: these records establish different stages and scopes, not comparative migration labour or standalone-inventory receipts, margins and renewals. SSH's order page conflicts between June and July 2025 and its warning leaves delayed products unidentified. Vodafone's current rollout and QuSecure's achieved IL6 authorization remain unverified; traffic telemetry measures observed connections, and raw signature sizes exclude surrounding encodings. This article makes no forecast for quantum compromise.

What would prove this wrong

The public Web offers a powerful counterexample to a universal inventory business. Google announced staged SHA-1 trust changes in September 2014. Mozilla's February 2017 account described final enforcement reaching all Firefox users the next day, with residual exposure well below 0.1% of traffic. Concentrated distribution and trust-policy control made replacement enforceable.

Three outcomes would distinguish today's business models. Routine supplier refreshes could absorb most migration, reducing independent inventory demand. Adequate discovery followed by persistent device and verifier incompatibility could shift the constraint toward replacement engineering. Repeated customer use of maintained evidence to complete changes, followed by renewals, would support a recurring coordination business.

The deciding evidence is who completes the handoff and gets paid again. The number of findings on a dashboard cannot answer that question.

What to watch

  • By December 31, 2026; base October 24, 2026: a low-confidence forecast that the FAR Council publishes the contractor PQC/FIPS proposal directed by section 6(c). Settle on an actual proposal through the official publication index, rather than an agenda entry.
  • By March 31, 2027; base October 24, 2026: the minimum-CBOM guidance specified in the call.
  • On March 15, 2027; base October 24, 2026: a medium-confidence forecast that the operative TLS requirements retain the scheduled 100-day ceiling; postponement or superseding scope change falsifies it.
  • Through December 31, 2027; base October 24, 2026: observe named customer acceptance and renewals in the DigiCert and Sectigo: introduces sectigo quantum ready qspm migration channels. This is an evidence window, not a prediction of customer wins.

The valuable record is a finding attached to a service, an accountable owner, a supported implementation and an accepted change that stays current after the next release.

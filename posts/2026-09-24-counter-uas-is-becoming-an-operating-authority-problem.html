Counter-UAS Deployment: Turning Equipment into Lawful, Staffed Protection

Equipment acquisition does not itself establish lawful, staffed counter-UAS protection. America's expanded local authority changes who can organize that protection, while regional support and reusable operating arrangements offer a way to spread it across more sites.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-09-24 · 12 min read

Counter-Drone Protection Is an Operating Capability, Not a Purchase

The central constraint is the overlap of accepted equipment, available qualified people and applicable operating arrangements during the hours a site needs coverage. Which dependency causes the most lost coverage remains an empirical question. The recurring system pattern is the conversion of an installed asset into an accountable operating service.

In brief

  • Regional agency support can spread capability while retaining public operating responsibility.
  • Standing arrangements can reduce repeated setup across protective missions.
  • Financing, technical acceptance and staffing compete to explain missing coverage.
  • Established suppliers have sales; sustained use is the next commercial test.

Los Angeles Shows Why Financing Belongs in Readiness

Los Angeles's April 3 financing report proposed advancing purchase funds while FEMA reimbursement processing was interrupted. Equipment delivery was promised within 70 days after receipt of the city's front-funding payment. The council adopted the item April 21, with final action and mayoral concurrence April 24. Financing report; council docket.

The purchase route linked FEMA funding, Cal OES, the city and Defense Logistics Agency procurement. Bridging their payment arrangements was part of obtaining usable equipment before a fixed event date. Municipal working capital could therefore matter even after a grant award.

That sequence explains why readiness should be measured as an interval. Equipment delivered after an event contributes no coverage to that event. An officer's qualification and a system's availability must coincide with the applicable plan and coordination. Milestones scattered across a calendar can look complete while leaving the requested shift uncovered.

The useful output is a staffed, system-compatible protection window at a named site. Counting interceptions alone would undervalue quiet but capable coverage. Counting orders alone would reward preparation regardless of whether it produced coverage.

Reform Makes Some Setup Reusable

The SAFER SKIES Act was enacted December 18, 2025. Its implementing rule took effect July 1, 2026 and was published July 6. It creates a conditional independent pathway for state, local, Tribal and territorial law-enforcement and correctional agencies. Federal operations and federally deputized local personnel retain separate frameworks. Published rule; scope.

Planning can recur without starting over. Ordinary operational windows last up to 30 consecutive days; qualifying persistent or recurring missions can use renewable windows of up to 365 days. Agency approval remains distinct from federal deconfliction review, and event-level coordination continues within a standing window. Operations plans.

Spectrum arrangements also have a reusable element. FCC Order DA 26-656 grants conditional nationwide temporary authority for eligible systems on the Authorized Systems List. It removes the need to obtain a fresh individual spectrum instrument for every operation covered by that order. The authorization remains limited by its term and stated operating restrictions. FCC instrument.

The rule separately permits categorical FAA determinations. It also treats specified non-transmitting, aviation-safe detection and warning differently from mitigation. These provisions make an economic difference: legal compliance can become repeatable operating infrastructure, reducing bespoke setup effort. Coordination rules.

Who Controls the Transition

The responsibilities are divided:

ActorControlled transition
Purchaser and treasuryFunding, payment and equipment acceptance
Supplier and integratorDelivery, configuration, maintenance and technical support
Accredited operating agencyAccountable staffing and operational responsibility
Agency approving officialThe operation-specific plan and authorized interval
FAA, FCC and federal partnersApplicable airspace, spectrum and mission coordination
Certified mitigation operatorThe intervention decision and execution

Ordinary supporting contractors can maintain equipment and assist training, but cannot take mitigation command by relabeling the contract a managed service. Regional support supplies a different model: an accredited agency can protect a non-accredited host under a written arrangement while retaining compliance responsibility. Personnel and mutual-aid rule.

This creates two potential efficiencies. A host can obtain coverage without duplicating a rarely used program. A supplier can serve additional sites through shared agency capability. Scheduling, transport and simultaneous demand still determine how far a regional team can stretch.

The intervention itself requires a credible-threat assessment and a proportionate response. The individual executing a flight-affecting command needs current category-specific mitigation certification and a valid Part 107 remote-pilot certificate. The operating standard also requires reasonable efforts to identify authorized aircraft before intervention. Mitigation standards.

Butler Shows the Cost of Missing the Window

At Butler on July 13, 2024, the existing counter-UAS system became operable at 4:33 p.m., according to Ranking Member Rand Paul's initial Senate findings, nearly 40 minutes after the assailant's alleged drone flight. The findings also identify denied requests for additional counter-UAS assets. Initial findings.

The pertinent failure was timely availability during a real protective event. Federal authority and on-site equipment had to become functioning capability at the right time. Redundancy, field support and operator preparation belong in the same operational assessment.

Identification is another requirement. GAO reports that Secret Service personnel initially failed to recognize authorized CBP drones at the January 2021 inauguration, prompting a clarification of lead responsibility. GAO review.

Senator Maria Cantwell's March 2026 oversight letter describes more dangerous federal coordination failures: airspace disruption near El Paso and Santa Teresa, followed by the military downing a CBP drone near Fort Hancock. Her account gives deconfliction a concrete safety purpose. March letter.

Technical Fit Can Be the Pacing Constraint

FAA's selected airport evaluation covered 30 technologies and 14,772 flights. Twelve participating vendors completed the program and six washed out, primarily for RF and performance reasons. Seven other vendors withdrew before contracting. The presentation explicitly separates evaluation from vendor approval. Section 383 results.

This is a concrete reason to resist a staffing-only explanation. A fully qualified team still needs a configuration suited to its signals, airspace and mission. Testing and receiving acceptance address different questions from purchasing authority.

There is technical relief as well. The Army's September 27 account of Falcon Peak 26.2 describes enduring test lanes at Yuma and a centralized performance-data repository. Reusable evaluation could reduce suppliers' repeated setup work and give buyers more comparable evidence. Its civilian value will depend on whether receiving agencies accept and use that evidence. Yuma account.

Real Operations Already Exist

FAA reported more than 700 unauthorized-drone seizures during the 2026 World Cup, attributing them to FBI and DHS operations with federal and local partners. In Kansas City, DOJ reported eight drones and controllers seized by the FBI during June 16 joint operations with FAMS and city police. National account; Kansas City record.

Patel's September testimony attributes roughly 80% of World Cup counter-drone operational hours to certified local and other SLTT partners. That is meaningful evidence of personnel supplying actual shifts. Written testimony.

NYPD's September 17 briefing announced counter-drone teams for the forthcoming UN General Assembly and described completed security for earlier events, including the U.S. Open. The record establishes operational preparation alongside a broader history of event protection. A completion account identifying the authority used would make independent local repeatability observable. City briefing.

Physical confiscation, flight-affecting intervention and staffed readiness are distinct outputs. Keeping them separate makes the existing activity more informative and gives future after-action accounts a clear evidentiary job.

The Industry Predates the New Local Pathway

Axon's Q2 disclosure establishes Dedrone sales and reports support for all 11 U.S. World Cup stadiums plus more than 50 additional sites. Its commercial base reaches beyond the new independent local pathway. Issuer disclosure.

Fortem likewise has an existing family. In January it announced first customer deliveries of DroneHunter 5.0. Its March contract announcement names Lockheed Martin as the counterparty, with Fortem supplying TrueView radar and DroneHunter interceptors for integration with Lockheed's Sanctum software. That identifies an equipment and integration relationship rather than merely an investment. Deliveries; Contract announcement.

September's $50 million Series B close includes Lockheed's earlier $25 million commitment. October's MBDA announcement expands collaboration dating to 2024 and adds a route to offer DroneHunter through established air-defense networks, alongside a jointly developed FireThorn offering. Financing; MBDA announcement.

FireThorn's name already appeared in Fortem's February 2025 disclosure for an explosive interceptor. October marks the announced partnership expansion; dating the entire hard-kill family to that month would misstate its history. Earlier disclosure.

Where Commercial Value Can Accumulate

The platform relationship can survive a sensor replacement. Published Dedrone terms require a software license for each connected Dedrone or third-party hardware unit, where the customer's quote activates the appendix. That is a mechanism for recurring software revenue around a mixed equipment fleet. Contract terms.

Data rights form another control point. Axon's notice distinguishes Dedrone Data from Customer Content and permits specified reuse and aggregate or de-identified compilation. The same notice reports no CCPA-defined sale or sharing of covered Personal Data in the preceding year. Privacy notice.

Distribution is consolidating: Motorola announced completion of its $1.5 billion D-Fend acquisition on August 20. Established public-safety channels can connect specialized airspace products with existing agency buyers. Acquisition announcement.

The economic test is contribution after hardware, installation, labor, hosting, warranty and support, alongside capital committed to delivery. Stable profitable continuation can be attractive. Revenue growth alone says little about who retains the efficiency gains.

Contractual risk matters too. San Bernardino staff identify liability caps, insurance-standard departures and the absence of convenience termination. Their analysis shows why a buyer must examine residual exposure alongside the equipment specification. County analysis.

What Would Prove the Constraint Story Wrong

Three outcomes would distinguish the competing mechanisms.

First, dedicated or regional teams repeatedly deliver requested coverage using standing arrangements. Operating requirements then become routine infrastructure, while financing or technical acceptance may set the pace.

Second, accepted systems remain available but requested shifts go uncovered because qualified personnel cannot be supplied. A matched request-and-hours record would establish a staffing constraint for that segment.

Third, reusable testing and interchangeable components lower deployment effort while procurement remains competitive. Buyers could gain coverage without suppliers gaining proportionate pricing power. CBO's military analysis already finds a substantial supply base and competition; its illustrative layered installation costs $74 million to acquire and $5 million annually to support, excluding operating personnel. CBO analysis.

Limits: available records lack a representative matched independent-local request cohort, paid-renewal history and product-specific margins. Federal cases, aggregate seizures and supplier-reported deliveries establish different things; readiness also differs from effectiveness. Missing public records leave forecast outcomes unresolved unless affirmative evidence or sufficiently complete records settle them.

What to Watch

  • By March 31, 2027: an effective successor extends class-wide eligible SLTT spectrum authority beyond the original term. Base September 29, 2026. FCC baseline.
  • By June 30, 2027: FAA delivers its substantive integration update, evidenced by transmittal or GAO acknowledgment. Base September 29, 2026. GAO commitment.
  • By September 30, 2027: NYPD completes an additional independently authorized mitigation-ready window with eligible equipment, staffing and coordination. Base September 29, 2026. Operating baseline.
  • By December 31, 2027: completed regional agency-led coverage meets the call's conditions. Base September 29, 2026; reaffirmed October 16, 2026. Regional pathway.
  • By December 31, 2028: Fort Worth makes a nonzero payment for an exercised post-initial software/support renewal. Base September 29, 2026. Renewal authorization.

Useful coverage delivered when requested, with an attributable explanation for coverage lost, is the measure that connects procurement to protection. It also reveals whether shared capability creates additional service, saves duplicate purchases, or simply moves the same finite team between sites.

Counter-Drone Protection: Turning Purchases into Staffed Coverage

Published September 24, 2026. Updated September 29, 2026. Los Angeles’s April 3, 2026 financing report described trained counter-drone personnel waiting for equipment. The city proposed front-funding a $9,770,209 purchase while grant reimbursements were paused. Delivery was expected within 70 days after receipt of the city’s payment.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-09-24 · 14 min read

In brief

  • SAFER SKIES widened local mitigation authority while preserving agency responsibility.
  • 180-day FCC coverage can avoid new individualized spectrum instruments for qualifying operations.
  • Qualified staffing becomes a bottleneck when unavailable teams prevent coverage at otherwise-ready sites.
  • Los Angeles documented trained personnel awaiting financing and equipment.
  • Dedrone’s $100 million-plus quarterly revenue does not establish standalone margins.

Los Angeles shows why the sequence matters

Los Angeles’s April 3, 2026 financing report described trained counter-drone personnel waiting for equipment. The city proposed front-funding a $9,770,209 purchase while grant reimbursements were paused. Delivery was expected within 70 days after receipt of the city’s payment. The unresolved dependencies described in that document were financing and delivery, rather than an untrained operating team. (Los Angeles financing report)

The council acted on April 21; the mayor concurred on April 24. Those dates establish an authorization sequence. They do not establish when the city paid, accepted equipment or began coverage. The promised delivery interval cannot be presented as an observed 70-day cycle. The episode also predates July’s independent local operating framework, so it cannot supply that framework’s deployment statistics. (Los Angeles council actions)

Completing each prerequisite at some earlier date is insufficient. Qualifications, spectrum coverage and operating windows have conditions and limits; a qualified officer may be assigned elsewhere. (personnel qualifications) (operating windows)

This is a necessary-dependency argument. The evidence available through September 29, 2026 does not establish that authority or staffing is nationally scarcer than equipment, or generally takes longer to arrange. A requirement becomes a bottleneck only where its absence demonstrably prevents otherwise-ready coverage.

Reform makes setup reusable without transferring responsibility

SAFER SKIES, enacted December 18, 2025, expanded conditional authority for State, local, Tribal and territorial agencies—SLTT. The implementing rule became effective July 1, 2026, and was published July 6. Equipment ownership alone still gives an ordinary infrastructure owner no general power to interfere with an aircraft’s flight. (SAFER SKIES enactment) (implementing rule)

The crucial organizational restriction concerns who operates the system. Ordinary SLTT supporting contractors may maintain equipment and provide permitted training or technical assistance, but cannot command mitigation, make the operative threat determination or retain de facto control. The operating agency remains responsible. Certain contracted correctional operators and specified personnel under the separate Defense Department statute have different treatment; this is not a universal prohibition on private participation. (agency and contractor responsibilities) (separate defense authority)

Nor is the process one serial federal permission queue. The agency approves its plan and obtains legal review. Ordinary advance coordination begins at least 30 days before operation; the plan has a separate ordinary seven-day submission clock. Both contain shorter-notice provisions. Justice and Homeland Security coordination is not a general approval application, while applicable aviation, spectrum and interagency obligations remain distinct. (plan submission) (advance coordination)

FCC’s July 2 order already supplied conditional nationwide spectrum authority for 180 days, covering eligible operators using qualifying Authorized Systems List equipment. It removes the assumption that every covered operation needs a fresh individualized FCC instrument. Transitional technology eligibility under the DOJ/DHS rule does not automatically receive this coverage. (FCC nationwide authorization) The August 14 CTIA petition notice is neither a stay nor an extension; its disposition remains unresolved in the available record. (FCC petition notice)

Regional support, possible FAA categorical determinations and qualifying renewable windows of up to 365 days offer further reuse. Standing windows retain event-level coordination and material-change duties; an unused regional agreement supplies no crew. These routes could make operating requirements routine without eliminating them. (regional support) (standing-window conditions)

Readiness also does not predetermine intervention. The actual flight-affecting operator needs current mitigation qualifications and a valid Part 107 remote-pilot certificate, plus a qualifying threat assessment and proportionate response. Unauthorized presence alone is insufficient. When an imminent threat to life makes advance coordination impracticable, qualifying action requires subsequent notification as soon as practicable and in any event within two hours of the action. This exception does not create missing credentials or eligible equipment. (intervention conditions) (emergency coordination exception)

Technical readiness can set the pace

FAA’s August 28, 2024 evaluation account describes 30 technologies from 18 participating vendors, tested through 14,772 flights at Atlantic City, Syracuse Hancock, Rickenbacker, Huntsville and Seattle-Tacoma. Twelve vendors completed evaluation; six washed out, principally for radio-frequency or performance problems. Seven other vendors withdrew before contracting, with reasons including Ukraine-related demand and supply-chain pressure. Those seven belong to a different population and cannot be added to the participating-vendor failure denominator. (FAA Section 383 results)

The closest historical comparison here is FAA’s selected technical cohort, which ended with both completed evaluations and attrition. Like today’s deployments, it required compatibility and reliable equipment; unlike today’s independent SLTT pathway, it was a selected airport evaluation conducted before the new rules. It supplies neither a national deployment base rate nor a drone-defeat probability. Evaluation completion was not blanket vendor approval.

GAO’s August 2026 status reinforces that distinction: authority expansion was recorded as implemented while aviation integration remained open. FAA’s planned June 30, 2027 update is a reporting milestone, not a promised date for operational clearance. (GAO aviation-integration status)

There is tangible technical relief too. A September 27, 2026 account reports completion of Falcon Peak 26.2 and enduring counter-UAS test lanes at Yuma, with centralized performance data and common-interface work. Reusable testing could reduce repeated setup if customers accept the evidence. Neither faster civilian acceptance nor lower installation costs has yet been established by that account. (Falcon Peak and Yuma test lanes)

Certificates and event totals measure different things

The National Counter-UAS Training Center controls mitigation certification. The rules permit discretionary authorization of additional federal training providers; they do not establish that extra classrooms or seats are operating. Previously mitigation-certified personnel faced a September 29 deadline to complete the detection-and-warning curriculum, making current qualification records more useful than cumulative graduate counts. (certification and transition requirements) (National Counter-UAS Training Center training footage)

July’s rulemaking reported 61 certified officers representing approximately 46 agencies. August’s interagency roundtable instead reported almost 500 detection-and-warning trainees. These are different dates, populations and qualification tiers, not a consistent series of available mitigation crews. (July training record) (August training roundtable)

At the September 15 Senate hearing, Grassley described a maximum of 16 trainees monthly; Patel sought expansion and identified equipment, technology and personnel needs. This is substantive evidence of training pressure and an institutional funding request—not an admissions ledger or a measured national staffing shortfall. Patel’s written testimony separately attributed roughly 80% of World Cup counter-drone operational hours to SLTT partners, without resolving their certification tiers or independent versus federal roles. (Senate training-capacity exchange) (FBI operating-hours testimony)

NYPD’s August U.S. Open announcement identified intended independent mitigation; the September 17 city briefing subsequently described completed tournament security and planned UN General Assembly counter-drone coverage. Those statements do not fully attribute a completed independent mitigation-ready window, but missing records cannot establish nondeployment. These records indicate initial deployment activity, while repeatable independent mitigation coverage remains unverified. A redacted agency completion attestation could establish the authority, staffing, configuration and coordination without disclosing tactics or requiring a hostile engagement. (U.S. Open announcement) (New York security briefing)

The municipal figures come from approved purchasing records; the Marine Corps entry comes from an official fielding account under a different authority.

Dallas shows how an established public-safety relationship can carry additional counter-UAS purchasing. It does not prove competitor displacement. Federal grants, state subawards, municipal orders and supplier receipts can also describe the same money; adding them exaggerates spending. A purchasing ceiling, an accepted device and a staffed protection-hour remain different units.

Federal purchasing buys several different outputs

FEMA’s January 6 bulletin reported $250 million awarded on December 30, 2025, and described a further $250 million FY2027 tranche with broader participation. That offers a route to additional purchasing, not evidence that future awards have been executed or equipment accepted. Municipal working capital and continuing operating budgets remain separate dependencies. (FEMA funding bulletin)

CBP’s July 24, 2026 acquisition forecast specified $5 million–$10 million for passive RF data-service expansion to Rio Grande Valley East, Laredo, El Paso, Yuma and Blaine. Its planned FY2026 fourth-quarter award was not matched to an executed contract or accepted service. A purchased sensing footprint is a different commercial unit from a staffed mitigation operation. (CBP data-service forecast)

DHS’s August 5 procurement announcement includes integration, training, maintenance and vendor-operated turnkey services. That federal purchasing scope creates potential work for primes and specialists, but does not itself establish funded service orders or task-level operating delegations. It cannot be imported into the ordinary SLTT contractor pathway. (DHS lifecycle procurement)

Software spending can grow while responsibility stays public

San Bernardino’s allocation is revealing: $456,860 for equipment, $962,872 for software and $21,000 for services. Software can therefore represent a substantial portion of an approved detection package without establishing annual recurring revenue or supplier profit. These allocations cover the approved five-year agreement; dividing software spending by five would not establish its billing or revenue-recognition schedule. (San Bernardino financial analysis)

Axon’s published Dedrone terms describe licensing each connected hardware unit using its software, including third-party equipment. The potential control point is the software relationship across hardware suppliers. But the form applies conditionally through a customer quote; it proves neither a Dedrone purchase by the municipality publishing it nor identical executed terms elsewhere. Axon’s privacy notice separately distinguishes Dedrone Data from Customer Content. Such contractual rights do not establish realized data-sales income or costly switching. (conditional Dedrone licensing terms) (Axon data definitions)

Broader commercialization is established. Axon reported more than $100 million of Dedrone quarterly revenue in August, but disclosed neither standalone Dedrone margins nor an independent-SLTT mitigation revenue split. Its Software & Services gross margin fell to 71.3% from 75.6%, with professional-services mix and scaling costs among the causes. Integration creates work; retained economics depend on pricing, support obligations and capital requirements, not on applying a software margin to an entire equipment-and-service package. (Axon Q2 results)

Incumbents are buying capabilities and financing integration. Motorola’s August 20 announcement reported completion of its $1.5 billion D-Fend acquisition; Lockheed’s April 22 announcement described a $25 million Fortem investment and intended Sanctum integration. These establish disclosed ownership and financing developments, not achieved synergies or incremental factory output. (Motorola acquisition completion) (Lockheed investment announcement)

Operational control and financial risk can remain public while suppliers earn this revenue. San Bernardino’s staff analysis flags insurance-standard departures, no supplier indemnity, liability limits and no convenience termination, although specified cause and insufficient-funding termination remain available. It does not establish that Axon lacks insurance. The separate SAFETY Act framework addresses defined terrorism-related liability, not an all-risk performance guarantee. (county risk allocation) (SAFETY Act insurance obligations)

Three outcomes would change the explanation

Through end-2027, repeated coverage delivered by dedicated or regional teams, with little attributable staffing or coordination loss, would make the operating gate routine. Standing arrangements and reusable test evidence could expand coverage while reducing equipment and bespoke implementation expenditure per site. Suppliers would gain customers without necessarily gaining pricing power.

Alternatively, otherwise-ready sites repeatedly missing requested coverage because qualified teams are unavailable would support a narrower operating-capacity bottleneck. That finding needs matched requests, available equipment, staffing and attributable lost hours. Selecting only successful announcements—or only equipment-ready sites lacking crews—cannot establish national severity.

A third outcome would shift the explanation toward financing, delivery or technical acceptance: ready teams and operating arrangements repeatedly waiting on those dependencies. Los Angeles and FAA testing make that alternative substantive. Through 2028, even properly staffed and coordinated operations must demonstrate useful performance, sustainable full costs and continued use; readiness alone guarantees none of them.

At the September 29 cutoff, no comparable complete-denominator history supported numerical probabilities for independent coverage or paid continuation. The decisive record joins the same deployment’s readiness to requested coverage delivered or lost, including abandoned attempts, interruptions and concurrent demand. Completion order alone can mislead when a late equipment change itself triggers retraining or renewed coordination. (operating changes) (operational reporting)

What to watch

  • Base date: September 29, 2026. September 30, 2026–March 31, 2027: An effective successor FCC instrument supplies class-wide eligible SLTT mitigation spectrum authority beyond the original term. Proposals, manufacturer test authorizations and single-agency grants do not qualify; uninterrupted coverage is not required. (FCC baseline order)
  • Base date: September 29, 2026. September 30, 2026–June 30, 2027: FAA delivers its substantive integration update to GAO, established by dated submission or acknowledgment—not another promise, deployment clearance or recommendation closure. (GAO update milestone)
  • Base date: September 29, 2026. September 30, 2026–September 30, 2027: NYPD completes an additional independent mitigation-ready window with qualified staffing, eligible available equipment and applicable coordination. No hostile engagement is required; detection-only, ordinary confiscation and exclusively federal coverage do not qualify. (NYPD operating baseline)
  • Base date: September 29, 2026. September 30, 2026–December 31, 2027: An accredited provider completes mitigation-ready coverage for a different, non-accredited host under a written regional arrangement, retaining responsibility and supplying qualified personnel. An unused agreement, detection-only service or exclusively federal operation does not qualify. (regional-support pathway)
  • Base date: September 29, 2026. December 31, 2027 cohort review: A predeclared multi-site request cohort, retaining unsuccessful and abandoned attempts, attributes most coverage loss to financing, delivery or technical acceptance despite available personnel and operating arrangements. That would reject operating-capacity dominance in that cohort. (protection-request reporting route)
  • Base date: September 29, 2026. September 30, 2026–December 31, 2028: Fort Worth makes a nonzero software/support payment attributable to an exercised post-initial D-Fend renewal under M&C 26-0447. Original equipment payments, unused options and no-charge extensions do not qualify. (Fort Worth renewal authorization)

These tests remain open at September 29, 2026. Missing records do not establish failure, and one operation could satisfy both coverage tests. Their completion would establish specific transitions—not nationally scarce permission or exceptional supplier returns. The consequential measure is repeatable, useful coverage when requested, with evidence identifying what prevents it.

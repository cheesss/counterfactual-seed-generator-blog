The Jet-Fuel Mandate Runs Through a Restaurant's Back Door

Europe's first mandatory year of sustainable aviation fuel supply shows why refinery capacity alone is an incomplete guide to supply. Mandates increase demand for material that is authentic, eligible and acceptable to an operating plant. Collection, treatment, conversion and delivery each control a different part of that chain.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-06-07 · Updated 2026-10-24 · 13 min read

The Jet-Fuel Mandate Runs Through a Restaurant's Back Door

The recurring pattern is qualified supply: industrial capacity creates useful output only when the inputs and their supporting evidence can pass through every gate. In aviation, that gives a restaurant's oil collector a strategic role alongside the refinery developer.

In brief

  • Europe's reported SAF supply exceeded its first aggregate mandate.
  • Established cooking-oil collectors are adding aviation to road-fuel supply chains.
  • Qualification, plant reliability and product economics determine which constraint matters next.

The collection business was here first

Chick-fil-A provides a useful control against treating SAF as the origin of the collection industry. In April 2024, the restaurant company described more than 1,700 participating locations in the United States and Canada using DAR PRO Solutions to support renewable-diesel production. Its account linked the program to more than 637,000 gallons of renewable diesel in 2023. Chick-fil-A's customer account.

Darling Ingredients' August 2026 case adds an aviation outlet. It reports 9.9 million pounds collected from Chick-fil-A restaurants during 2025, supporting more than 1.1 million gallons of renewable diesel and SAF combined. DAR PRO installs holding tanks, collects through outside truck connections and removes impurities locally before sending feedstock to Diamond Green Diesel. Darling's operating case.

The combined-fuel figure describes an established service acquiring another product destination. An extra jet-fuel customer can redirect previously collected oil. The economically important question is how much additional waste is recovered, alongside how securely an individual refinery obtains its supply.

Dallas Fort Worth International Airport offers a longer history. McDonald's dates the airport collection partnership to 2019. DFW records Avfuel delivering an 8,000-gallon demonstration load to its corporate-aviation operation in November 2021. These records establish actual collection and airport delivery before ReFuelEU's mandate. McDonald's account, DFW's delivery record.

The asset being secured is a continuing relationship with a kitchen: storage, access, dependable servicing and ownership records. Neste's March 2020 agreement to acquire Mahoney concerned an existing collection and pretreatment business. Its rationale was access to material and a platform for expansion. The acquisition announcement.

Mahoney's advertised terms show who pays for what. UCO pickup and containers are free, qualifying oil earns market-linked rebates, and contracts establish ownership and traceability. Grease-trap cleaning is separately priced. A restaurant can therefore receive both a service and payment for material. Mahoney's service terms.

Higher oil prices can flow back to the kitchen. Collector earnings depend on the spread after rebates, transport, labor, equipment and treatment. Route density and service reliability may improve that spread; stronger fuel demand alone cannot establish who retains the premium.

Refining at home is not sourcing at home

EASA reports that 86% of SAF supplied at Union airports in 2025 was manufactured in the EU, while 85% was based on foreign feedstocks. EASA identifies used cooking oil as the predominant feedstock. The separate percentages describe manufacturing location and material origin. EASA's September 17, 2026 release.

The reporting summary covers 102 of 115 fuel suppliers. Its aggregate result supports the case that international procurement helped serve the first mandate year. A domestic refinery can reduce reliance on imported finished fuel while retaining exposure to overseas collection, freight and qualification. EASA's annual-report summary.

Road fuels supply an observed analogue. USDA describes higher US feedstock prices, a strong dollar and port-linked renewable-diesel plants attracting imports. The share of exporters' feedstocks directed to the United States rose from 13% in 2020/21 to 29% in 2023/24. Trade reallocation helped the supply chain respond. USDA's historical account.

Policy can redirect that trade again. IRS Notice 2026-53 states that transportation fuel produced after December 31, 2025 from imported non-Canadian/Mexican UCO is ineligible for the section 45Z credit. This changes supported demand for particular origins through tax eligibility. IRS guidance, section 4.02(2).

Imports can relieve a local collection constraint. Their wider effect depends on whether the oil represents newly recovered waste or material diverted from another destination.

Expansion begins with suppliers

The Vietnam PVOIL/FatHopes partnership is a network-development case. Petrovietnam's September 2026 account describes an initial Ho Chi Minh City rollout using PVOIL's logistics, with PVOIL Trans organizing collection. Its wording makes expansion conditional on accessible domestic supply. Petrovietnam's partner account.

FatHopes' August announcement sets an ambition exceeding 200,000 tonnes annually for export into its supply chain. The supplier invitation is more revealing about the immediate task: existing traders and collectors are invited to a November 5, 2026 event. At the October 24 cutoff, recruitment remains ahead of the scheduled meeting. The development announcement, the supplier invitation.

A newer October 20 FatHopes post still describes its Malaysian refinery as being developed and places Vietnam alongside sourcing partners in Spain and China. The expansion mechanism is a cross-border supplier network. Weighed receipts and export consignments would establish its realized contribution. The updated company account.

Eni's Kenyan collection campaign addresses the same source-level work. Its June 2023 announcement recruits hotels, restaurants and food businesses, offers financial contributions and identifies licensed collection with ISCC-EU traceability. The activities being organized are material acquisition and collection. Eni's campaign.

Treatment makes another gate visible

At Gela in Sicily, Enilive announced dedicated SAF production in January 2025 with stated capacity of 400,000 tonnes a year. This extended earlier SAF activity: the release identifies airline supply agreements since September 2022, supported by production across Eni facilities. The dedicated branch added reactor, separation, storage and logistics changes; a third degumming line was nearing completion. Enilive's production announcement.

Treatment determines which collected materials a plant can use. Alfa Laval's technical FAQ explains that impurities can reduce catalyst activity and cause pressure-drop buildup, shortening operating life. That creates a practical requirement for feed specifications and appropriate pretreatment. The technical FAQ.

A named buyer backs that requirement. In June 2026, Acelen contracted for SEK 1.1 billion of Alfa Laval pretreatment technology for its Brazilian biorefinery, with phased execution toward expected 2029 completion. The equipment handles solids removal, degumming and other treatment steps across a proposed feed mix including UCO and crop oils. The equipment contract.

The commercial object here is equipment and engineering. Better treatment can enlarge the usable input pool and improve operating continuity, making feed flexibility valuable before a new fuel plant reaches sustained sales.

Records and chemistry do different jobs

The Union Database's defined scope runs from collection-point purchases through conversion and downstream fuel transactions. It provides infrastructure for recording a chain of custody. The Commission's scope description.

Authenticating the first material description requires source controls as well. ISCC's September 2026 account reports 18 UCO samples from five regions analyzed by nine laboratories. Their variability prevented universal standard values; the preliminary chemical-screening tool remained a research outcome outside certification processes. ISCC's analytical findings.

The distinction cuts both ways. The Commission's July 2025 examination of Chinese biodiesel allegations identified systemic weaknesses in certification audits but could not confirm fraud from the information gathered. Procurement needs evidence specific to the material and transaction. The Commission's conclusion.

Operational data serve another purpose. AT&T's Darling case identifies LTE-M connectivity and device management used to improve pickup timing and reduce theft risk. Sensors help manage a physical collection service, creating a complementary role for connectivity and tank monitoring. The named deployment.

A plant can have oil and still make less jet fuel

Diamond Green Diesel's Port Arthur SAF branch began operating in the fourth quarter of 2024. Valero describes an option to upgrade roughly half the site's renewable-diesel capacity to neat SAF. Darling offers feedstock at market prices, while DGD can source elsewhere. Input sourcing and product allocation remain active commercial choices. Valero's operating disclosure, printed pp. 10-11.

IATA's June 2026 briefing forecasts 2.4 million tonnes of global SAF production in 2026 against flexible capability expected to exceed 9 million tonnes. It attributes part of the gap to more profitable renewable-diesel production. As the airline association's assessment, this supplies a competing explanation for low jet output: the supported product return. IATA's briefing, slide 12.

Reliability supplies another. Neste's first-quarter 2026 report identifies maintenance and delayed equipment replacement in Singapore. Its July update reports strong Renewable Products earnings alongside production limitations. Repairs and execution address a different problem from acquiring more kitchen oil. First-quarter report, half-year report.

Shell's September 2025 Rotterdam cancellation adds capital economics. Its evaluation found completion insufficiently competitive, while its biofuels trading and supply business continued. An airline supplier can procure fuel without completing every proposed asset. Shell's decision.

These are competing diagnoses. A collection-first remedy fits only the first row.

Fuel reaches customers through more than a refinery

Neste's annual tables separate 2025 SAF production of 841,000 tonnes from sales of 867,000 tonnes. KLM's annual report records 157,000 tonnes consumed in flight operations, versus 57,000 in 2024. Producer sales and airline consumption establish recurring operating activity at different points of the chain. Neste's tables, KLM's report.

Delivery responsibility also matters. Singapore Airlines Group's May 2024 agreement purchased 1,000 tonnes of neat SAF from Neste and assigned Neste blending and delivery into Changi's hydrant system. The purchased supply package therefore included integration with airport infrastructure. The supply agreement.

Alternatives change the required inputs

LanzaJet reported on-spec jet fuel and integrated Freedom Pines operation in November 2025. Its chronology separates January 2024 opening from May mechanical completion and June commissioning. The opening celebrated a plant still moving toward production. First-fuel announcement, company chronology.

In October 2026, it announced at least USD 20 million of committed financing, with an October 14 first close and full close expected by December. Investors were funding the production ramp and customer delivery capability. Ethanol-to-jet had achieved a technical milestone and still required commercial execution. The financing announcement.

ERA ONE in Frankfurt reached a flight demonstration in June 2026. INERATEC made and hydrogenated the material, ASG distilled it, and MB Energy blended it before Schiphol uplift for a KLM Cityhopper flight using 5% synthetic kerosene. The flight record.

Industrial host Infraserv identifies biogenic CO2 and chemical-industry by-product hydrogen as inputs, with capability up to 2,500 tonnes of e-fuels annually. The required complements shift toward suitable hydrogen, carbon, synthesis and finishing. Infraserv's site account.

Failure belongs in the comparison too. Fulcrum's September 2024 debtor motion records Sierra synthetic-crude production from landfill waste in December 2022, cessation in May 2024 amid liquidity problems and September bankruptcy petitions. A different waste input still faced the task of sustaining a commercial operation. The court filing.

What would change the diagnosis

Three counterfactuals separate procurement exposure from a binding shortage.

First, stronger domestic recovery or treatment could let usable supply grow faster than demand. That would weaken the foreign-sourcing call and reduce collection pressure. The discriminating evidence is recovered, accepted volume at comparable cost.

Second, road-fuel demand can retreat. USDA reports disclosed oil-and-fat use in US biomass-based diesel falling to about 34 billion pounds in MY 2024/25. EIA separately documents weaker finished-biofuel consumption and imports in the first half of 2025 under changed tax incentives and blending margins. Installed capacity did not compel continuously rising input use. USDA's feedstock record, EIA's market account.

Third, repeated non-lipid deliveries could reduce dependence on waste oils. ATJ and synthetic-fuel demonstrations make that route tangible. The commercial test is recurring delivered output at a viable cost.

The pressure case remains credible. The IEA's October 2025 outlook forecasts biofuels using 80% of estimated broad waste-and-residue-oil supply potential by 2030. Its category and estimated potential define a scenario for resource pressure. Renewables 2025, p. 152.

Limits: Public records do not establish global collection saturation, net-new recovery, SAF-only cash receipts or premium allocation. Reporting populations differ; combined fuels, capacity, demonstrations and financing measure different things. Foreign sourcing establishes exposure rather than a shortage, and neither capacity-output gaps nor dated announcements establish which constraint binds first.

What to watch

  • By December 31, 2027: EASA's comparable 2026 origin data settle the sourcing call. Reporting hub.
  • By December 31, 2026: a dated full-close announcement tests LanzaJet's financing schedule; silence leaves it unresolved. Company expectation.
  • By December 31, 2027: Vietnam receipts and consignments establish realized supply from the developing network. Recruitment baseline.
  • By December 31, 2027: repeated customer deliveries and disclosed run-rates test whether non-lipid plants are becoming routine suppliers. Freedom Pines baseline, ERA ONE baseline.

Fuel buyers should specify responsibility for rejected loads and failed eligibility checks. Developers need feed specifications, reliable equipment and workable product returns together. Policymakers need to observe qualifying deliveries and material origins alongside installed capacity.

The restaurant's back door is part of the SAF constraint map. The evidence supports treating it as one consequential control point, while diagnosing the next limiting gate plant by plant.

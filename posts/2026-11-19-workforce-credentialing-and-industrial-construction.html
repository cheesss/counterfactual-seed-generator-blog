A Factory Isn't Finished Until the Work Passes

In brief

  • An experienced crew and an authorized verifier can determine when installed equipment becomes usable.
  • TSMC's first Arizona fab recovered its target year after warning about scarce installation expertise.
  • Power, equipment and customer demand can set the pace even when crews are ready.

What Arizona's First Fab Actually Shows

At a new fab, the arrival of the first tools is a milestone for the builders. For the people who have to install them and get them working, it is the start of another job. In December 2022, TSMC marked its first tool-in at Phoenix and targeted 2024 production for its first Arizona fab. By July 2023, it told investors that it lacked enough workers with semiconductor-grade equipment installation experience and moved its forecast to 2025. It proposed bringing experienced technicians from Taiwan to train local workers. That December it reached an agreement with the Arizona Building and Construction Trades Council.

The scarce input at that point was experience doing a particular job. A trade license, a site qualification and a supervisor's judgment about who can handle a particular installation answer different questions. The same distinction matters on the other side of the handoff: a crew can finish its work before the owner has evidence that the work meets the standard. Across these projects, the recurring constraint is the ability to deliver work that can be accepted, when the site is ready for it.

Fab 1 reached volume production in the fourth quarter of 2024, inside the original target year. That makes the 2023 warning evidence of a scheduling risk that management took seriously, rather than a measure of net delay.

Apple dates mass production of its own chips to January 2025. By October 2026, it said Arizona-made chips were used across iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch and put 2026 production for Apple at more than 100 million chips. That customer figure describes annual production, while the two ramp dates mark different milestones. Commerce's 2024 award of up to $6.6 billion was milestone-based support for the buildout; Apple's disclosure shows what an operating customer relationship looks like.

It also shows where the next handoff sits. Apple and Amkor already had a packaging relationship when they announced a Peoria facility to package Apple silicon made at TSMC. Apple reported shell construction there in 2026 and expects to be among the first customers. Scaling wafer fabrication in Phoenix and establishing local packaging and test in Peoria are separate transitions with separate equipment, crews and acceptance dates.

What a Second Fab Can Reuse

A repeat campus gives the owner a chance to retain supervisors, vendor relationships and people who have worked through a startup. In October 2026, TSMC said Fab 2 construction was complete, tools were moving in and installation was underway. Management put volume production in Q3 2027, specifying the earlier of the two quarters in its previous second-half 2027 window. The target was still later than the 2026 production year announced in 2022. TSMC also described tight tool supply across the company. Those dates give a benchmark for the next ramp; they cannot isolate what crews contributed.

The local training record is getting more concrete. Rio Salado College reported eight process technician apprentices at an April 2025 signing event for a two-year program sponsored by Phoenix. TSMC's published technician role listings lists 12–18 months for its facilities, equipment and process tracks. In a separate program at Grand Canyon University, TSMC reported that 43 trainees finished an 11-week manufacturing specialist course in August 2026 and earned interviews.

At the same time, an October tool-installation posting described a dedicated startup team and sought experience relevant to equipment hookups and fab startup. A plant needs both pipelines: people to get tools into service and people to run and maintain them afterward.

When Work Has to Be Done Twice

In the Columbia submarine program, a supplier got development funding starting in fiscal 2019 to expand fabrication and train and certify welders. GAO's 2024 audit found that staffing problems and defects, including welding deficiencies, kept delaying deliveries. A separate supplier still could not meet future demand four years after funding for equipment intended to raise production. Outsourcing also added work for the Navy offices overseeing quality, while the shipbuilders remained responsible for oversight at their suppliers. Buying floor space, training and machinery creates capacity on paper; accepted components are what free up the next stage of assembly.

The Navy pays for construction and ultimately accepts delivery. Electric Boat and HII's Newport News yard share production on the established Virginia class: the Navy accepted Idaho from Electric Boat in December 2025, its second Virginia-class delivery that year. GAO reported both boats were more than three years late. The Navy's June 2025 construction pace was about one a year, against a goal of two. Two handovers can occur in a year even when work in progress is advancing at a slower rate.

HII's Goose Creek operation has been under Newport News since 2025 and adds an operating fabrication site. Its 2026 agreements with Path Robotics and GrayMatter Robotics offer a different route: HII intends to source up to $900 million of work over seven years if the companies meet readiness and performance requirements. The first stage is development and qualification. The economically meaningful transition is from proving the process to delivering work HII can integrate.

Nuclear Power Has Two Different Gates

At Vogtle Unit 3 in Georgia, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission cited two electrical quality violations, involving failure to correct nonconforming installations promptly and failure to meet cable separation requirements. It issued the combined-license acceptance finding on August 3, 2022, after reviewing Southern Nuclear's completion notices and inspecting relevant work. Unit 3 entered commercial operation on July 31, 2023; Unit 4 followed on April 29, 2024. Southern Nuclear operates the plant for its four co-owners. The dates distinguish proving the construction work from putting a unit into commercial service. Licensing an individual reactor operator involves a separate set of examinations.

Michigan's Palisades illustrates why even an existing plant has multiple milestones. The original unit entered commercial operation in 1971 and shut in May 2022. During the restart, Holtec paused fuel loading after a fuel assembly tilted and reported recovering it on October 21, 2026. Holtec's November 13 status sheet describes restoration work and preparations for reconnecting to the grid. Wolverine Power Cooperative and Hoosier Energy have long-term agreements to buy electricity; the federal loan guarantee and Michigan grant support the restart effort. The separate November application notice for the proposed Pioneer units concerns new construction, not the recovery of the old unit.

A restart with buyers under contract has a different economic problem from a build whose owners no longer want to pay to finish it. In South Carolina, the utilities halted V.C. Summer's new reactors in 2017 after rising costs, delay, weaker demand and Westinghouse's bankruptcy. Better crew coverage is worth paying for where it changes the date of a project the buyer still needs.

When Qualified Crews Would Still Wait

Intel gave a different explanation for its February 2025 Ohio schedule reset. It moved the first New Albany module's expected operating window to 2030–31 to align spending and construction with demand. That is distinct from the Phoenix installation warning. Likewise, the Department of Energy reports multi-year waits for some grid equipment. Berkeley Lab found that just 13% of proposed generation and storage capacity seeking transmission connection in 2000–20 was online by the end of 2025. That history concerns suppliers of power; customers connecting a load face a different process.

A customer can sometimes change which work the project is waiting for. Bloom Energy said it delivered an operating fuel-cell system to Oracle in 55 days in 2025, against 90 expected for that deployment. In April 2026, Bloom said Oracle had contracted for an initial 1.2 GW across projects under a wider agreement. Oracle and BorderPlex then announced a fuel-cell microgrid for Project Jupiter in New Mexico: a separate design milestone. On-site generation can move some work out of the utility's schedule into the customer's project. That brings fuel supply, permits and system integration into the customer's delivery plan.

Who Controls the Constraint

The owner can reserve scarce installation windows, the contractor can keep experienced supervisors with a crew, and equipment vendors can coordinate delivery and startup. The owner, utility or regulator then controls the relevant release to service. Off-site assembly can move work ahead of the on-site schedule. It still takes integrated commissioning to see whether power, cooling and controls work together under a failure as well as in normal service.

That is what electrical contractors sell. Quanta's July 2024 purchase of Cupertino Electric cost about $1.54 billion upfront in cash and stock. Cupertino Electric runs factory assembly and field installation and testing, and reports 2,000 data-center modules installed across its business. EMCOR recognized about $1.09 billion of Miller Electric revenue from the February 2025 acquisition through year-end. In the same filing it described pressure on profitability on some projects in new markets with lower labor productivity or availability. A contractor can win more work and still pay heavily to deliver it.

The payer changes earlier in the pipeline. Students buy instruction from Universal Technical Institute; the established UTI division reported about $577 million of fiscal 2026 revenue and opened campuses including San Antonio and Atlanta, while its new Skilled Trades Max format was still a plan.

In registered apprenticeship the employer or sponsor provides paid, supervised experience, and the federal government can offset some of that cost. The July 2026 Department of Labor awards allocated nearly $162 million across five organizations; eligible sponsors could apply from November for incentives tied to progress and retention. The St. Louis Fed reported a 46% national completion rate for registered apprenticeships in 2024. The federal measure allows a year beyond expected completion and excludes probationary exits. For the next project, the question is how many people finish and stay long enough to work independently or supervise others.

What to Watch by December 31, 2027

Limits: The evidence identifies specific handoffs that can hold up work. It does not measure how many days credentials alone cost nationally, and public schedules rarely disclose what changed at crew level.

Use the October 15, 2026 TSMC call as the base. The company's own Q3 2027 target is tighter than this column's year-end test. A confirmed start after year-end defeats the timing claim; a disclosed installer shortage that moves the schedule defeats the skills claim. An on-time start without a disclosed warning is consistent with a repeat-campus advantage. A production start without a disclosed cause cannot distinguish local staffing from vendor support or other changes.

For owners making decisions before then, put four dates side by side: equipment and power ready, qualified crew available, first test passed, work accepted. If a staffed site is waiting for a transformer or demand has moved out, address that first. If qualified supervision or inspection is what holds up testing, reserve those hours alongside craft time and track first-pass acceptance. Where off-site assembly reduces on-site hours per accepted unit without more rework, repeat the design. These are different responses to different reasons an asset isn't yet usable.

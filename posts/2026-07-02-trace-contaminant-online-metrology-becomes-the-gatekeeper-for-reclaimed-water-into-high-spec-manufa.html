Trace-Contaminant Online Metrology Becomes The Gatekeeper For Reclaimed Water Into High-Spec Manufacturing

The visible water problem around advanced fabs is usually measured in gallons, acre-feet, reservoirs, drought maps, and municipal politics. That framing is incomplete. A semiconductor fab does not merely need access to water.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-07-02 · 14 min read

The first tell is that this looks like a gallons problem

The visible water problem around advanced fabs is usually measured in gallons, acre-feet, reservoirs, drought maps, and municipal politics. That framing is incomplete. A semiconductor fab does not merely need access to water. It needs a stream that process engineers will allow into a yield-sensitive system after the stream has been converted, monitored, and documented at contaminant levels ordinary water infrastructure was never built to prove.

The hidden gate is not only reclamation capacity. It is validation evidence: the online measurement, alarms, sampling discipline, and contaminant-specific proof that reclaimed municipal or industrial wastewater can become a reliable feedstock for ultrapure water systems without exposing wafers to low-level excursions in urea, boron, silica, TOC, ammonia, particles, metals, salts, PFAS-related chemicals, or poorly characterized organics. Reclaimed water becomes strategically useful only after the fab, the water operator, the regulator, and the utility can agree that this evidence is continuous enough, sensitive enough, and operationally actionable enough.

That distinction changes the investment-language map. If the scarce item were simply raw water, the control points would sit with utilities, reservoirs, groundwater rights, pipelines, and municipal permits. Those still matter. But once a fab commits to high reuse, control shifts toward actors that can make variable waste streams predictable: water EPC contractors, design-build-own-operate operators, online analyzer vendors, UPW system integrators, membrane and ion-exchange suppliers, calibration services, environmental labs, standards bodies, and the internal fab teams that decide whether a water stream can touch process-critical tools.

TSMC shows the mechanism

Taiwan shows the mechanism most clearly. TSMC says it began research and pilot testing for reclaimed water in 2015, then built the Southern Taiwan Science Park reclaimed water plant with CTCI. The plant started operation in September 2022 and TSMC described it as the world's first industrial reclaimed water plant for advanced semiconductor processes. The company tied the project not only to volume but to “multiple and real-time automated water quality monitoring,” and said the STSP site would act as a processing, monitoring, and supply center for its fabs in the Southern Science Park as well as a monitoring station for municipal reclaimed water from the Yongkang and Anping plants. [Source: TSMC, 2022, https://esg.tsmc.com/en-US/articles/10]

CTCI’s account of the same project is more useful than a generic sustainability statement because it names the operational burden. The raw water is complex industrial wastewater. Some molecules are small and difficult to treat. The output requirement approaches the purity expectations of ultrapure water because the water goes into semiconductor manufacturing, where process stability and yield are the customer’s real standard. CTCI states that the STSP project was structured as a 20-year design-build-own-operate project, with CTCI responsible for investment, design, construction, and operation. It also says the project used continuous experimentation and pilot testing before full-scale operation. [Source: CTCI, 2024, https://www.ctci.com/e-newsletter/EN/481/discover-reliable/article-01.html]

The first implication is that water reuse at an advanced fab is not a single municipal procurement. It is a long-duration operating contract with yield risk embedded inside it. CTCI’s numbers show the scale: a 20,000-ton-per-day plant, completion in September 2022, reclaimed water introduced into wafer manufacturing in January 2023, and 12.61 million tons of reclaimed water supplied in 2023. The project is therefore not a lab curiosity. It is an operating asset inside the industrial boundary of one of the most demanding manufacturing systems in the world. [Source: CTCI, 2024, https://www.ctci.com/e-newsletter/EN/481/discover-reliable/article-01.html]

Water quality is not one number

The second implication is that “water quality” is not one number. A Taiwan reclaimed-water review gives the specific flavor of the problem. The Yongkang water reclamation plant treats municipal sewage and transports reclaimed water to the Southern Taiwan Science Park for semiconductor and TFT-LCD users. For TSMC, the source reports a urea requirement below 5 ppb, average urea after reverse osmosis of 3.5 ppb, a boron requirement below 100 ppb, and an average boron concentration of 70 ppb. It also describes an additional advanced oxidation process using sodium hypochlorite and sodium bromide for urea removal, with O&M cost split into sewage treatment, RO, and urea-removal components. [Source: Sustainable Environment Research, 2023, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42834-023-00177-8]

Those figures are small enough to move the analysis away from ordinary water infrastructure. They are also contaminant-specific. Urea is not the same problem as boron. Boron is not the same problem as particles. Silica is not the same problem as PFAS segregation. Conductivity does not see everything. A facility that can meet bulk TDS or turbidity limits can still fail a fab’s acceptance criteria if it cannot see the contaminant that matters before that contaminant breaks through a membrane, an ion-exchange bed, an electrodeionization stack, or a polish loop.

Who controls the constraint

The online metrology vendors understand this. Veolia’s Sievers Boron Ultra Analyzer is marketed for continuous online boron monitoring down to single-ppt levels, with a stated range of 0.010 to 100 ppb as boron, a 0.030 ppb limit of quantitation, and 20 data points per hour. The product page explicitly ties boron monitoring to advanced semiconductor UPW systems, resin regeneration, and detection before silica release. [Source: Veolia Water Technologies, https://www.watertechnologies.com/products/analyzers-instruments/sievers-boron-ultra-analyzer] Shimadzu introduced a TOC-1000e S online analyzer in 2026 and framed the problem around trace organic contamination in UPW, hard-to-oxidize compounds such as urea, and real-time response. [Source: Shimadzu, 2026, https://www.ssi.shimadzu.com/news/2026-03-04-Shimadzu-Introduces-the-TOC-1000es-online-analyzer-for-ultrapure-water-monitoring.html] Hach’s semiconductor materials list points to conductivity, particles, TOC, silica, sodium, dissolved oxygen, dissolved hydrogen, carbon dioxide, ATP, chlorine, pH, turbidity, and biological or waste-stream metrics across UPW and reuse systems. [Source: Hach, https://sea.hach.com/industries/semiconductors-electronics]

This is where the bottleneck can hide in plain sight. A fab’s public presentation may say “90% reuse” or “95% reuse.” The hidden work is deciding which contaminants must be measured online, where the sample points sit, how fast a bad signal diverts a stream, which lab methods validate the online instruments, how calibration is handled, how a shock-load event gets traced to its source, and who bears the cost when a water train is technically operating but not released for the highest-grade use.

Named cases show where the constraint moves

Several named cases sharpen the point.

At the ASE Nanzih Technology Industrial Park reclamation plant, the Taiwan review describes a 30,000-CMD system that treats mixed industrial effluents using biological activated carbon, multimedia filtration, ultrafiltration, and reverse osmosis. The same source says organic rejection can be incomplete because source streams differ, and describes FEEM and HPSEC measurements used to characterize feedwater and unit performance. Most important, it notes that sources with high breakthrough signals were excluded from the recovery system, reducing unqualified RO water and downstream treatment burden. That is a metrology-driven routing decision, not a simple volume decision. [Source: Sustainable Environment Research, 2023, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42834-023-00177-8]

At Fengshan, the same review records COD shocks linked to illegal dumping of TPH and agricultural waste between 2019 and 2021. During shock periods, ammonia removal dropped materially and stable nitrification recovery took more than two weeks. The episode is a useful warning for fabs: reclaimed water is exposed to upstream behavior that the fab does not fully control. Online monitoring and early warning systems become part of the supply chain, because the contaminant event begins outside the wafer plant and arrives as chemistry. [Source: Sustainable Environment Research, 2023, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42834-023-00177-8]

Intel is the counterargument

At Intel’s Ocotillo campus in Chandler, Arizona, the control problem looks different but not simpler. The 2024 draft environmental assessment for Intel’s CHIPS-supported project states that total water demand for three fabs is 14 million gallons per day, with 7.9 MGD supplied from Intel and City of Chandler reclaimed systems and 6.1 MGD from city potable water. The document also describes on-site reclaim systems, a city reclaimed-water interconnect, the Ocotillo Brine Reduction Facility, and pretreatment requirements for hydrogen peroxide, TDS, nitrogen, and COD. [Source: NIST draft environmental assessment, 2024, https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/07/08/Intel%20Ocotillo%20EA_DRAFT%20FINAL_VOLUME%201%208July2024.pdf] Intel’s own materials show technicians calibrating and reviewing sensors in the reverse-osmosis building and describe an on-site reclamation facility that treats up to 9.1 MGD, plus an OBRF that removes hardness and silica from water for reuse. [Source: Intel, 2023, https://download.intel.com/newsroom/archive/2025/en-us-2023-07-11-intels-ocotillo-campus-honored-for-water-stewardship.pdf]

That case is also a serious counterargument. Intel has operated a large, mature water-reuse system for years. The binding constraint there may be salts, municipal agreements, expansion coordination, or infrastructure redundancy, not novel trace-contaminant instrumentation. The same pattern can undermine the stronger version of the thesis elsewhere: once the process is standardized, online metrology may become a necessary operating layer rather than the scarce gate.

TSMC Arizona is the live test

TSMC Arizona is the live test. The 2024 environmental assessment states that TSMC Arizona’s first two fabs already had environmental permits and that TSMC would need an updated wastewater discharge permit for the second fab before June 30, 2028, likely including the planned industrial reclaimed water plant. The same document says that once the industrial reclaimed water plant is installed, TSMC Arizona will achieve 95% or greater water reuse, with losses mainly from evaporation. It also describes segregation of known PFAS-containing chemicals in closed bulk storage for off-site permitted treatment and disposal to reduce water contamination. [Source: NIST draft environmental assessment, 2024, https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/06/04/TSMC%20Draft%20EA%20June%203%202024%20.pdf]

Public reporting on that Arizona plant gives a slightly different operating ramp: construction announced in 2025, a 15-acre plant, expected operation in 2028, current first-fab recycling around 65%, initial recycling around 85%, and a target of more than 90%. [Sources: Axios Phoenix, 2025, https://www.axios.com/local/phoenix/2025/08/27/tsmc-industrial-water-reclamation-plant-phoenix; AZBEX, 2025, https://azbex.com/planning-development/tsmc-building-water-reclamation-plant/] The range between 85%, 90%+, and 95%+ is not just a public-relations discrepancy. It is the terrain where acceptance criteria, permit language, ramp timing, and contaminant control can decide how much water actually returns to the fab and how quickly.

Samsung broadens the map

Samsung’s cases broaden the map. In Korea, Samsung Semiconductor signed an MOU with the Ministry of Environment, Gyeonggi Province, Hwaseong, Osan, Korea Water Resources Corporation, and Korea Environment Corporation to regenerate sewage water from Hwaseong and Osan and supply 120,000 tons per day of reclaimed water to Giheung and Hwaseong semiconductor sites beginning in 2029 after feasibility studies, private-investment review, design, and facility installation. [Source: Samsung Semiconductor, 2024, https://news.samsungsemiconductor.com/global/samsung-semiconductor-signs-mou-with-ministry-of-environment-and-local-governments-for-reclaimed-water-collaboration/] In Texas, EPCOR was selected to develop, design, construct, finance, own, and operate the Sandow Water Project and Blue Sky Water Reclamation Facility for Samsung’s Taylor fab, targeting 75% process-water reclamation and reuse with an initial equity investment of $300 million. [Source: EPCOR, 2023, https://www.epcor.com/ca/en/news/media-releases/2023-07-13-epcor-tapped-water-partner-central-texas.html]

The strongest version of the thesis does not say membranes are unimportant, or that municipal permits can be ignored, or that every fab reuse project will bottleneck on online analyzers. It says the economic control point may migrate after the visible water deal is signed. The city may provide water. The EPC contractor may build the train. The permit may authorize discharge. But the fab still needs a documentable, continuously monitored quality envelope that supports yield protection. The actor that controls that envelope can influence spend, vendor selection, operating procedures, maintenance cycles, resin regeneration, diversion decisions, and the pace at which reclaimed water displaces city water.

Markets can miss that migration because the line items are small relative to fab capex. A multi-billion-dollar fab makes a TOC analyzer, a boron analyzer, an ICP-MS program, or a sampling cabinet look trivial. Yet those small systems can sit near a large leverage point: the release valve between “treated water exists” and “treated water can enter process-grade systems.” That is the same industrial pattern seen in other high-spec manufacturing bottlenecks. The gating component is not always the largest component. It is the component that creates auditable trust.

What would prove this wrong, and what to watch

The thesis weakens if three facts become visible. First, new high-reuse fabs routinely accept reclaimed water using conventional monitoring only, with no added trace-contaminant control layer and no new validation burden. Second, project delays cluster around pipelines, water rights, power, construction labor, or wastewater permits while water-quality acceptance never appears in local records, contracts, vendor tenders, or operating narratives. Third, operators prove that contaminant shocks are rare, quickly isolated, and irrelevant to UPW systems because source segregation and pretreatment remove the risk before fab-grade trains are exposed.

The watch list is concrete: wastewater permit revisions that specify contaminant classes; procurement of online TOC, boron, silica, particle, sodium, ATP, and conductivity instruments; new DBOO contracts that make the operator liable for water-quality excursions; local records of upstream dumping or shock loads; resin regeneration frequency; bypass or diversion language in treatment diagrams; semiconductor customer specifications for reclaimed feed; and public differences between stated reuse targets and achieved reuse after plant startup.

A restrained conclusion follows. Reclaimed water can relieve the visible political constraint around advanced manufacturing, but it creates a less visible operating constraint inside the fab supply chain. The critical question is not whether a city can deliver more water. It is whether a variable wastewater source can be transformed into a trusted, monitored, documented input for a manufacturing process that prices contamination in yield loss. The strongest control points sit where chemistry, proof, and liability meet.

Reclaimed Water Reaches the Fab Only After It Earns Trust

The visible water problem around semiconductor fabs is measured in reservoirs, pipelines, municipal allocations and gallons per day. Those constraints decide whether a project can obtain enough water. They do not decide whether reclaimed water is suitable for the most demanding uses inside a fab.

By Bottleneck Briefs · Published 2026-07-02 · 15 min read

In brief

  • 20,000 tons/day: TSMC’s STSP plant shows semiconductor reuse at industrial scale.
  • 14 MGD: Intel Ocotillo shows metrology can become routine inside mature reuse systems.
  • 2028: TSMC Arizona will test qualification after plant construction and permitting.
  • CTCI’s 20-year DBOO model places leverage in integrated operations, not standalone analyzers.

That decision comes later. A variable municipal or industrial wastewater stream must pass through treatment, monitoring, laboratory confirmation, source control and operating procedures before a fab’s facilities and process-yield teams will release it into a qualified water system. The decisive asset is therefore an integrated validation chain rather than a particular analyzer.

This distinction turns the original bottleneck thesis into a sequencing argument. Developers first clear water rights, conveyance, plant construction, permitting and residual-disposal constraints. Contaminant-specific qualification becomes consequential only after treated water is physically available.

The evidence supports validation as a necessary operating layer that can affect routing, diversion and fab acceptance. It does not establish online trace metrology as the universal gatekeeper, or analyzer hardware as a scarce control point.

A functioning qualification chain may include sample-point design, online TOC or boron instruments, conductivity and particle monitoring, laboratory cross-checks, calibrated alarms, automated diversion, source tracing and final release authority. No audited project document in the supplied record discloses the complete chain. That absence is precisely why the next commissioning cycle matters.

Taiwan shows what qualification looks like in practice

TSMC began reclaimed-water research and pilot work in 2015. Its Southern Taiwan Science Park reclaimed-water plant began operating in September 2022, and reclaimed water entered wafer manufacturing in January 2023. TSMC describes the site as a processing, monitoring and supply center using multiple real-time automated water-quality measurements. (TSMC STSP)

CTCI designed, built, owns and operates the facility under a 20-year design-build-own-operate contract. Its account describes complex industrial wastewater, difficult-to-remove small molecules, repeated pilot testing and output requirements approaching those of ultrapure-water systems. The disclosed plant scale is 20,000 tons per day. (CTCI STSP)

The commercial lesson is larger than the equipment list. The customer purchases a continuously operated water service, while the specialist operator assumes responsibility for treatment integration, reliability and long-term operation. The public record does not reveal whether CTCI also bears penalties for off-spec water, lost reuse, residual disposal or manufacturing interruption.

The reported capacity and supply figures also require caution. CTCI separately reports 12.61 million tons supplied during 2023, an average volume above the simple annualized output of a 20,000-ton-per-day plant. The disclosed figures may cover different plant-only and regional monitoring-and-supply scopes; the source does not reconcile them.

Yongkang provides the clearest contaminant-specific example. A 2023 peer-reviewed review reports that the municipal reclamation plant treated 29,000 cubic metres per day and delivered an average of 15,500 cubic metres per day to the science park. It attributes to TSMC a urea requirement below 5 parts per billion and a boron requirement below 100 parts per billion. Reported averages after treatment were 3.5 ppb and 70 ppb, respectively. (Taiwan reclaimed-water review)

Those figures should not be treated as independently verified current contractual specifications. They still demonstrate the operating regime. Bulk conductivity or turbidity cannot substitute for contaminant-specific evidence when individual species interact differently with reverse osmosis, oxidation, ion exchange and polishing systems.

ASE’s Nanzih Technology Industrial Park adds a routing example. Its 30,000-cubic-metre-per-day system treats mixed industrial effluent through biological activated carbon, filtration, ultrafiltration and reverse osmosis. The review reports that fluorescence excitation-emission matrices and high-performance size-exclusion chromatography were used to characterize organic breakthrough. Sources with strong breakthrough signals were excluded from the recovery system.

That is an analytical decision with physical consequences: the plant sacrifices some feed volume to protect downstream recovery and water quality. The measurements were not established as continuous online release instruments, but they show how evidence can determine which streams enter the treatment chain.

Fengshan shows the failure mode. Illegal dumping and upstream shock loads drove chemical oxygen demand above normal conditions, reduced ammonia-removal performance and required more than two weeks to restore stable nitrification. Fengshan supplied industrial rather than semiconductor-grade users, so it is not direct evidence of a fab qualification gate. It does show that reclaimed water inherits behavior from dischargers the final customer does not control.

Arizona shows why the constraint is sequential

Intel Ocotillo is the strongest counterexample to the universal gatekeeper claim. A 2024 draft environmental assessment estimated total demand for three fabs at 14 million gallons per day: 7.9 MGD from Intel and City of Chandler reclaimed systems and 6.1 MGD from potable supply. Intel also discloses an on-site reclamation facility capable of treating up to 9.1 MGD and a brine-reduction facility that removes hardness and silica. (Intel Ocotillo assessment)

Intel documents technicians calibrating instruments and reviewing sensors. Yet its public materials emphasize TDS, nitrogen, chemical oxygen demand, silica, municipal interconnection and brine management rather than a novel trace-analyzer bottleneck.

This is what a mature system looks like when measurement has become embedded infrastructure. Instruments remain necessary, but they no longer pace expansion. The scarce capability may instead be treatment reliability, residual handling, municipal coordination or redundancy.

TSMC Arizona is at an earlier stage. Its planned industrial reclaimed-water plant is expected in 2028 and is intended to support a high-reuse, near-zero-liquid-discharge design. The 2024 environmental assessment states that an updated wastewater permit will be required before June 30, 2028 for the second fab. (TSMC Arizona assessment)

Public descriptions use different reuse figures: approximately 85% for an initial ramp, above 90% as an operating objective and 95% or greater in the environmental assessment. These percentages may reflect different facilities, operating stages or accounting boundaries rather than contradictory achieved performance.

The sequence is therefore clear. The plant must first be designed, contracted, built and permitted. Only then can commissioning records reveal whether treated water enters qualified use immediately or waits for a contaminant-specific validation envelope.

A meaningful confirmation would be a documented interval between mechanical completion and credited high-grade reuse, accompanied by thresholds, laboratory confirmation, alarms or diversion rules. Immediate progression to the stated reuse range through conventional treatment and standard UPW procedures would push the thesis toward routine metrology rather than a binding qualification gate.

Samsung tests whether the model travels

Samsung’s Taylor project initially illustrated a finance-own-operate structure. EPCOR announced that it would develop, finance, own and operate the Sandow water project and Blue Sky Water Reclamation Facility, with $300 million of initial equity and a 75% process-water reuse target. (EPCOR Samsung Taylor)

EPCOR later disclosed the transfer of Blue Sky to Samsung. That transaction shows that water-asset ownership can migrate toward the fab after development. It does not establish whether Samsung also internalized operations, whether EPCOR retained a service role or whether the change reflected financing rather than operating strategy.

The distinction matters for value capture. External ownership can generate long-duration water-service revenue. Fab ownership can shift capital and strategic control onto the manufacturer’s balance sheet while preserving outside opportunities in design, equipment, maintenance, laboratory work and specialist operations.

Samsung’s Korean project is larger and less mature. The company and public agencies plan to regenerate municipal sewage from Hwaseong and Osan and supply 120,000 tons per day to the Giheung and Hwaseong semiconductor sites beginning in 2029. The project must still pass feasibility review, private-investment review, design, installation and commissioning. (Samsung Korea)

This project will test whether Taiwan’s integrated model transfers into a different regulatory and institutional environment. A formal execution award, water-quality annex or operating contract would reveal who owns performance responsibility. Until then, the visible gate remains project development rather than trace-contaminant qualification.

Control belongs to the workflow owner

Analyzer vendors already offer semiconductor-relevant sensitivity. Veolia’s Sievers Boron Ultra product covers a stated range down to hundredths of a part per billion, while Shimadzu markets continuous TOC monitoring for ultrapure water and difficult organic compounds such as urea. Technical availability weakens any argument that analyzer manufacturing capacity itself is scarce. (Veolia boron analyzer)

The more defensible control point is the validated workflow surrounding the instruments. Someone must decide where to sample, how to condition the sample, how often to calibrate, which laboratory method confirms an excursion, when a stream is held or diverted, how treatment settings change and who can release water into qualified use.

That control can sit with different actors. CTCI can bundle design, financing, digital monitoring and 20 years of operation into a water service. TSMC Arizona can internalize design governance and operating knowledge while outsourcing construction and equipment. Utilities retain leverage over feedwater, wastewater collection and permits. Fab facilities and process-yield owners retain final manufacturing acceptance.

Standards can reduce bespoke engineering without transferring control to one vendor. SEMI F75 addresses monitoring locations, testing frequency, feedwater stability and online-analyzer coverage. The report does not identify a named reclaimed-water project that contractually mandates F75, but the framework shows how qualification could become more repeatable. (SEMI F75)

The recurring spend path therefore extends beyond instrument hardware. It includes calibration standards, reagents, replacement components, field service, reference instruments, laboratory confirmation, sample cabinets, control-system integration, historians, alarm management, cybersecurity, operator training and residual handling.

Public-company exposure follows this architecture without implying an investment recommendation. TSMC, Intel and Samsung control end-user demand and final acceptance. CTCI represents integrated DBOO exposure. Veolia, Shimadzu and Danaher through Hach supply analytical and water-treatment capabilities. Their economic benefit remains unproven until named deployments, fleet sizes, service contracts or protected qualification status become visible.

Standalone analyzer revenue can also be double-counted easily. A DBOO contract may already include treatment equipment, controls, analyzers, consumables and service. Counting the operator’s full contract value and every embedded component as separate market expansion would overstate the economic pool.

The closest base rate cuts both ways

TSMC’s STSP project is the closest historical base rate. Research and pilots began in 2015, the plant entered operation in September 2022 and reclaimed water entered wafer manufacturing in January 2023. The cycle took years because the project required treatment development, physical construction, monitoring and operating integration.

The current Arizona and Korean projects share that long sequence. They must move from design and institutional agreement into construction, commissioning and final operating release. This supports a qualification horizon extending through the 2028–2029 cycle rather than an immediate equipment-demand story.

The difference is equally important. Taiwan combines severe water pressure, science-park policy, concentrated semiconductor demand and an experienced operator ecosystem. Arizona must integrate city infrastructure, wastewater permitting, brine and residual controls. Texas has already experienced a shift in asset ownership. Korea remains at the public-private development stage.

Intel supplies the second base rate. It shows that large reclaimed-water shares can operate without public evidence that trace metrology remains the pacing constraint. That outcome is not a minor caveat; it is a plausible destination for every successful project. Qualification may matter most during design and ramp, then disappear into ordinary facilities operations.

The 2028–2029 cycle will settle the thesis

Three outcomes are plausible.

In the first, validation becomes a visible sequential gate. TSMC Arizona completes the physical plant, but permits or commissioning documents require contaminant-specific thresholds, laboratory confirmation, defined monitoring locations and automated hold or diversion actions before reclaimed water counts toward high-grade use. Integrated operators, laboratories, control-system firms and service providers gain leverage because they own the qualified workflow.

In the second, residual handling displaces validation as the tighter constraint. Higher recovery concentrates salts, organics, PFAS-related chemicals and reverse-osmosis reject. Permits, disposal contracts, near-zero-liquid-discharge equipment and operating cost then determine the achievable reuse rate even when water-quality measurement works as designed.

In the third, metrology becomes routine. Arizona, Texas and Korea reach their reuse targets through conventional reclamation and standard UPW polishing, with no special qualification interval or separately priced validation obligation. Instrument vendors benefit from ordinary procurement, but control and margin remain with incumbent EPC firms, utilities, UPW integrators and fab-owned facilities organizations.

The first public milestone should be procurement visibility. A plant targeting 2028 operation must move through named engineering, treatment, automation and residual-handling packages. Public identification of a supplier by the end of 2027 would show execution progress, though confidential bundling could obscure an otherwise active project.

Wet commissioning by the end of 2028 would shift the question from construction to release. The most informative evidence would then appear within the first 180 days: achieved recycling, the interval between water introduction and qualified use, and any documented acceptance or diversion controls.

A hardware-scarcity claim requires affirmative evidence. Silence about analyzer-related delays cannot prove that none occurred. A credible test would be a named project disclosing extended lead times, single-source qualification or a commissioning delay of at least 90 days caused primarily by unavailable analyzer hardware. The current record contains no such case.

What to watch

  • By 2027-12-31, a public record names a TSMC Arizona IRWP supplier and contracted package.
  • By 2028-06-30, Samsung or a Korean authority announces a formal Giheung–Hwaseong execution award.
  • By 2028-12-31, TSMC or Phoenix confirms Arizona IRWP wet commissioning or initial operation.
  • Within 180 days of commissioning, TSMC reports achieved recycling and any qualification interval before high-grade use.
  • A permit or commissioning record names a threshold, monitoring point, laboratory check, alarm, hold or diversion action.
